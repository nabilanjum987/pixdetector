About This Tool

An AI image analyzer uses computer vision and machine learning to automatically read the content of an image — the same way a person would glance at a photo and describe what's in it, but broken down into structured data. Instead of a one-word "yes/no" answer, you get a written breakdown: what's in the scene, what colors and mood dominate, whether there's text or people in it, and how the image was likely composed.

This matters because a lot of image questions aren't really "is this fake" questions — they're "what am I looking at" questions. If you're sorting a folder of stock photos, moderating user uploads, or just want a second opinion on what a picture contains, a quick content breakdown is often more useful than a bare authenticity score.

How It Works

When you upload an image, it's sent to an AI vision model that examines it across several layers at once: object and scene recognition (what's physically in the frame), color and lighting analysis (the palette, contrast and mood), and pattern analysis (the kind of pixel-level regularities that tend to differ between a camera photo and a synthetically generated one). The model combines these into the probability score and the written summary you see in the results — it isn't a lookup against a known-image database, it's a fresh read of the image you upload each time.

What the Results Mean

The AI probability score is the model's confidence that the image was AI-generated rather than captured by a camera — higher isn't "worse," it's just a likelihood. The verdict translates that into a plain-language label, and confidence tells you how sure the model is about its own answer — a "Low" confidence result is worth double-checking rather than treating as final. The details grid below that breaks the scene down further: subjects, colors, composition and so on.

Limitations

No AI detector is 100% accurate, and this one is no exception. Heavily edited real photos, very old or low-quality images, and AI images that have been re-compressed or upscaled can all confuse the model in either direction. Treat the result as a strong first read, not a legal or forensic verdict — for anything high-stakes, cross-check with the source or a second tool.

FAQ

Does it store my image?

No — the image is analyzed in real time and discarded immediately after. Nothing is saved on our servers.

Why does it give a probability instead of a yes/no?

Because AI detection genuinely isn't certain — a probability is more honest than forcing a binary answer the model isn't fully confident about.

Can it analyze screenshots or heavily cropped images?

Yes, but with less certainty — cropping and re-compression can remove some of the signals the model looks for.

Is this different from the Analysis Tool?

Yes — this tool covers general content (what's in the image). The Analysis Tool focuses on technical image data like sharpness and compression instead.
