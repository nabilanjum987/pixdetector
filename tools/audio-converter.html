The problem: the file you have is not the file you need

Audio incompatibility is a small problem that arrives at the worst moment. An iPhone voice memo is an M4A, and plenty of Windows apps, car stereos and older podcast hosts still only accept MP3. A WhatsApp voice note is an OPUS file, and Apple Music, Voice Memos, iMessage and CarPlay all refuse it outright — Apple has never shipped system-wide Opus support. A recording booth exports WAV, a field recorder saves FLAC, and the app you need to upload to wants MP3. People search for a free audio converter because they want one file changed, fast, without installing software, creating an account, or handing a private recording to a random server — and they want to know the converted file will actually play where they need it.

An audio converter is a tool that decodes a sound file from one format and re-encodes it in another. Audio formats fall into two families: lossless formats (WAV, FLAC) keep every sample, and lossy formats (MP3, M4A/AAC, OGG, OPUS) discard data the ear barely notices to shrink the file to about a tenth of its size. Converting between them changes the container and the encoding, never the performance itself — and converting lossy to lossy always loses a little more detail, so you should convert from the original file whenever you can.

Why your iPhone audio is not MP3 — and what to do about it

Apple picked its own formats: Voice Memos exports M4A (AAC in an MP4 container), and messaging apps save voice notes as OPUS. Neither is a bug — but both break when the next app in line only accepts MP3. The fix is a one-way conversion to MP3 at 128–192 kbps for speech, or to M4A if the destination is Apple-only. For a WhatsApp voice note, convert OPUS to M4A at 64–96 kbps mono: the note was recorded around 24 kbps, so picking 320 kbps only makes a bigger file with no better sound.

Bitrates, honestly

96 kbps is fine for voice and the smallest file. 128 kbps is the standard for speech and most music. 192 kbps sounds better on good headphones. 320 kbps is the biggest file and only worth it for music from a high-quality source — it cannot rescue a low-quality recording. WAV and FLAC have no bitrate setting because they are lossless: WAV stores every sample (about 10 MB per minute), FLAC stores the same audio losslessly at roughly half that size.

FAQ

What is the best free online audio converter?

Depends on the job: XConvert for the widest format support (19 inputs); Mokivo/AudioSaw for in-browser privacy; Castos for a zero-config podcast MP3; AudioConvert.online for a balance of formats + privacy. No single tool wins on all four in this run.

Is there a free audio converter with no signup?

Yes — XConvert, Mokivo, AudioSaw, AudioConvert.online and Castos all convert without an account; 123apps also needs no login but has a paid tier for some features (per its own page’s comment replies). This page needs no account either.

Can I convert audio without uploading my file?

Yes — Mokivo (Web Audio API), AudioSaw (ffmpeg.wasm in-browser), AudioConvert.online and Castos process locally; XConvert, Aspose and Restream upload to a server. Check before converting private recordings. This page processes locally in your browser — nothing is sent to a server.

How do I convert M4A to MP3?

Drop the M4A into any converter above, pick MP3, choose a bitrate (192–320 kbps for music, 128 kbps for speech) and download. iPhone voice memos are M4A — this is the most common conversion in the run.

How do I convert a WhatsApp voice note to MP3?

WhatsApp saves voice messages as OPUS in an OGG container; upload it to a converter that accepts OPUS (XConvert, Mokivo — and this page) and export MP3 so it plays in QuickTime, Windows Media Player or PowerPoint. Use the “WhatsApp voice note” button above for the one-click path.

Does converting audio lose quality?

Converting lossy → lossy (M4A → MP3) loses a little more each generation; converting to WAV/FLAC loses nothing further but can’t restore what was already lost. Always convert from the best source you have and keep the original.

What bitrate should I choose?

320 kbps for music you care about, 192 kbps for general listening, 128 kbps for speech/podcasts (Castos fixes 128 kbps for exactly this reason); higher bitrate = bigger file, same content. Use the slider presets on this page: Speech 128 / Music 192 / Best 320 / Lossless WAV-FLAC.

Can I convert audio on my phone?

Yes — Mokivo, AudioConvert.online (25 MB cap per file on touch devices) and the small in-browser tools work in mobile browsers; the App Store’s “The Audio Converter” app is the install route on iPhone (free with in-app purchases). This page works in a mobile browser too, but the ~30 MB engine and conversion are slower on phones — keep clips short.

Why does the converter take so long the first time?

In-browser converters download ffmpeg.wasm (~30 MB) on first use — AudioConvert.online states ~32 MB; dev.to advises lazy-loading it only after a file is picked. Later visits reuse the browser cache. This page loads it only after you pick a file, never on page load, and shows a progress bar with a cancel option.

Can I convert audio from a video file?

Some converters accept video input (Restream: MP4/MOV → MP3/WAV; AudioSaw: drop MP4; The Audio Converter app: MOV/MP4 in) — or extract the audio first, then convert it. Castos is audio-only and says so. This page is audio-only too: upload an audio file, not a video.

Is it safe to use a free online audio converter?

Depends on the tool — in-browser tools never receive your file; server tools vary (XConvert deletes after a few hours, Aspose after 24 hours). Commenters on the 123apps reels report virus warnings — those reports were not verified against the site itself in this run, but the trust concern is real and repeated. This page never receives your file: after the first conversion the engine is cached, so turn off wifi and convert again — it still works.

What is the difference between MP3, WAV, FLAC and AAC?

MP3 = small, lossy, universal; WAV = uncompressed, big, for editing; FLAC = lossless compression, smaller than WAV with nothing lost; AAC = Apple’s MP3-equivalent, better quality per megabyte.

Can I convert multiple files at once?

Yes on batch tools — XConvert, Convertr.org and AudioSaw support batch; AudioConvert.online allows 10 files per batch. Free tiers on server tools may queue batches. This page converts one file at a time — batch conversion and ZIP download are not supported here yet.

Can I convert OPUS to MP3?

Yes — XConvert and Mokivo both list OPUS as an input; pick MP3 as output. OPUS is the WhatsApp/Telegram voice-note format. This page accepts OPUS files too.

Why won’t my file play in my car / PowerPoint / editor?

Format support differs per device — older car stereos read MP3/WMA only, PowerPoint wants MP3/WAV, editors want WAV/AIFF. Convert to the format the destination accepts; the converter page’s format guide is the fix.
