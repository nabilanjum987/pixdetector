Why turning speech into text is still a hassle

Everyone wants the same thing: talk, and get accurate text back. Students want lectures as notes, creators want video captions and scripts, and busy people want to write emails and documents by talking instead of typing. Social feeds are full of demos promising exactly that — short clips showing a video or voice note turned into text in seconds, and web guides comparing free tiers obsessively because the catch is always the same.

The catch is the free tier. Most transcription tools give you 60–300 free minutes and then ask you to pay — Notta's free tier is 120 minutes a month, Otter's is 300, Descript's is 60 — and commenters on every demo reel ask the same thing: "is it really free?" Accuracy is the second problem: "the output was bad for accents", users report on Reddit, and regional-language failures are named again and again in comments — "can it transcribe Hindi?" is a real question people ask. Then there is the input problem: many web tools are speech-only and do not say so until you try — users with a recorded MP3 or a WhatsApp voice note arrive expecting file transcription and find a microphone button instead. The most-shared workflow in the research is the chain itself: a video turned into an MP3 and then into a transcript, from an Instagram demo with 1.3M likes. This page solves the instant, private half of the problem: live dictation with no minute cap, no account and no upload. It does not solve file transcription — that limit is stated above, honestly, because pretending otherwise would waste your time.

What this tool does — and what it does not do

What it does: live speech-to-text from your microphone, in eight languages, with live interim results so you can see words as you speak, plus copy and .txt download. There is no time limit and no account, so a long dictation session costs nothing.

What it does not do: it cannot open an MP3 or voice note and transcribe it, it cannot separate two speakers, and it cannot guarantee 100% accuracy. Names, numbers, dates and uncommon words are the most likely to be misheard, so proofread them before quoting or publishing. Noisy rooms, overlapping voices and mumbling all lower accuracy — a close microphone and clear, steady speech are the biggest quality levers you control.

Dictation is not transcription — which one do you need?

The confusion this page exists to clear up: dictation turns live speech from a microphone into text as you speak; transcription turns a recorded audio file into text after the fact. They are different tools, and most "free audio to text" pages are one or the other without saying which. This page is dictation. If you have a file, use the route below instead of fighting the microphone.

  • You are speaking now — dictate here. Live, free, no minute cap, no signup.
  • You have an MP3 or voice note — play it near the microphone in a quiet room in short clips (the workaround on this page), or upload it to a file transcriber built for that job. A WhatsApp voice note goes: export it (WhatsApp → share → save), then upload it to the file tool.
  • You have a YouTube video — use a YouTube-to-transcript tool (Clipto.ai), copy YouTube's own auto-captions, or run the chain: video → MP3 on our video-to-MP3 page → transcript in a file tool.
  • You have a meeting or interview — you need timestamps and speaker labels, which only diarization tools (Notta, Otter, Descript, Fireflies) provide; this page outputs one continuous block of plain text.

FAQ

Is this audio to text converter really free?

Yes. Live dictation is free with no minute limit, no sign-up, no watermark and no upload. Your speech is processed by your browser's speech recognition — nothing is sent to our servers.

Can I upload an MP3 file and convert it to text here?

No — and we say so upfront. This tool transcribes live speech from your microphone only; it cannot open or transcribe uploaded audio files. As a workaround, play your recording out loud near the microphone in a quiet room and let it dictate live, in short clips. For long files, use a file transcriber built for that job.

Is there a free audio to text converter with no time limit?

Truly unlimited free tools are rare — most give 60–300 free minutes then charge; browser dictation (Web Speech API) is unlimited but only works live from a microphone, not from files. This page is that unlimited dictation: no minute cap, no signup.

How do I transcribe a voice note from WhatsApp?

Export the voice note as an audio file (WhatsApp → share → save), then upload it to an audio-to-text tool; some tools accept M4A/OPUS directly, others need MP3 first. This page cannot open the file — play it near the microphone in a quiet room, or use a file transcriber.

Can I transcribe a YouTube video to text for free?

Yes — use a YouTube-to-transcript tool (Clipto.ai) or download the audio and run it through a transcription tool; YouTube's own auto-captions can also be copied. The chain on this site is: video → MP3 (video-to-MP3 page) → transcript.

Is audio transcription accurate for Indian accents / Hindi?

Accuracy varies a lot by tool; Whisper-based tools handle many accents and languages better than older engines — test with a 30-second sample before committing to a long file.

How long can the audio be?

Free tiers usually cap at minutes (e.g. 10–60 min/month); desktop Whisper has no cap but needs setup; browser dictation has no length cap but only works live.

Can I get a transcript with timestamps?

Yes — tools like Notta, Otter and Descript add timestamps and speaker labels; simple free converters usually output plain text only. This page outputs plain text only.

What format should I export the transcript as?

TXT for plain text, DOCX for documents, SRT for video subtitles — pick based on what you'll do with it next. This page downloads .txt.

Can transcription tools handle multiple speakers?

Only tools with speaker diarization (Notta, Otter, Descript, Fireflies); basic free converters output one continuous block of text. This page does not separate speakers.

Why is the transcript wrong on names, numbers and accents?

Speech recognition is never 100% accurate. Usually the cause is background noise, overlapping speakers, low volume, or a strong accent — clean audio (one speaker, quiet room) transcribes far better than a noisy recording. Accuracy varies a lot by tool; Whisper-based tools handle many accents and languages better than older engines. Always proofread names, numbers and dates before quoting — that is the single most important step.

Which browsers work best for dictation?

Chrome and Edge work best, on a computer or Android phone. Speech recognition may be limited or unavailable in some other browsers — if the Start button reports that it is unsupported, switch to Chrome or Edge.

Does this tool store my voice or my transcript?

No. Everything runs in your browser: your voice goes to your browser's speech recognition, and your transcript stays on this page until you copy or download it. We never receive, store or see it.

Can I dictate in Hindi, Urdu or Spanish?

Yes. Pick your language before you start — English (US and UK), Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Urdu and Arabic are available. Dictating in the matching language improves accuracy noticeably.
