The problem: post the photo, leak the plate

The reason people reach for a blur tool is usually a deadline: a car photo for a listing, a group photo for social media, a screenshot for a support ticket — and somewhere in the frame sits a license plate, a child's face, a name, an address on a label, or an account number. The use cases are everyday ones: a stranger in the background, a child, a witness, a coworker who never agreed to be posted. The free tools people find first often make it worse: one gives a single free edit per week, another watermarks free exports, a third uploads your uncensored photo to its server to do the work. And the quiet trap: a light blur looks finished but can sometimes be partially reversed, so a code or plate character survives. People want a quick, private way to hide exactly the right area — and to know it is actually hidden before they post.

Blurring an image means softening pixels across part or all of a photo, so faces, text or backgrounds turn soft and unreadable. A Gaussian blur averages each pixel with its neighbours; a stronger blur averages over a wider area. Pixelation is different: it shrinks an area into big blocks. A solid cover replaces the pixels entirely. The three look different and hide different amounts — blur for looks, pixelate for a clear censor, solid black for secrets.

How far is far enough? The strength guide

The slider on this page runs 1–30, with labelled zones: 1–5 is a subtle look, 6–14 softens faces but leaves them recognisable, and 15–30 is redaction-grade. The same idea is behind Pi7's blur tool, which uses a 1–40 scale and notes that past 25, faces and text stop being readable. Whatever the scale, the rule is the same: push past the middle for anything sensitive, then zoom into the downloaded file and check that nothing is still readable. If it must never be readable, skip blur entirely and use the solid black cover.

Background blur: a style effect, not privacy — and not in this tool

Background blur keeps the subject sharp and softens everything behind it for style — it is not a privacy measure, and it is not the same as blurring a face. It needs an AI model that finds the subject, and hair, glass, transparent edges and low-contrast scenes are where that separation fails, leaving a smudged outline. This tool does not offer background blur: here you can blur the whole image or draw boxes over selected regions instead. Don't use a style blur where you need redaction.

Auto face detection: not in this tool

This tool does not auto-detect faces — there is no "blur all faces" button. In Regions mode you draw a box over each face, plate or piece of text by hand, which takes longer on group photos but keeps you in control of exactly what gets hidden. Auto-detect tools such as Facepixelizer and Convertlo blur several faces in one pass, in about 30–40 seconds per photo; hand-drawn masking is the honest trade-off this page makes for staying fully in-browser with no upload.

FAQ

How do I blur an image online for free?

Upload a photo to a free blur tool, pick a blur, drag the intensity slider, and download — this tool is free with no signup, and everything runs in your browser so nothing uploads.

How do I blur a face in a photo?

This tool has no auto face detection, so switch to Regions mode and draw a box over the face by hand, set the strength high, preview, and download. Make the box slightly larger than the face, including hair and ears.

Is it safe to blur a photo online — won't the tool keep my photo?

Depends on the tool. In-browser tools never upload your photo; server tools copy it first. For anything sensitive, use a no-upload tool — this one processes entirely in your browser.

Does a light blur really hide a face?

No — a light blur leaves shape and skin tones readable and can sometimes be partly recovered. Push the strength high, or use pixelation with a large cell size for real anonymity.

What is the difference between blurring a face and blurring a background?

Face blur hides a specific region for privacy; background blur keeps the subject sharp and softens everything behind it for style — it is not a privacy measure. This tool does not offer background blur.

Can I blur just one part of an image, like a license plate?

Yes — switch to Regions mode and drag a box over the plate, face or text, then pick blur, pixelate or a solid cover. Whole-image-only tools can't select one region by hand.

How do I blur the background of a photo?

This tool does not have a background-blur mode — background blur needs an AI model that finds the subject, keeps it sharp, and blurs the rest, and hair and glass edges are where it struggles most. Here you can blur the whole image or selected regions instead.

What is the difference between Gaussian and motion blur?

Gaussian softens evenly in every direction — best for hiding faces and backgrounds. Motion blur pulls pixels along one angle for a sense of speed. This tool uses a Gaussian-style soft blur.

Can I blur a photo on my phone?

Yes — this page works in a mobile browser; upload, drag the boxes with your finger, and download.

Is blurring better than pixelating for privacy?

No — pixelation hides a face more reliably than a light blur; a strong pixel mosaic throws detail away in solid blocks, while a soft blur smears it. Both are weaker than a solid cover.

Does blurring remove the location data from my photo?

The download here is a fresh image drawn from pixels only, so EXIF data such as GPS does not carry over — the location data is removed on export.

Can I blur multiple faces in a group photo at once?

This tool has no auto face detection, so there is no one-click “blur all faces” here — drag a box over each face separately in Regions mode. Auto-detect tools blur several faces in one pass.

Can I undo a blur after downloading?

Not with certainty — a strong blur scrambles pixel data for good, but a light blur over plain text can sometimes be partly guessed back by sharpening tools, so use a high blur factor for anything sensitive.

How do I blur text or an address in a screenshot?

Drag a box over it in Regions mode and pick the solid black cover — blur alone does not guarantee that text cannot be reconstructed, and a solid cover is recommended for anything that must be hidden.
