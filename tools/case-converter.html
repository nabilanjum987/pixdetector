The problem: a whole paragraph typed with Caps Lock on

It happens to everyone: you look up and realise the last two paragraphs were typed with Caps Lock on, or a headline pasted from a spreadsheet is in the wrong case, or a variable name needs to be camelCase and you have it as a sentence. The complaints are always the same three. First, retyping: people retype whole blocks by hand because they do not know a converter exists. Second, title case done wrong: naive tools capitalise every word, but real title style (Chicago, AP) lowercases short words like “and”, “of” and “the” mid-title — and people argue about which is right. Third, the tools that do exist are either buried in a word processor menu, or they convert one case at a time and make you copy-paste back and forth. What people want is the simplest possible fix: paste once, click the case, copy the result — and for developers, the code cases (camelCase, snake_case, kebab-case) in the same place as the writing cases.

Which case should I use for file names?

kebab-case is the web convention: lowercase, hyphens, no spaces. URLs and most static hosts treat it most predictably. snake_case is the convention for Python code and database columns, camelCase for JavaScript variables, and PascalCase for classes and components. For a document title, use Title Case — but remember the “Simple” option capitalises every word, while real style guides lowercase short words like “and”, “of” and “the” in the middle of a title. Pick AP, APA, Chicago or MLA in the style picker above if your style guide requires that.

The small-words cheat sheet

The classic title-case errors are all small words. Articles (a, an, the) and short prepositions and conjunctions (and, but, or, in, of, to) stay lowercase in the middle of a title — except as the first or last word, which is always capitalised. The four-letter split is the trap: APA capitalises With, From and Through (four or more letters), while Chicago and MLA lowercase them. And a few words are always capitalised whatever the guide: Is, It, If and Than are a verb or a pronoun, not a minor word — so “Love It or List It” keeps It capitalised, while “The Catcher in the Rye” keeps in lowercase and “Pride and Prejudice” keeps and lowercase.

How do I convert text to title case?

Paste your text, click Title Case — but check which style guide you need first: AP, APA, Chicago and MLA give different outputs for the same title. Use the style picker above the buttons to apply the guide your assignment or publication follows.

Which words are not capitalised in title case?

Articles (a, an, the) and short prepositions and conjunctions (and, but, or, in, of, to) stay lowercase in the middle of a title — except as the first or last word, which is always capitalised. The exact set depends on the style guide: APA capitalises words of four or more letters, while Chicago and MLA lowercase prepositions of any length.

What is the difference between Title Case and Capitalize Words?

Title Case follows a rule — some words stay lowercase. Capitalize Words capitalises every word with no exceptions. This tool's “Simple” Title Case option is the capitalise-every-word version; the AP, APA, Chicago and MLA options apply the real rules.

What is sentence case?

Sentence case capitalises the first letter of each sentence and lowercases the rest. It does not recognise proper nouns or the pronoun “I” mid-sentence, so give the result a quick manual pass.

What is camelCase used for?

Code identifiers — variable and function names. The first word is lowercase, each following word is capitalised, with no spaces: helloWorld.

What is snake_case vs kebab-case?

snake_case joins words with underscores (hello_world) and is the convention for Python variables and database columns. kebab-case joins words with hyphens (hello-world) and is the web convention for URLs and file names.

How do I fix text typed with Caps Lock on?

Paste it into the converter and click lowercase or Sentence case — the standard fix, no retyping. That is the most common use of this tool.

Does Title Case follow APA or MLA?

A generic converter follows neither — it capitalises every word. For APA, words of four or more letters are capitalised even if they are prepositions; for MLA and Chicago, prepositions of any length stay lowercase. This tool's style picker applies the rule you choose instead of guessing.

What is the difference between Title Case and Sentence case in APA?

APA uses title case for journal and website titles in references, and sentence case — first word and proper nouns only — for article titles.
