What each speed is good for

Changing video speed means playing the footage faster or slower than it was recorded. 2× is the workhorse: it halves the length of a slow process, a long walkthrough or a rambling explanation, and it is the speed most people reach for first. 4× turns a long, boring stretch — a drive, a build, a download bar — into a quick time-lapse. 1.25× and 1.5× are the subtle ones: they tighten a talking-head video just enough to feel snappier without sounding rushed. On the slow side, 0.5× doubles the length and shows detail the eye misses at full speed — a golf swing, a magic trick, a product feature — and 0.25× is full slow motion for the single moment worth savouring.

Speed changes work best on short clips, because export runs in real time: a 2× export of a 2-minute clip takes about a minute, but a 0.5× export of the same clip takes about four. That is also why the instant preview matters — you can hear and see the speed before you commit to the export, instead of waiting minutes to find out 4× was too fast. Try the preview first, especially at the extremes: 4× can make camera shake feel violent, and 0.25× can make a shaky handheld shot feel like an earthquake.

Two honest limits before you start. First, the export is WebM, not MP4, and it keeps the audio only when your browser allows it — speed changes the picture and the sound together, so if the clip needs its original sound at the new speed guaranteed, use a desktop editor. Second, slowing a video down cannot add frames that were never recorded: 0.25× on 30 fps footage shows each frame four times, which looks steppy rather than silky. True smooth slow motion needs a high-frame-rate source — 60 fps or 120 fps — so shoot fast if you plan to slow down.

FAQ

Is changing video speed online really free?

Yes. Change the speed of as many videos as you like, from 0.25× to 4× — no watermark, no sign-up and no upload. Everything runs in your browser.

Is there a length limit on speed changes?

No length cap on this page — but export runs in real time, so very long clips are slow. It is best on clips under a few minutes.

Why is the exported video a WebM file?

Browsers can only re-encode video into formats they natively support, and WebM (VP8/VP9) is the one every modern browser can write. WebM plays in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari. If you need an MP4, convert the WebM afterwards with HandBrake or FFmpeg.

Does the speed-changed video keep the original audio?

Yes, when your browser allows it — the export carries the original audio playing at the new speed, so a 2× export sounds twice as fast. If a browser blocks that, the export still finishes but is video-only, and the page tells you so. If your clip needs its original sound at the new speed guaranteed, use a desktop editor such as Shotcut or DaVinci Resolve instead.

Can I preview the speed before exporting?

Yes — the preview plays at the speed you pick as soon as you pick it, so you can check 2× or 0.5× instantly before you commit to the export.

Will slowing a video down make it smooth slow motion?

Only if the source was shot at a high frame rate (60 fps or 120 fps). Slowing 30 fps footage to 0.25× repeats each frame, which looks steppy rather than silky — the frames were never recorded.
