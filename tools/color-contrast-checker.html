The problem: your brand colour fails — and you only find out in the audit

Low-contrast text is the single most common accessibility failure on the web. The usual suspects turn up in audit after audit: light grey captions (#9CA3AF on white is 2.54:1), white text on a bright green button (2.28:1), white on a red error badge (3.76:1 — fails for normal text), pale placeholder text, and a light-mode token reused in dark mode that passes on white and fails on dark grey. Designers discover these in a Lighthouse report or an accessibility audit, long after the palette was locked — and then the question is not whether it fails, but how to fix it without throwing the brand away. What people want is a thirty-second check they can run before shipping: two colours in, a ratio and a pass/fail out, plus a concrete suggestion for the shade that would pass.

What contrast ratio do I need for WCAG AA?

WCAG 2.1 level AA needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (24px and up, or about 19px bold). Level AAA needs 7:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text, and UI components and icons need 3:1 against adjacent colours. Enter your text and background colours above and this page computes the ratio with the official WCAG formula and shows pass or fail for all five checks. It is free, with no signup, and everything runs in your browser. Note what it does not do: it checks the ratio only — it does not fix your design, and it only measures two flat colours.

The contrast ratio comes from the relative luminance of the two colours — roughly, how bright each one is to the human eye — through the formula (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), where L1 is the lighter colour. Black on white is the maximum, 21:1. The thresholds are not rounded: 4.49:1 does not meet 4.5:1.

My brand colour fails. What now?

Do not throw the palette away. Three fixes keep the brand intact: adjust lightness, not hue — keep the brand's hue and darken the text shade slightly on light backgrounds (or lighten it on dark ones); increase the text size — a pair at 3.8:1 fails AA for normal text but passes for large text at 3:1, so 24px (or 19px bold) can resolve it without changing a colour; or use the brand colour as an accent, not as text — keep it for borders, icons and backgrounds, and set the text in a darker variant of the same hue. The suggestion button below does the lightness walk for you: it keeps your hue and finds the nearest shade that passes 4.5:1. For text over a photo, none of this applies directly — the background is not one colour, so add a semi-transparent dark overlay or a solid block behind the text and test the worst-case region, not the average.

Does passing the ratio guarantee accessibility?

No. Contrast is one criterion, and it is necessary but not sufficient. Colour-only meaning (red versus green with no other cue), focus visibility, and text over images all need their own checks. Automated checkers catch only a fraction of real accessibility failures — a pass here means the colour pair is fine, not that the page is accessible. It is still the fastest check you can run, and the one that catches the most common failure first.

FAQ

What ratio do I need to pass WCAG AA?

4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (24px regular, or about 19px bold) at level AA. AAA needs 7:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text. UI components and icons need 3:1.

What counts as large text?

WCAG defines large text as 18pt (24px) or larger, or 14pt (about 19px) bold and larger. Anything smaller is normal text and needs the stricter 4.5:1 ratio.

Why does grey text on white fail?

Light grey is close in brightness to white, so the ratio is low: #9CA3AF on white is 2.54:1, and even #767676 on white is 4.48:1 — just under AA. Use #757575 or darker for body text on white.

Can I check text on an image with this tool?

Not reliably. This checker measures two flat colours; text over a photo sits on many colours at once, and the worst-case region is what matters. Add an overlay behind the text and test the darkest or lightest region it covers.

My brand colour fails — do I have to abandon it?

No. Keep the hue and darken the text variant for text use, use the brand colour as an accent instead of as text, or enlarge the text so the 3:1 large-text threshold applies. The suggestion button finds a same-hue shade that passes.

Does passing the contrast check guarantee accessibility?

No. Contrast is one WCAG criterion. Colour-only meaning, keyboard focus visibility and text over images need separate checks. A pass here means the colour pair is fine — not that the whole page is accessible.

Related questions

What is the highest possible contrast ratio? 21:1 — black on white. Nothing scores higher, because the formula caps at the two extremes.

Do placeholders need to pass? If placeholder text conveys essential information, yes — darken it. #9CA3AF placeholders on white are 2.9:1 and fail; #6B7280 passes at 5.74:1.

Is dark mode automatically accessible? No. Recalculate every pair for the dark theme — a grey that passes on white can fail on a dark background.

What about WCAG 3.0 and APCA? WCAG 3.0 will replace the ratio with APCA, which weighs text size and weight more accurately. WCAG 2.1 is still the standard you are audited against today.
