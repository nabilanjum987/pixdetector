The problem: what is halfway between these two colours?

Brand work runs on this question. You have two brand colours and need the midpoint for a hover state, a tint for a background, or a smooth ramp of five shades for a chart — and guessing the hex in between never lands right. The confusion is worse because "mixing" means two different things: painters mix blue and yellow and get green, while screens mix blue and yellow light and get a greyish neutral. Forum threads are full of people surprised by exactly that, and of developers hand-computing midpoints channel by channel. What people want is a slider: set the ratio, see the result and every step between, and copy the hex that looks right.

A colour mixer blends two colours by a ratio. At 0% you get colour B, at 100% you get colour A, and every value between is a weighted average of the two. The midpoint — 50% of each — is the single most useful mix: it is the natural hover colour, the in-between step in a gradient, and the base for a tint scale.

Why do blue and yellow make grey here — and green in paint?

Because screens and paint mix opposite things. Screens add light: red, green and blue light together make white, so mixing blue and yellow light averages toward a desaturated neutral. Paint subtracts light: pigments absorb some wavelengths and reflect the rest, so blue and yellow paint together reflect green. This tool mixes like a screen (RGB), with an HSL hue-blend mode that travels around the colour wheel instead — blue to yellow via hue passes through green and looks far more vivid. Neither mode predicts real paint: pigment, base and brand all change the physical result, so treat any on-screen mix as a starting point and test it on paper before buying paint.

What is the midpoint good for?

Three everyday jobs. Hover states: mix a button colour 15–25% toward white or black for a hover shade that still reads as the same colour. Tints: mix toward white in steps (90/10, 80/20, 70/30) for a background scale. Gradients: the 50/50 midpoint is the colour to add as a middle stop when a two-colour gradient goes grey in the middle — see the Gradient Generator for the same idea in CSS form.

What colour do blue and yellow make?

Mixed as light on a screen, they head toward a greyish neutral — this tool's RGB mode shows that. Mixed as paint, they head toward green. The two kinds of mixing follow different rules, so check which one you mean.

How do I find the midpoint between two colours?

Set the ratio slider to 50/50. The result is the exact halfway blend, with its HEX code ready to copy. The shade strip below shows the steps on either side too.

Can I mix more than two colours?

This tool mixes two at a time. For three, mix the first two, copy the result, paste it in as colour A and mix with the third.

Is this an accurate paint mixing simulator?

No — it is a close visual guide for screen colours, not a physical model of pigments. Real paints vary by brand and pigment load, so test the mix on paper before committing.

Which blend mode should I use?

Use Light (RGB) when you want the literal screen average. Use Hue blend (HSL) when the midpoint looks muddy — it travels around the colour wheel, so blue to yellow passes through green instead of grey.

Related questions

How do I make a lighter version of a colour? Mix it with white: set colour B to #FFFFFF and slide the ratio toward white. 80% colour + 20% white is a gentle tint.

How do I make a darker version? Mix with black the same way — 80% colour + 20% black is a shade that still reads as the same hue.

What colours make brown? Mix complementary colours, like red and green, or all three primaries together, then darken the result toward black.

Can I use the mixed colour in CSS? Yes — copy the HEX and paste it as a CSS value, or copy the RGB/HSL form if your codebase uses that notation.
