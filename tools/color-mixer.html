The problem: "I hate mixing"

That is the exact phrase painters use in the comments under colour-mixing reels — they avoid painting because they cannot match the colour they see in a reference photo, and they buy dozens of pre-mixed tubes when a few basics plus the right ratios would do. One Instagram reel urges viewers to "stop settling for the wrong colors" and "stop wasting money on extra paints": pick a few Golden Heavy Body acrylics, set the ratios, and see the mixed colour before buying. The same frustration shows up in Facebook artist groups, where members say they "struggle with matching colors from reference photos" and that a recipe tool could help them "overcome reluctance to paint". And when a mix is made once, it usually cannot be remade — no saved ratios, no shareable link, no recipe card. What people want is a mixer that speaks paint, not just hex: named paints, parts and percentages, a recipe they can repeat, and an honest warning when the screen will lie about the tube.

Paint vs light: why the same two colours give different answers

Because screens and paint mix opposite things. Screens add light: red, green and blue light together make white, so mixing blue and yellow light averages toward a desaturated neutral. Paint subtracts light: pigments absorb some wavelengths and reflect the rest, so blue and yellow paint together reflect green. Mixing all paints gives a muddy brown or black; mixing all light gives white. This page's Paint mode mixes in RYB space (red-yellow-blue, the painter's primaries) as a simulation of pigment behaviour; Light mode mixes the numbers your screen actually uses. Neither mode predicts real paint exactly: pigment, base and brand all change the physical result, so treat any on-screen mix as a starting point and test it on paper before buying paint.

What colours make…? The questions people actually ask

Green: blue + yellow paint. Orange: red + yellow — Cadmium Red Light + Cadmium Yellow Medium is about #F16A1D. Purple: red + blue, but a warm red gives a dull purple-brown (#4F3C3D); use Quinacridone Magenta for a clean violet. Brown: complementary colours (red + green) or all three primaries together, then darken — one commenter put it as "red black". Pink: red + white. Skin tone: balanced primaries plus white to set the value — the hardest match, and the one where a photo-to-recipe tool helps most; sample the skin colour from the photo in the Color Picker from Image and paste the HEX into the reverse tab here. Try each pair in Paint mode above and watch the result — the steps strip shows the whole ramp, not just the endpoint.

What is the midpoint good for?

Three everyday jobs. Hover states: mix a button colour 15–25% toward white or black for a hover shade that still reads as the same colour. Tints: mix toward white in steps (90/10, 80/20, 70/30) for a background scale. Gradients: the 50/50 midpoint is the colour to add as a middle stop when a two-colour gradient goes grey in the middle — use it as a stop in the Gradient Generator. And once a mix works as text on a background, run the pair through the Color Contrast Checker — a pretty midpoint often fails the 4.5:1 test.

FAQ

What colours make green?

Blue + yellow paint makes green. In light, blue + yellow makes white — the two systems disagree, so say which one you mean. In this tool, switch to Paint mode and mix blue and yellow to see green; Light mode shows the screen answer instead.

What colours make purple?

Red + blue in principle — but a warm red + blue gives a dull purple-brown (artincontext.org's example, Cadmium Red Light + Ultramarine Blue, is #4F3C3D). For a clean violet, use a cool red such as Quinacridone Magenta with blue.

What colours make brown?

Mix complementary colours — red + green — or all three primaries together, then darken the result toward black. One social-media peer answer puts it even shorter: 'Red black'.

What colours make orange?

Red + yellow paint. Artincontext.org's worked example is Cadmium Red Light + Cadmium Yellow Medium = #F16A1D. In this tool's Paint mode the same two paints mix to a very close vivid orange.

Why do blue and yellow make green in paint but white on a screen?

Paint pigments absorb light — the mix reflects only the green both pigments let through. Screen colours are light that adds up: blue + yellow light together contain red, green and blue, which we see as white. Paint is subtractive, light is additive.

What happens if you mix all the colours together?

Paint: a dark muddy brown or black. Printing ink (CMY): dark grey or near-black. Light (RGB): white. More paint means darker and duller; more light means brighter.

How do I mix colours to get skin tone?

Start with balanced primaries (red, yellow, blue) plus white to adjust the value, then nudge warmer or cooler. Skin tones are the match painters find hardest — the Golden mixer posts single out skin tones as the case where a photo-to-recipe tool helps most.

How do I calculate paint mixing ratios?

Set parts per paint: 3 parts white + 1 part blue is 75% white and 25% blue. For a target colour, use the reverse tab on this page — enter the HEX and it calculates the ratio for you. For a wall, the batch calculator below turns the ratio into millilitres per paint.

Can a colour mixer replace a physical colour mixing chart?

As a dynamic guide, yes — the makers of one mixer describe it as 'faster, more flexible, and more comprehensive than a static chart'. That is a vendor claim, not independently verified. It does not replace a swatch test: acrylics dry darker and brands differ, so test the mix on paper before buying paint.

Why does my digital mix look muddy or grey?

Simple RGB averaging pulls the blend through grey territory — blue to yellow averages to grey, not green. Mix in OKLab (a perceptual space) instead, or add a middle stop or colour rather than averaging straight across. This page's Light mode offers sRGB, Linear and OKLab so you can see the difference.

What is the difference between mixing paint and mixing light?

Paint is subtractive: each pigment absorbs wavelengths, so more pigment means darker and duller. Light is additive: more light means brighter, and all three primaries at full strength make white. That is why the same two colours can give opposite answers.

How do I make a lighter tint or darker shade of a colour?

Add white for a tint and black for a shade. The tints and shades row under the result shows both in steps — click any step to copy it. In Paint mode, mix in Titanium White or Ivory Black as a real paint in the recipe instead of just lightening the screen colour.

Can I mix colours for a wall or furniture before buying paint?

Yes, for planning: blend two base shades, adjust the tint or shade, and use the batch calculator to turn the ratio into millilitres per paint. But the on-screen result is an approximation — real paint depends on the base, pigment and manufacturer, so test a swatch first.

What is a colour mixer or colour combiner?

A tool that shows the colour you get when you combine two or more colours in certain amounts. 'Colour combiner' is the same thing with several colours at once. This page mixes two colours at a time; mix the result with a third colour to combine three.

Related questions

How do I make a lighter version of a colour? Mix it with white: set colour B to #FFFFFF and slide the ratio toward white. 80% colour + 20% white is a gentle tint — or use the tints row under the result.

How do I make a darker version? Mix with black the same way — 80% colour + 20% black is a shade that still reads as the same hue.

What colours make pink? Red + white. In Paint mode, mix Cadmium Red Light with Titanium White; more white makes a paler pink.
