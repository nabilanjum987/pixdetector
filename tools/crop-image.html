The problem: the platform crops your photo — badly

The frustration people repeat most is losing control of the crop. You upload a photo to Instagram and it crops it to its own ratio, usually from the centre — cutting off a head, a product edge, or the text you carefully placed. A profile picture is worse: Instagram shows it as a circle, so the corners of any square upload disappear, and a face near the edge gets clipped. The same happens with link previews (Facebook wants 1200×630), YouTube thumbnails (1280×720) and Stories (9:16) — every platform has its own shape, and guessing wrong means the platform crops for you. People want to choose the crop themselves, at the exact ratio, before they upload anywhere.

How do I crop an image online for free?

Upload your image here, pick an aspect ratio (1:1 for a square post or profile picture, 16:9 for a YouTube thumbnail, 9:16 for a Story or Reel, 4:3 for a classic photo) or leave it on Free, then drag the selection box over the part you want to keep. Drag inside the box to move it and drag the corner handle to resize it, then download the crop as PNG, JPG or WebP. It is free, with no signup and no upload, because the crop runs in your browser — and the download is rendered from your full-resolution original, not the small preview, so nothing you keep loses sharpness.

Cropping an image means cutting away part of it to change its shape or focus — turning a wide landscape photo into a square for Instagram, or cutting a stranger out of the edge of a group shot. It is different from resizing: resizing changes the whole picture's pixel dimensions, while cropping removes content. The aspect ratio is the width-to-height proportion of the frame: 1:1 is square, 16:9 is wide, 9:16 is tall.

How to crop an image in 4 steps

Step 1: upload your image — it is read in your browser and never uploaded. Step 2: pick a ratio preset or leave it on Free and drag a rectangle on the image. Step 3: move the selection with a drag inside it, resize it from the corner handle, and use the rule-of-thirds grid to place your subject on a crossing point. Step 4: pick PNG, JPG or WebP and download. If the platform will crop your image anyway, crop to its ratio here first — then it has nothing left to cut.

FAQ

Is this image cropper really free?

Yes. Crop as many images as you like, free, with no signup, no watermark and no upload — everything runs in your browser.

Does cropping reduce image quality?

No. Cropping only cuts away pixels outside the frame — the pixels you keep are untouched, so the kept area stays exactly as sharp as the source. Saving as JPG or WebP re-encodes once at the quality you pick; PNG is lossless.

What size should I crop for Instagram?

A square post is 1080 by 1080 (1:1), a portrait post is 1080 by 1350 (4:5), and Stories and Reels are 1080 by 1920 (9:16). Instagram shows profile pictures as a circle, so crop a square with the face centred and space around the head.

Why does Instagram crop my photo badly?

Because your photo's ratio does not match the platform's. Instagram crops uploads to its own ratios, usually from the centre — which cuts off heads, text and edges. Crop to the right ratio here first, and the platform has nothing left to cut.

What is the difference between cropping and resizing?

Cropping cuts part of the picture away to change its shape or focus. Resizing changes the whole picture's pixel dimensions. Crop first to get the shape right, then resize if you need exact pixels.

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. Your image is cropped in your browser with the canvas element. Nothing is uploaded, stored, or shared.

Related questions

Can I crop an image on my phone? Yes — this page works in a mobile browser. Upload, drag the selection with your finger, and download.

Can I crop to an exact pixel size? The selection shows its size in source pixels as you drag. Pick the closest ratio preset, then resize the box until the numbers match what you need.

Why is my cropped image smaller than the original? Because cropping removes pixels — a crop can only ever be as big as the part you kept. If you need the crop at a larger size, resize it afterwards (see the Image Resizer), knowing upscaling always softens it a little.

Does cropping remove the background? No — cropping only cuts the frame. To remove a background you need a background-removal tool, which cuts around the subject instead of the frame.
