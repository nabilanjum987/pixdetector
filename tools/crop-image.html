The problem: the platform crops your photo — badly

The frustration people repeat most is losing control of the crop. You upload a photo to Instagram and it crops it to its own ratio, usually from the centre — cutting off a head, a product edge, or the text you carefully placed. A profile picture is worse: Instagram shows it as a circle, so the corners of any square upload disappear, and a face near the edge gets clipped. The same happens with link previews (Facebook wants 1200×630), YouTube thumbnails (1280×720) and Stories (9:16) — every platform has its own shape, and guessing wrong means the platform crops for you. People want to choose the crop themselves, at the exact ratio, before they upload anywhere.

How do I crop an image online for free?

Upload your image here, pick an aspect ratio (1:1 for a square post or profile picture, 16:9 for a YouTube thumbnail, 9:16 for a Story or Reel, 4:3 for a classic photo) or leave it on Free, then drag the selection box over the part you want to keep. Drag inside the box to move it and drag the corner handle to resize it, then download the crop as PNG, JPG or WebP. It is free, with no signup and no upload, because the crop runs in your browser — and the download is rendered from your full-resolution original, not the small preview, so nothing you keep loses sharpness.

Cropping an image means cutting away part of it to change its shape or focus — turning a wide landscape photo into a square for Instagram, or cutting a stranger out of the edge of a group shot. It is different from resizing: resizing changes the whole picture's pixel dimensions, while cropping removes content. The aspect ratio is the width-to-height proportion of the frame: 1:1 is square, 16:9 is wide, 9:16 is tall.

How to crop an image in 4 steps

Step 1: upload your image — it is read in your browser and never uploaded. Step 2: pick a ratio preset or leave it on Free and drag a rectangle on the image. Step 3: move the selection with a drag inside it, resize it from the corner handle, and use the rule-of-thirds grid to place your subject on a crossing point. Step 4: pick PNG, JPG or WebP and download. If the platform will crop your image anyway, crop to its ratio here first — then it has nothing left to cut.

Circle crop, done right: an image file can't actually be round — what you're making is a square picture with the corners turned transparent. Crop first, compress later

Cropping before compression is more stable than compressing first and cropping later. Crop to the shape you need here, then shrink the file with an image compressor if it is still too big — that way the compressor works on the pixels you actually keep, not on edges you were going to cut anyway. One warning: saving as JPG or WebP re-encodes the image once, which can soften fine texture slightly; PNG is lossless, so if every pixel matters, download PNG.

Batch cropping

This tool crops one image at a time — there is no batch mode or ZIP export here, and the page won't pretend otherwise. If you need the same crop applied to many images, batch tools such as Vayce offer per-file framing with a "Download all as ZIP" export, and iLoveIMG supports modifying multiple images at the same time.

FAQ

How do I crop an image online for free?

Open a free browser cropper, upload or paste your image, drag the crop box to frame what you want to keep, and download — no account needed. This tool runs entirely in your browser, so nothing uploads.

Does cropping reduce image quality?

No — cropping only removes pixels around the edges; the pixels you keep are untouched. A cropped PNG stays lossless; a cropped JPEG keeps its original quality unless the tool re-encodes it.

How do I crop an image to an exact size in pixels?

Use the width × height inputs on this page to set the selection to exact source pixels, then download. Drag-only croppers need a second resize step to hit exact pixels.

What is the difference between cropping and resizing?

Cropping removes part of the image; resizing changes its dimensions. Cropping changes framing, resizing changes scale. Crop first to get the shape right, then resize if you need exact pixels.

Can I crop an image on my phone?

Yes — this tool works in a mobile browser on iOS and Android; pick from Photos, drag the handles with your finger, and download.

What aspect ratio should I use for a profile picture?

1:1 square — most avatar uploads want a square; crop to 1:1 first, then export. Instagram shows profile pictures as a circle, so keep the face centred with space around the head.

How do I crop a photo into a circle?

Crop to a square first (otherwise you get an oval), turn on Circle mode on this page, and download as PNG — JPG has no transparency, so the corners turn white or black. An image file can't actually be round: a circle crop is a square picture with the corners turned transparent.

Is it safe to crop an ID photo or screenshot online?

Only with an in-browser tool — the crop happens on your device and nothing uploads. Server-upload tools copy your image elsewhere, so check before cropping sensitive images. This tool never uploads.

Can I crop a GIF?

Not with this tool — it handles still images (JPG, PNG, WebP). For GIFs, EZGIF is the most-used free GIF cropper: no account and exact dimensions, though its interface looks dated. Kapwing and VEED crop GIFs but add signup and export limits.

What size should I crop a YouTube thumbnail / Instagram post to?

Common targets from the guides: square feed post 1080 × 1080, vertical story 1080 × 1920, link preview 1200 × 628, profile picture 400 × 400 — always verify the platform's current guidelines.

Do I need to install anything to crop an image?

No — this tool is a web page, not an app, which is handy on locked-down work laptops where you cannot install software.

Can I crop multiple images at once?

Not with this tool — it crops one image at a time, and there is no batch mode or ZIP export here. Batch tools such as Vayce crop a batch with per-file framing and ZIP export, and iLoveIMG supports modifying multiple images at the same time.

Will cropping remove the background?

No — cropping only trims the edges of the frame. Removing a background needs a background-removal tool, not a cropper.

Where does the downloaded cropped image go?

Wherever your browser normally puts downloads — usually the Downloads folder on desktop, or Files/Photos on mobile; the original on your device is untouched.

Should I crop before or after compressing?

Crop first — cropping before compression is more stable than compressing first and cropping later.
