The problem: every converter shows a different number, and none of them says when

Ask three currency converters what 100 dollars is in euros and you will get three different answers — and most of them will not tell you why. One is using this morning's rate, one is using yesterday's, and one has quietly baked in a margin without saying so. The travel forums are full of the same story: someone budgeted a trip on a converter's number, then found the hotel cost 10% more because the bank's rate, the card's fee and the weekend's stale rate all piled up. The worst version of this is a converter that fails silently — the rate feed goes down and the page keeps showing an old number as if it were live, or quietly substitutes 1.0 and tells you 100 dollars equals 100 euros. What people want is not another number. They want a number they can trust: the rate, the date of the rate, the source of the rate, and an honest admission when the rate is not available.

A currency converter multiplies your amount by the exchange rate — the price of one currency in another. If 1 US dollar equals 0.89 euros, then 100 dollars converts to about 89 euros. Rates move all day as banks trade, so any converter is only as good as the freshness of its rate — which is why the rate date matters as much as the number.

How to convert currency in 3 steps

Step 1: type the amount — 100, for example. Step 2: pick the currency you have in From and the one you want in To, or tap a popular pair; the ⇄ button swaps them. Step 3: press Convert and read the result: the converted amount, the rate (1 USD = 0.89 EUR), the inverse rate, and the date of the rate. Press Refresh rates any time to pull the latest available rate.

Mid-market, bank rate, and what you actually pay

The mid-market rate is the wholesale price large banks trade at with each other — the fair benchmark, and the rate this page shows. Your bank, card network or transfer service adds a spread on top, typically 0.5% to 4%, plus sometimes a fixed fee. So if this page says 100 USD = 89.34 EUR and your bank offers 87.50 EUR, the bank kept about 2% — that is normal, and now you can measure it instead of guessing. Airport and hotel counters are the worst deal of all, often 5–10% below mid-market.

What this converter cannot do

It cannot tell you what your bank will charge — only your provider can. It cannot convert cryptocurrency, and it cannot give you a real-time ticking rate: the ECB reference rate updates once a day, so intraday moves are not reflected. On weekends the rate shown is Friday's closing rate, because the foreign-exchange market is closed. All of that is stated on the result itself, next to the date.

FAQ

Is this currency converter really free?

Yes. Convert any amount between 32 major currencies, free, with no signup and no ads. Rates come from the free Frankfurter API, which publishes European Central Bank reference rates.

Where do the exchange rates come from?

From the Frankfurter API (api.frankfurter.app), a free keyless service that republishes the European Central Bank's daily reference rates. Every result on this page shows the date of the rate it used, so you can see exactly how fresh it is.

Why is the rate my bank gives me different?

This converter shows the mid-market (interbank) rate — the fair benchmark rate banks trade at with each other. Your bank, card network or transfer service adds a margin of roughly 0.5% to 4% plus sometimes a fixed fee, so what you actually receive is a little less. Use this page to check the benchmark, then compare it with the quote you are offered.

What happens if the rate service is down?

The page shows a clear error — 'Live rates unavailable' — and no conversion at all. It never shows yesterday's rate as if it were live. Wait a few minutes and press Refresh rates to try again.

Can I use this for a large transfer or a business payment?

Only as a planning estimate. Rates move during the day, the ECB reference rate updates once a day, and your provider will add its own margin. For a large or time-sensitive transfer, always confirm the exact rate with your bank or transfer provider on the day.

Is my conversion data sent anywhere?

Only the two currency codes are sent to the Frankfurter rate API to fetch the rate — no amount, no name, no account details and nothing stored. Your amounts are calculated in your browser and never leave your device.
