The problem: no dice on the table, and nobody trusts a hidden roll

Board game night without the dice box, a D&D session over video call, a classroom probability lesson — the moment someone needs a die that is not there, the search starts. The complaints about online dice rollers are not about the maths. They are about trust and clutter: players suspect the roller is rigged after a bad streak, the tool is buried in ads, it only rolls one d6, or it hides the individual dice and shows only a total. On forums for virtual tabletops, the same thread comes up every few months — "the rolls are either super high or super low" — and the answer is always the same: that is what random looks like in a small sample. What players actually want is a roller that shows every die, adds the total honestly, and says plainly how the numbers are drawn.

The other half of the complaints is about notation. To a new D&D player, 4d6kh3, 2d20kl1 and the ! for exploding dice read like a foreign language — and plain click-a-die tools cannot express them at all. So the player either goes back to physical dice or opens a second tool. This page puts both on one screen: type the notation if you know it, tap a die if you do not.

How do I roll dice online? The direct answer

Pick a die — d4 to d20 — choose how many to roll, add a modifier if your game uses one, and press ROLL DICE, or type notation like 4d6kh3 in the notation box. Each die is shown, then added up with your modifier into one total. It is free, with no signup, and every face has the same chance.

A dice roller is a virtual replacement for physical dice: it picks a number from 1 to the number of sides, once per die, and adds the results. A d20 is the twenty-sided die used in Dungeons & Dragons; a d6 is the ordinary six-sided die from board games. Rolling 2d6+3 means roll two six-sided dice, add them, then add 3.

How to roll dice in 3 steps

Step 1: choose your die type — d6 for board games, d20 for D&D. Step 2: set how many dice (up to 6) and a modifier, such as +3 for a strength bonus or -2 for a penalty. Step 3: press ROLL DICE (or hit Space) and read each die plus the total. Your last 10 rolls stay in the history below.

Is this dice roller really random — or rigged?

It is random, and the streaks are normal. Each die is drawn independently with crypto.getRandomValues, the browser's secure random source, so a roll has no memory of the last one. A run of low numbers never makes a high number "due" — that feeling is the gambler's fallacy, and it is why bad streaks feel rigged when they are not. In fact, computer dice are fairer than most physical dice: real dice can be slightly weighted or unevenly shaped, while a good digital draw gives every face exactly equal odds. The draw uses rejection sampling: raw values that would favour low faces are thrown away, so every face has exactly equal odds. The wheel and number tools in this family shuffle with the Fisher–Yates algorithm; dice need no shuffle, because each die is drawn independently. If your whole group rolls at the same moment and gets similar results, that is coincidence in a small sample, not a shared seed.

What do the colours mean?

On a d20, a natural 20 — the highest face — is shown in green: a critical hit in D&D. A natural 1 is shown in red: a critical miss. The colours only flag standout dice; they never change the total. The modifier is added after the dice are summed, exactly as the rules of most tabletop games require.

FAQ

How do I roll dice online for D&D?

Pick the die (d4 to d20, or d100) or type the notation — for example 1d20+5 for an attack, 3d6 for damage — press ROLL DICE, and read each die plus the total.

What does 4d6kh3 mean?

Roll four six-sided dice and keep the highest three — the standard way to generate D&D ability scores. Dropping the lowest die shifts the average from 10.5 to about 12.24.

What are advantage and disadvantage in D&D?

Roll two d20s and keep the highest (advantage, written 2d20kh1) or the lowest (disadvantage, 2d20kl1). The toggle on this page sets that up for you.

What are exploding dice?

When a die rolls its maximum value it rolls again and adds — explosions can chain if you hit the maximum again. Tick “Exploding dice” to turn this on.

How do I type dice notation?

Type it in the notation box: NdS for N dice with S sides, + or - a modifier, khN to keep the highest N, klN to keep the lowest N, and ! for exploding dice. Examples: 4d6kh3, 1d20+5, 2d20kh1, 3d6!.

Which dice can I roll?

d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20 and d100 — tap a die button for the beginner route, or type any of them in the notation box. Roll 1 to 20 dice at once.

How does the modifier work?

The modifier is added to the total after the dice are rolled. Rolling 2d6+3 means roll two six-sided dice, add them together, then add 3.

What do kept and discarded dice mean?

When you keep only some dice (for example kh3 out of 4d6), the kept dice count towards the total and the discarded ones are shown dimmed with a line through them, so you can see exactly what was dropped and why.

What is a natural 20 or a natural 1?

On a d20, a natural 20 is the highest possible roll — a critical hit in Dungeons & Dragons — and a natural 1 is a critical miss. This roller highlights them in green and red.

Are the rolls really random?

Yes. Each die is drawn with your browser's secure random source (crypto.getRandomValues()) with rejection sampling, so every face has exactly equal odds. Some well-known rollers honestly state they use Math.random() instead — fine for games, but not for a roll anyone might challenge.

Why do I keep rolling low numbers?

Random has no memory. A bad streak feels rigged, but in a small sample it is expected — five rolls coming up 3, 3, 4, 3, 5 looks rigged and is not. A bad streak never makes a good roll “due”.

Can I roll more than one die at once?

Yes — up to 20 dice in one roll, either by setting the count or by typing notation like 8d6. Every die is shown separately, kept or discarded, before the total.

Does the roller keep a history?

Yes — your last 20 rolls stay visible below the results, with the notation and the total, so the whole table can check a roll. Press “Clear history” to wipe it.

Can I use this roller for games other than D&D?

Yes — d6 covers board games, d20 covers D&D ability checks and attacks, and d100 covers percentile rolls. The notation box covers any combination a tabletop game asks for.

Is this dice roller really free?

Yes. Roll as many times as you like with no signup, no payment and no app to install. Everything runs in your browser.
