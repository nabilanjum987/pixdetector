The problem: the favicon is blurry, or it never shows up at all

Favicon complaints come in two flavours, and both are maddening. The first is the blurry 16-pixel icon: a detailed logo that looks perfect at 512 pixels turns into grey mush in a browser tab, because at 16×16 there are only 256 pixels to draw with — and if the artwork's edges do not line up with the pixel grid, every stroke comes out half-grey. The second is the favicon that simply never appears: the file is in the wrong folder, the HTML link tag is missing or mistyped, or the browser is showing a cached old icon from days ago and no amount of refreshing seems to fix it. People want a free generator that shows the icon at its real sizes before they download it, gives them a proper multi-size .ico file (not a renamed PNG), and hands them the exact HTML to paste — so the icon actually shows up.

A favicon is the tiny icon a browser shows in the tab, in bookmarks, and on a phone's home screen — the smallest but most-seen piece of a site's branding. A favicon generator is a tool that turns one logo into the set of files browsers ask for: favicon.ico, a multi-size container holding 16, 32 and 48-pixel versions for tabs and bookmarks, plus PNG sizes for retina tabs (32), and the 180-pixel Apple touch icon for iOS home screens. Modern browsers prefer a PNG or SVG when one is linked, but the .ico is still the fallback older browsers and Google read.

Why is my favicon not showing up?

Almost always one of four things. First, the files are not where the HTML says they are — upload them to the site's root folder and open the favicon.ico URL directly in your browser to check it loads. Second, the link tag is missing or mistyped — use the snippet from this page, with the leading slash. Third, the browser cached the old icon (or the absence of one): hard-refresh with Ctrl+Shift+R, test in an incognito window, or add ?v=2 to the href to force a fresh fetch. Fourth, Google search results show favicons on their own schedule — a new favicon can take days to appear there even when it works in tabs. If the icon loads at its direct URL and shows in incognito, the setup is right and the rest is cache.

Should the favicon background be transparent?

Transparent is fine for browser tabs, but it can halo on dark tabs, and iOS renders a transparent Apple touch icon on black. A solid background is the safer choice for the 180-pixel icon.

No. Your image is resized and the .ico file is built in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, stored or shared.
