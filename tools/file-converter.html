About This Tool

Every image on the web lives in one of a handful of formats, and each one is a trade-off. PNG keeps every pixel exactly and supports transparency, which makes it perfect for logos, screenshots and graphics — but photos saved as PNG can be enormous. JPG throws away detail your eyes barely notice and shrinks a photo to a fraction of its size, which is why it is still the default for cameras and websites. WebP is the modern middle ground: it compresses photos even smaller than JPG while keeping transparency like PNG, and almost every browser and phone made in the last few years supports it. The problem is that files arrive in the wrong format all the time — a screenshot that needs to go in an email, a logo that has to be a JPG for a form that rejects PNGs, a photo that would load twice as fast as WebP. This converter fixes that in seconds, without installing software or handing your files to a stranger's server.

It works entirely in your browser. When you drop in an image, the page draws it onto a canvas — the same drawing surface browsers use for games and photo editors — and saves the canvas back out in the format you picked. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is queued on a remote machine, and there is no watermark or file-size limit beyond what your own device can handle. You can convert up to 20 images at once, and each one shows its original size next to its converted size so you can see exactly what the switch saved you. The quality slider only applies to JPG and WebP, because those are lossy formats where you choose the balance between size and detail; PNG is lossless, so its output is always pixel-perfect regardless of any setting.

Be clear about what this tool is not. It converts images only. It does not convert audio files like MP3 or WAV, video files like MP4 or MOV, documents like PDF, Word or Excel, or archives like ZIP and RAR — those need completely different software, and any site promising to convert everything in one box is usually uploading your files to a server to do it. Here, if it is not an image your browser can open, the file is simply declined. That limitation is deliberate: it is what lets the conversion stay private, instant and free.

Which format should you choose?

The right format depends on what the image is and where it is going. The short version:

FormatBest forTransparencyGood to know
PNGLogos, screenshots, graphics, anything needing transparencyYesLossless — every pixel is kept exactly. Files are bigger, especially for photos.
JPGPhotos, email attachments, forms that only accept JPGNo — becomes whiteThe smallest choice for photos. Quality below 80% starts to show visible artefacts.
WebPWebsites, social posts, anywhere modern browsers are usedYesSmaller than JPG at the same quality, with transparency. The best default for the web.

A practical rule of thumb: photos and screenshots you want small go to JPG or WebP; anything with transparency or sharp text and lines stays PNG. If you are converting for a website, WebP usually wins — the same photo at the same quality is often 25–35% smaller than its JPG version, which means faster page loads. If you are converting for a form, a printer or an older app, JPG is the safest bet because nothing rejects it.

FAQ

Is this file converter really free?

Yes. Convert as many images as you like between PNG, JPG and WebP, with no watermark, no sign-up and no daily limit. The tool runs entirely in your browser, so there is nothing to pay for and no account to create.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. Every conversion happens on your own device using your browser's built-in image tools. Your files are never sent to a server, stored, or seen by anyone else — close the tab and they are gone.

What files can I convert with this tool?

Images only — PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF, BMP and any other image format your browser can open. It does not convert audio, video, documents, spreadsheets, PDFs or archive files such as ZIP.

Will converting to JPG or WebP make my file smaller?

Usually, yes — often a lot smaller. JPG and WebP use lossy compression, so a PNG screenshot or photo can shrink to a fraction of its original size. Use the quality slider to balance file size against visible detail: 85–92% looks near-identical for most photos.

What happens to transparency when I convert a PNG to JPG?

JPG has no transparency, so see-through areas are filled with white before saving. If you need to keep transparency, convert to PNG or WebP instead — both keep the transparent areas intact.
