About This Tool

Photos straight from a phone or camera are far bigger than they need to be. A single phone photo can be 4–8 MB, which makes websites load slowly, eats mobile data, and gets rejected by forms and email attachments with size limits. This compressor shrinks your images right in your browser — no upload, no account, no watermark, and no limit on how many files you process. Drop in one photo or a whole batch, pick a format and a quality level, and download the smaller files straight away.

Compression works in two ways. First, the quality slider controls lossy compression for JPEG and WebP: the encoder throws away fine detail your eyes cannot see, which is why a photo can lose 70% of its file size and still look identical. Second, the optional resize step shrinks the image dimensions — a 4000 px wide photo displayed at 800 px on a website is carrying five times more pixels than anyone will ever see. Combine a sensible resize with quality 80 and most photos end up at 10–20% of their original size.

One honest warning: PNG files compress very little. PNG is a lossless format, which means it keeps every single pixel exactly as it is — re-saving a PNG just re-packs the same data, typically saving only a few percent. If a PNG is too big, the saving route is not a better PNG but a different approach: resize it down, or convert it to WebP, which keeps near-identical quality at a fraction of the size. The one exception is PNGs with transparency — JPEG has no transparency channel, so converting a transparent PNG to JPEG fills the see-through areas with white. Keep those as PNG or WebP, which both support transparency.

Which format should you use?

The right format depends on what the image is for. Photos compress best as JPEG or WebP; graphics, screenshots and anything with transparency belong in PNG or WebP. The short version:

FAQ

Is the image compressor really free?

Yes. Compress as many images as you like, with no sign-up, no watermark and no file-size limit beyond what your browser can hold in memory. The tool runs entirely in your browser, so there is nothing to pay for on our side either.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Your images never leave your device. Every step — reading the file, drawing it to a canvas and re-encoding it — happens locally in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, stored, or seen by anyone else.

Why does my PNG barely shrink?

PNG is a lossless format, so it cannot throw away image data the way JPEG and WebP can. Re-saving a PNG only re-packs the same pixels, which usually saves a few percent at most. If you need a much smaller file, resize the image or convert it to WebP, which keeps near-identical quality at a fraction of the size.

Which format should I choose — JPEG, WebP or PNG?

Choose WebP for the smallest files on websites and social media — it beats JPEG at the same quality and supports transparency. Choose JPEG for maximum compatibility, since every device and app opens it. Choose PNG only when you need pixel-perfect, lossless quality, such as screenshots with text or graphics with transparency.

Will compressing my images make them look worse?

At quality 80 or above, JPEG and WebP compression is almost impossible to notice on photos — the savings come from data your eyes cannot see anyway. Below 60 you may start to see blocky artefacts or soft edges, especially in flat areas of colour.
