About This Tool

Every day, useful text gets trapped inside images: a receipt you need to expense, a whiteboard full of meeting notes, a screenshot of an error message, a page from a book, a business card, a handwritten note. Retyping it all by hand is slow and error-prone. This image to text converter — also called OCR, short for optical character recognition — reads the words in your picture and turns them into text you can copy, edit, search and save.

It works with the kinds of images people actually have: phone photos of printed pages, screenshots, scanned documents, photos of whiteboards and noticeboards, and even product labels. The recognition is powered by Tesseract.js, the well-tested open-source OCR engine, running entirely in your browser. That matters for privacy: unlike most online converters, your image is never uploaded to a server, never stored, and never seen by anyone else. A photo of a confidential document stays on your own device from start to finish, and the tool works the same whether the image is a personal note or a work document.

Accuracy depends mostly on the quality of the image. Clean, well-lit printed text on a plain background is usually recognised at over 95% accuracy, and the tool shows you a confidence score so you know how much to trust the result. Blurry photos, very small text, decorative or script fonts, handwriting and text photographed at a steep angle are all harder — the engine will still try, but expect a few mistakes. That is why the extracted text lands in an editable box: a quick skim to fix any misread letters takes seconds, and it is still far faster than typing a whole page from scratch. For the best results, photograph the page straight on in good light, fill the frame with the text, and crop away borders, shadows and anything that is not words.

What can you use extracted text for?

Once text is out of the image, it becomes useful in ways a picture never is. Students copy textbook pages and lecture slides into their notes instead of retyping them. Office workers pull figures off scanned invoices and receipts into spreadsheets. Developers paste the text of an error message from a screenshot straight into a search engine. Researchers digitise pages from old books and archives so the words become searchable. And anyone who has ever tried to copy text out of a screenshot knows the frustration this tool removes — what took ten minutes of typing now takes ten seconds, with a quick proofread at the end.

The tool is deliberately simple: no account, no upload queue, no watermark on anything, and no limit on how many images you convert. Because everything runs locally, it also works well on a slow connection once the page has loaded — the only download is the OCR engine itself, which your browser caches for next time.

FAQ

Is this image to text converter really free?

Yes. You can convert as many images to text as you like with no watermark, no sign-up and no usage limit. The OCR runs entirely in your browser using Tesseract.js, so there is nothing to pay for and no account to create.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Your image never leaves your device. The text recognition runs locally in your browser with Tesseract.js, so photos of documents, receipts, whiteboards or handwritten notes stay private on your own computer or phone.

Which image formats can I convert to text?

Any image your browser can display: JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP. Screenshots, phone photos of printed pages, scanned documents and even photos of whiteboards all work — the clearer and straighter the text, the better the result.

How accurate is the OCR?

On clean, well-lit printed text it is usually over 95% accurate. Accuracy drops on blurry photos, very small text, decorative fonts, handwriting or text at a steep angle. For best results, use a sharp, straight photo with good contrast and crop out anything that is not text.

Can I edit the extracted text before copying it?

Yes. The extracted text appears in an editable text box, so you can fix any misread characters, tidy up line breaks and remove anything you do not need before copying it or downloading it as a .txt file.
