The problem: a layout with no words in it

Designers and developers keep hitting the same small problem: the layout is ready, the real copy is not, and an empty box tells you nothing about how the page will look. Real text is the wrong filler — reviewers start reading and judging the words instead of the design — and obvious filler like “text text text” tells you nothing about how a paragraph feels at full length. The complaints are practical: generators that only give one fixed block, generators that give a random amount with no exact count, and the classic embarrassment — lorem ipsum shipped to production because nobody searched the built site before deploy. People also argue about whether placeholder text is even right: for final designs and forms, real content reveals length problems that lorem hides, and for internationalised designs, Latin filler hides how much longer German or French text runs. What people want is exact: 3 paragraphs for an article body, 1 for a card, 20 words for a button tooltip — with the classic opener on when stakeholders expect it, and HTML tags when the text goes straight into markup.

How much placeholder text do you need?

One or two paragraphs for hero sections and cards, three to five for article bodies. Generate slightly more than you need and trim — it is easier to cut filler than to generate again mid-review. For a button tooltip or a form label, generate words, not paragraphs: a single sentence is already too much. And keep the classic opener on when stakeholders expect the traditional first line; turn it off when you want a fully fresh passage.

When you should NOT use lorem ipsum

Do not use it for final designs and mockups for review — real content reveals length problems lorem hides, and an “About Us” page does not fit in three sentences. Do not use it for forms and labels, which need real words to test whether they fit, wrap or truncate. Do not use it for internationalised designs — test with the real strings instead, because Latin filler hides how much longer German or French text runs. And never ship it to production: search your built site for “lorem” before every deploy. Placeholder text is a development tool, not a design deliverable — use it to make structural decisions, then replace it with realistic content as early as possible.

Where lorem ipsum comes from — a 2,000-year-old accident

Lorem ipsum is scrambled Latin from Cicero's De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum (“On the Ends of Good and Evil”), written in 45 BC, sections 1.10.32–1.10.33. A printer scrambled the passage in the 1500s into a type specimen, and Latin scholar Richard McClintock traced the source in the 1980s–90s. Letraset dry-transfer sheets in the 1960s printed it to demonstrate typefaces, then Aldus PageMaker in the mid-1980s shipped it in its templates, and desktop publishing spread it to QuarkXPress, InDesign and every design tool since. The word “lorem” itself is not even a real Latin word: a printer dropped the first two letters of “dolorem”. That is why the passage has the shape and rhythm of real prose without being readable — which is exactly what makes it useful as filler, and exactly what makes it embarrassing when it ships.

How do I generate Lorem Ipsum in Microsoft Word?

Type =LOREM() and press Enter — Word auto-generates paragraphs of standard Lorem Ipsum. =RAND(10) inserts ten paragraphs of randomised English text instead. Word's built-in trick gives a fixed standard passage with no count, theme or HTML control; a generator gives you exact counts and formats.
