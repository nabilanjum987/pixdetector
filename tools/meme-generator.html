A meme generator is a tool that puts caption text on an image — usually one line at the top that sets the scene and one at the bottom that delivers the punchline. The classic style is heavy, condensed, all-caps text in white with a thick black outline, which stays readable over bright and dark photos alike. Good memes are short: if the joke needs more than two lines, it is probably two memes.

What makes a meme actually funny?

Short beats long: the best memes are one setup line and one punchline, both readable in two seconds. Specific beats generic: "when the Wi-Fi goes down during the final" lands harder than "when the Wi-Fi goes down". The picture should do half the work — pick an image whose face or situation already tells the story, then let the text finish it. And check it on your phone before posting: text that fits on a desktop preview can cover the whole face on a small screen, so keep the size moderate and the lines short.

Square or vertical — which one?

Square fits feeds, group chats and most repost accounts, and it survives being cropped by almost any platform. Vertical 9:16 fills the whole phone screen on TikTok and Instagram Reels, which is where most memes are seen now — but it looks wrong in a square feed. This tool draws the same meme in either shape: the image is fitted to the shape you pick, so a wide photo in vertical mode will have space above and below it, and the text sits where it can be read.
