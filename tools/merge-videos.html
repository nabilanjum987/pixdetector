When merging clips works — and when it does not

Merging videos means joining separate clips end to end into a single video file. It is the edit behind almost every phone-shot story: three takes of the same scene, a morning clip and an afternoon clip, a reaction followed by the thing being reacted to. This tool joins clips in the order you set, in your browser, with no upload and no watermark. The output takes its size from the first clip, and clips with a different shape are cropped to fill the frame, so a mix of landscape and portrait footage comes out consistent rather than jumping between black bars.

Merging in the browser beats installing an editor when the job is small and the clips are short: a holiday video from five phone clips, a product demo stitched from three takes, a screen recording joined to a talking-head intro. Where it stops being the right tool is scale. Joining an hour of 4K footage, or twenty clips at 100 MB each, will be slow and heavy in any browser — that is a desktop job, and pretending otherwise would waste your afternoon. The same applies to sound: this export keeps the audio only when your browser allows it, so if the merged video needs its original audio guaranteed, use a desktop editor instead.

A practical tip: trim each clip before you merge, or at least know which parts you want. The merge mode uses every clip in full, so a clip with ten seconds of dead air at the end carries that dead air into the finished video. If one take has a bad ending, switch to Trim, cut it, and come back to Merge. And keep the tab in the foreground while exporting — merging runs in real time, roughly as long as all the clips put together, and a background tab can pause the encoder.

FAQ

Is merging videos online really free?

Yes. Join as many clips as your browser's memory can hold — no watermark, no sign-up, no daily cap and no upload. Everything runs in your browser.

How many clips can I merge at once?

There is no fixed clip-count cap here, unlike tools that stop at five clips. The real limit is your device: each clip is held in memory, so very many large clips will slow the browser down. Short phone clips merge fine in batches of ten or more.

Why is the merged video a WebM file?

Browsers can only re-encode video into formats they natively support, and WebM (VP8/VP9) is the one every modern browser can write. WebM plays in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari. If you need an MP4, convert the WebM afterwards with HandBrake or FFmpeg.

Does the merged video keep the original audio?

Yes, when your browser allows it — the merged video carries each clip’s original audio, joined in the same order as the pictures. If a browser blocks that, the export still finishes but is video-only, and the page tells you so. If your merged video needs its original sound guaranteed, use a desktop editor such as Shotcut or DaVinci Resolve instead.

Can I merge videos without uploading them to a server?

Yes. This tool merges entirely in your browser — your clips never leave your device, which is also why there is no file-size limit imposed by a server, only what your browser can handle.

What if my clips are different sizes or shapes?

The output size matches the first clip, and clips with a different aspect ratio are cropped to fill the frame. For the cleanest result, put the clip whose shape you want first, or resize the clips to match before merging.
