Browser metronomes drift — and that ruins practice

Musicians want a simple thing: a steady, accurate click with tap tempo, time signatures and subdivisions — free, in the browser, no app install. Social chatter on this is thin (almost nothing solid was found on social; one Instagram Reel, 27K likes, about a free browser metronome with tap tempo and subdivisions), but musician and developer sources name the real problems clearly, and they are technical, not cosmetic.

The biggest problem is drift. Most browser metronomes are built on setInterval, which lags, stutters and throttles — especially in background tabs — so the click wanders off the beat exactly when you are trying to lock into it. The second problem is missing features: basic tools lack tap tempo, odd time signatures and subdivisions, so you cannot practise 3/4, 6/8 or sixteenth-note patterns. The third is a limit of the whole category: a metronome cannot tell you whether you are actually in time — without a microphone it has no listening or feedback, so it can play the beat but never judge your playing. This page answers the first two directly: a Web Audio scheduler with lookahead (the direct answer to drift), plus tap tempo, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 and 6/8, quarter, eighth and sixteenth subdivisions, and an accent on beat 1.

How to practise with a metronome (and why drift matters)

Start slow — half the target tempo — and only speed up when you can play a passage cleanly three times in a row. Practise with subdivisions: eighth notes at a slow tempo teach even spacing better than quarter notes at a fast one. Use the accent on beat 1 to feel the bar, and turn the accent off once you can feel it yourself. Drift matters because practice only works if the reference is true: a click that wanders teaches you to follow a wandering beat. This tool's lookahead scheduler checks the audio clock every 25 ms and schedules clicks 0.1 seconds ahead, so the beat stays put even if the browser tab is busy — that is the whole point of building it this way.

Is this metronome really free?

Yes. It is free with no sign-up, no app install and no watermark. It runs entirely in your browser and works offline after the page loads.

Why is this metronome more accurate than other browser metronomes?

Most browser metronomes use setInterval, which lags, stutters and throttles in background tabs, so the click drifts. This one uses Web Audio lookahead scheduling: it checks the audio clock every 25 ms and schedules clicks 0.1 seconds ahead, so the beat stays steady even when the tab is busy.

Can the metronome tell me if I am playing in time?

No — and no metronome without a microphone can. It only plays the click; it cannot listen to your playing or tell you whether you rushed or dragged. Use it as a steady reference, and record yourself to check your timing.

Why does the click sound late on Bluetooth headphones?
