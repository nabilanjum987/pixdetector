Cutting an MP3 should take seconds — so why is it a chore?

Most people who search for an MP3 cutter want one simple thing: keep these exact seconds, throw the rest away. A ringtone from a favourite song, the good part of a voice note, an intro for a podcast, a clip for a video. It is a quiet, utilitarian job — demand is not loud on social media, and most of what exists is tool pages' own troubleshooting docs and old forum threads rather than excited posts — but it is a job people need done cleanly, right now.

And cleanly is where basic cutters fail. Three complaints come up again and again. First, re-encoding loses quality or changes volume — one well-known editor's own docs note that imports can “increase the gain slightly so that it clips”, so the exported clip sounds louder, harsher or thinner than the original. Second, file-size limits and slow processing on large files: upload, wait, cut, wait again. Third, no fine control — no waveform to see what you are cutting, no precise times, no fade, no noise removal. Some of the top tools also push paid extras or charge for WAV export. This page answers with a waveform you can click, exact second inputs, preview before you download, and fades — all in your browser, with no upload and no account.

What this tool does — and what it does not do

What it does: open MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG and other common audio, show a real waveform, cut to exact seconds, preview the selection or the full file, add fade in/out, and export a lossless WAV clip. The whole job happens on your device, so there is no upload wait and no privacy worry.

What it does not do: it cannot export MP3 directly, it cannot remove noise or change EQ, and it cannot promise millisecond-perfect cuts or zero quality change — cutting with re-encode always risks some change, and only lossless stream-copy preserves a file exactly, which limits format and fade options. For noise removal, EQ or multi-track editing, use a desktop editor.

How to get a clean cut

Cut on a natural pause or a beat, not in the middle of a word or a note — that is what makes a clip sound clean rather than chopped. Add a short fade out (the tool fades over 0.05 seconds at the edges) when a clip would otherwise end abruptly, and a fade in when it starts mid-sound. For ringtones, aim for 20–30 seconds and preview on your phone before setting it. For voice clips, keep a half-second of breathing room at the start so the first word is not clipped. If the exported WAV sounds different from the original, remember the export is lossless WAV — the difference, if any, comes from the decode and re-encode, not from compression.

FAQ

Is this MP3 cutter really free?

Yes. Cutting is free with no sign-up, no watermark and no upload. Your file is processed in your browser, and the WAV clip downloads directly to your device.

Why does it export WAV instead of MP3?

WAV is lossless, so the exported clip keeps full audio quality without compression loss. MP3 export needs an encoder this browser tool does not include — convert the WAV to MP3 in any player or editor if you need that format.

Will cutting reduce my audio quality?

Cutting with re-encoding always risks some quality change, and this tool re-encodes when it exports. The export itself is lossless WAV, which avoids compression loss, but it cannot promise zero change — only lossless stream-copy preserves a file exactly, and that limits format and fade options.

Can I cut precisely to the millisecond?

You can type exact times to 0.01 seconds and click the waveform to place cut points, which is precise enough for ringtones, clips and intros. True millisecond-perfect editing and multi-feature work (noise removal, EQ) are desktop jobs.

Is there a file-size limit?

There is no upload and no server limit, so nothing is capped by us. Very large files may take a few seconds to load and decode in your browser, and very long files are harder to cut precisely on a small screen.

Does my MP3 get uploaded anywhere?

No. The file is decoded in your browser and never leaves your device. Close the tab and it is gone — nothing is stored on our servers.
