    The problem: "my alarm didn't go off"

    The most common online-alarm complaints are the same everywhere: the alarm never rings because the tab was closed or the computer went to sleep; it rings but makes no sound because the system is muted, the volume is low, or the sound is routed to Bluetooth headphones still connected from the night before; or it rings and the sleeper sleeps straight through it. Power nappers have their own version of the failure — a nap without an alarm turns into a full sleep, and a nap longer than 20 minutes leaves you groggy and wrecks the following night. And on several popular tools you can only set one alarm at a time, so a wake-up plus a meeting reminder plus a nap will not fit. People want a loud, testable alarm they can set in seconds — and an honest answer about when a browser alarm can and cannot be trusted.

    How do I set an online alarm clock for free?

    Pick a time, add a label like "Wake up", and press Add alarm — or tap a quick preset. It rings while this tab stays open, with a loud beep that repeats until you press Stop or Snooze. Free, no signup, no app — but keep the tab open and the computer awake, or it cannot ring.

    An online alarm clock is a browser tool that plays a sound at a time you choose, without installing an app. Unlike a phone alarm, which is handled by the operating system, a browser alarm only exists while the page is open and the device is awake — that is the one limit that matters, and it is why this page says it upfront instead of hiding it in small print.

    How to set an alarm in 3 steps

    Step 1: choose a time and a label, then press Add alarm — or use a quick preset to ring in 10, 20, 30, 45, 60 or 90 minutes. Step 2: press Test sound to check the volume, and keep the tab open with the device awake. Step 3: when the alarm rings, press Stop alarm to turn it off, or Snooze to silence it for 5 minutes — a snoozed alarm is added automatically.

    The 20-minute nap rule

    A nap without an alarm is a sleep, not a nap. The rule from nap guides is simple: 20 minutes of sleep leaves you fresh; 30 to 60 minutes wakes you from deep sleep and leaves you groggy, and can also disturb the following night; around 90 minutes is a full sleep cycle and works if you have the time. Set the alarm for 25 minutes — 20 minutes of sleep plus about 5 minutes to drift off — and get up as soon as it rings. The "Nap 25 min" quick chip above does exactly that in one tap.

    What people use an online alarm for

    Wake-ups and morning routines are the obvious use, but most online alarms are set for shorter things: a 20-minute power nap, a study break, a meeting in 30 minutes, medication, laundry, or a parking meter. Labels matter most for that second group — "Take medicine" or "Leave in 10" tells you what to do the moment the alarm rings, instead of just telling you the time. Keep the list short: three alarms for waking up and three for the day is enough. More than that turns into noise.

    FAQ

    Is this online alarm clock really free?

    Yes. Set as many alarms as you like, free, with no signup, no app and no ads. Everything runs in your browser.

    Does an online alarm clock work if I close the browser?

    No. The alarm runs inside the browser tab. Close the tab or quit the browser and the alarm is lost. Minimising the window or switching to another tab is fine — the tab only needs to stay open.

    Will the alarm work if my computer goes to sleep?

    No. Sleep and hibernation suspend the browser, so the alarm will not fire. Keep the computer awake while the alarm is pending — on a laptop, plug it in and set sleep to Never for that period.

    Why didn't my online alarm go off?

    The usual causes are: the tab was closed, the laptop went to sleep, the volume was muted, the sound went to Bluetooth headphones, or the time was set to AM instead of PM. Work through the troubleshooter on this page, and test the alarm with the Test alarm in 1 minute button before relying on it.

    Why does my online alarm make no sound?

    Usually three causes: the system is muted or the volume is low, the sound is routed to Bluetooth headphones, or the browser blocked audio because you never clicked the page (autoplay policy). Click anywhere on the page after loading it, then press Test sound.

    Can I set multiple alarms at the same time?

    Yes. On this page you can set as many alarms as you like, each with its own time and label — for example 7:00 Wake up, 7:30 Leave, 12:30 Lunch. Some other tools allow only one alarm at a time, or cap it at five.

    How do I set an alarm online for free?

    Open the page, pick the hour and minute, add a label, and click Add alarm — no app, no account, no download. Or tap a quick preset like 10 min or Nap 25 min. Keep the tab open.

    Can I set an alarm for tomorrow morning?

    Yes. If the time you pick has already passed today, the alarm is scheduled for the same time tomorrow automatically. The page tells you when that happens. But a browser alarm is a backup for wake-ups — also set your phone alarm.

    Is an online alarm clock loud enough to wake me up?

    It plays at your device volume, so turn the system volume to maximum and press Test sound first. For heavy sleepers a phone alarm or dedicated app is more reliable than a browser tab, because it also works when the device is locked or asleep.

    Does an online alarm clock work on my phone?

    Yes, in a mobile browser, but less reliably — some phones mute browser audio when the screen locks and aggressively suspend background tabs. Keep the screen on, and treat a phone browser alarm as a reminder, not a wake-up.

    What is the difference between an alarm clock and a timer?

    An alarm rings at a specific time of day (7:00 AM); a timer counts down from a duration (20 minutes). Same page family, different job. For durations, use the free online timer on this site.

    Do online alarm clocks work without internet?

    After the page has loaded, yes — the alarm is JavaScript in the browser and fires offline. You need internet only to load the page.

    Can I use my own song or sound as the alarm?

    Not on this page — it has one built-in beep sound. Most free browser tools offer built-in tones only (bell, beep, rooster, siren); uploading a custom MP3 is rare on free browser tools, and was not verified for any top-ranked page in our research.

    How do I set a nap alarm for 20 minutes?

    Use the Nap 25 min quick chip: 20 minutes of sleep plus about 5 minutes to drift off. A 20-minute power nap leaves you fresh; 30 to 60 minutes wakes you from deep sleep and leaves you groggy.

    Is a browser alarm as reliable as my phone alarm?

    No. A phone alarm fires even with the app closed and the phone locked; a browser alarm needs the tab open and the computer awake. Use the browser alarm as a backup or for desk reminders, naps and meetings, and the phone alarm for wake-ups that cannot fail.
