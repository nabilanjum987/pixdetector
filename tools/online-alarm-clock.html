    The problem: "my alarm didn't go off"

    "My alarm didn't go off" is one of the most searched alarm complaints in the world — and the causes are almost always the same four. The tab was closed, so nothing was left to ring. The laptop went to sleep, and a sleeping computer runs no JavaScript. The volume was muted, or the alarm volume was turned down separately from the media volume. Or the time was set to AM when PM was meant — the classic 12-hour mistake. Add a fifth: too many alarms, stacked so thickly they become background noise you learn to ignore. People want a loud, reliable alarm they can set in seconds, with a label that says what it is for — and they want to know, honestly, when a browser alarm can and cannot be trusted.

    How do I set an online alarm clock for free?

    Pick the time, add a label like "Wake up" or "Meeting", and press Add alarm — or tap a quick preset like "in 30 minutes" and the alarm is set with the exact ring time worked out for you. The alarm rings while this tab stays open, even in the background, and a loud beep repeats until you press Stop alarm or Snooze. It is free, with no signup and no app. Before you rely on it, press Test sound once to confirm the beep is loud enough and your volume is on.

    An online alarm clock is a browser tool that plays a sound at a time you choose, without installing an app. Unlike a phone alarm, which is handled by the operating system, a browser alarm only exists while the page is open and the device is awake — that is the one limit that matters, and it is why this page says it upfront instead of hiding it in small print.

    How to set an alarm in 3 steps

    Step 1: choose a time and a label, then press Add alarm — or use a quick preset to ring in 10, 20, 30, 45, 60 or 90 minutes. Step 2: press Test sound to check the volume, and keep the tab open with the device awake. Step 3: when the alarm rings, press Stop alarm to turn it off, or Snooze to silence it for 5 minutes — a snoozed alarm is added automatically.

    What people use an online alarm for

    Wake-ups and morning routines are the obvious use, but most online alarms are set for shorter things: a 20-minute power nap, a study break, a meeting in 30 minutes, medication, laundry, or a parking meter. Labels matter most for that second group — "Take medicine" or "Leave in 10" tells you what to do the moment the alarm rings, instead of just telling you the time. Keep the list short: three alarms for waking up and three for the day is enough. More than that turns into noise.

    FAQ

    Is this online alarm clock really free?

    Yes. Set as many alarms as you like, free, with no signup, no app and no ads. Everything runs in your browser.

    Will the alarm ring if I switch tabs?

    Yes, the alarm rings while the tab is open, even in the background, as long as the device stays awake. If the tab is closed or the laptop sleeps, the alarm cannot sound — so keep the tab open and the device awake.

    Why didn't my online alarm go off?

    The usual causes are: the tab was closed, the laptop went to sleep, the volume was muted, or the time was set to AM instead of PM. Check all four, and test the sound with the Test sound button before relying on the alarm.

    Can I set more than one alarm?

    Yes. Add as many alarms as you need, each with its own time and label — for example 7:00 Wake up, 7:30 Leave, 12:30 Lunch. Each alarm rings at its own time.

    Does it have a snooze button?

    Yes. When an alarm rings, press Snooze to silence it for 5 minutes — a new snoozed alarm is added automatically. Press Stop alarm to turn it off completely.

    Can I use this as my morning wake-up alarm?

    You can, but be honest about the limits: a browser alarm only works while the tab is open and the device is awake. For a wake-up you must not miss, use this as a backup to your phone alarm, not a replacement for it.

    Related questions

    How do I set an alarm for 10 minutes from now? Tap the "10 min" quick preset — the alarm is set for exactly 10 minutes from now, with the ring time shown in the list.

    Can I set a repeating daily alarm? Not on this page — alarms here are one-time, for today. For a daily wake-up, use your phone's clock app, which repeats by design.

    Does the alarm work on my phone? Yes, in your phone's browser, as long as the tab stays open and the phone stays awake. Phones lock and sleep aggressively, so a phone browser alarm is a reminder, not a reliable wake-up.

    What is the difference between an alarm and a timer? An alarm rings at a clock time (7:00 AM). A timer rings after a duration (in 30 minutes). For durations, use the free online timer.
