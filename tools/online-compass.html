The problem: "online compass not working"

"Compass not working" is the most common thing people type after opening an online compass — and the reasons are almost always the same three. First, they opened it on a laptop or desktop, which has no magnetometer at all, and the page either showed a fake spinning needle or nothing at all. Second, they opened it on an iPhone, where Safari needs an extra permission for motion and orientation, and nobody told them to tap Allow. Third, the needle points the wrong way because the phone has never been calibrated, or because it is sitting next to a laptop, a speaker, or a magnetic phone case. People want a simple answer — which way is north, right now — and what they get instead is a page that pretends to know on hardware that cannot know. This page does the opposite: it reads the real sensor when one exists, and says so plainly when one does not.

How do I use an online compass for free?

Tap Start compass on your phone, allow motion access, and hold the phone flat — the dial turns live and shows your heading in degrees and as a direction like NE. It reads your phone's magnetometer, so it is free with no app or signup. A laptop or desktop has no such sensor, so this page tells you that honestly instead of faking a reading.

An online compass is a web page that reads the magnetometer — the tiny electronic compass chip — inside your phone or tablet, through the browser's DeviceOrientation sensor, and turns that reading into a heading. Magnetic north is where a compass needle points; true (geographic) north is the direction of the North Pole. The gap between them, called magnetic declination, is a few degrees and depends on where on Earth you are.

How to use the online compass in 4 steps

Step 1: open this page on your phone or tablet — not a desktop. Step 2: tap Start compass and allow motion access when asked (iPhone asks twice: once for the page, once for motion). Step 3: hold the phone flat, top edge pointing where you face, away from metal and magnets. Step 4: read the big number and direction — rotate until N reaches the top marker and you are facing north.

What to use it for — and what not to

Good uses: orienting a map on a hike, checking which way a window faces before buying a plant, aligning a satellite dish or solar panel, setting up a telescope, or satisfying simple curiosity about the direction you are looking. Not for: safety-critical navigation, surveying, or anywhere a wrong heading could put you in danger — a phone compass is accurate to about 5–10° after calibration, and that is plenty for everyday orientation but not for precision work.

FAQ

Is this online compass really free?

Yes. Free, no signup, no app to install, and no limit on use.

Why doesn't the compass work on my laptop?

Laptops and desktops do not have a magnetometer — the sensor this tool reads. No website can fix missing hardware. This page tells you that instead of showing a fake reading. Open it on your phone instead.

Why does my iPhone ask for permission?

iOS requires an explicit prompt before a website can read motion and orientation sensors. Tap Start compass, then Allow when Safari asks. Without it, the compass cannot start.

Why is my compass inaccurate?

Nearby metal, magnets, speakers, laptops and magnetic phone cases distort the magnetic field. Move away from them, remove a magnetic case, and wave the phone in a slow figure-8 to recalibrate.

Does the online compass use GPS or my location?

No. It only reads the magnetometer — the chip that senses magnetic fields. It does not know or care where on the planet you are, and nothing is sent to a server.

Does it show magnetic north or true north?

Magnetic north — that is what the phone sensor measures. True north is a few degrees away depending on your location; for everyday orientation the difference does not matter.

Related questions

Can I use the compass without internet? Yes — once the page has loaded, the compass runs entirely on your phone's hardware.

Do I need to install an app? No. It runs in your phone's browser.

How accurate is a phone compass? About 5–10° after calibration — fine for hiking and everyday orientation, not for surveying.

Why does the needle jump around? The sensor is picking up interference, usually metal or electronics nearby. Move away and recalibrate with a figure-8 wave.
