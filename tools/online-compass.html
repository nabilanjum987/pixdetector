The problem: "online compass not working"

The most common compass complaints are the same ones users report in forums and app reviews. First, the reading is wrong: magnetic interference from fridges, washing machines, speakers, magnets, metal cases and wireless chargers can skew a phone compass so much that readings differ wildly even within the same room — and for Qibla direction, the highest-stakes use case, a wrong reading is not a minor bug. Second, two devices disagree: one user photographed a Garmin watch showing 040° next to a phone compass showing about 127° at the same spot. Third, the compass gets stuck, showing only some directions and never others — a stuck or uncalibrated sensor. And fourth, people open a compass on a laptop or desktop, which has no magnetometer at all, and get a fake spinning needle or nothing at all. People want a simple answer — which way is north, right now — and what they get instead is a page that pretends to know on hardware that cannot know.

How do I use an online compass for free?

Tap Start compass on your phone, allow motion access, and hold the phone flat — the dial turns and shows your heading in degrees and as a direction like NE. It reads your phone's magnetometer, free with no app or signup. A laptop has no such sensor, so this page says so honestly and offers a demo mode instead.

An online compass is a web page that reads the magnetometer — the tiny electronic compass chip — inside your phone or tablet, through the browser's DeviceOrientation sensor, and turns that reading into a heading. Magnetic north is where a compass needle points; true (geographic) north is the direction of the North Pole. The gap between them, called magnetic declination, is a few degrees and depends on where on Earth you are.

How to use the online compass in 4 steps

Step 1: open this page on your phone or tablet — not a desktop. Step 2: tap Start compass and allow motion access when asked (iPhone asks twice: once for the page, once for motion). Step 3: hold the phone flat, top edge pointing where you face, away from metal and magnets. Step 4: read the big number and direction — rotate until N reaches the top marker and you are facing north.

How to calibrate: the figure-8 walkthrough

Phone magnetometers need calibrating before use — without it, headings can be off by several degrees to 45 degrees or more. Do this whenever the reading looks wrong, after dropping the phone, or when you change rooms:

Step 1: step away from metal — move away from fridges, washing machines, speakers, laptops, wireless chargers and magnetic cases; remove a magnetic phone case. Step 2: hold the phone in your hand, screen up. Step 3: wave it in a slow figure-8 pattern, rotating your wrist as you go, 3–4 times. Step 4: hold the phone flat again and watch the heading — it should stabilise within seconds. If it keeps jumping, move further from metal and repeat. The figure-8 is the visual language of this whole niche: compass apps show it as pink figure-8 arrows for a reason.

Finding the Qibla direction

Qibla direction is the use case that drives most compass-app installs: a traveller in a new city, or anyone praying away from home, needs to know which way to face. The classic failure is an uncalibrated phone compass — readings can be off by a large margin near fridges and washing machines, and GPS does not help, because GPS only tells you where you are, not which way you are facing. So calibrate first, away from metal, then turn on Qibla mode above: use your location or pick your city, and the page shows the Qibla bearing in degrees plus a live "turn left / turn right" guide — turn until the green marker reaches the top and the guide says you are facing it. What to use it for — and what not to

Good uses: orienting a map on a hike, checking which way a window faces before buying a plant, aligning a satellite dish or solar panel, setting up a telescope, or satisfying simple curiosity about the direction you are looking. Not for: safety-critical navigation, surveying, or anywhere a wrong heading could put you in danger — a phone compass is accurate to about 5–10° after calibration, and that is plenty for everyday orientation but not for precision work.

FAQ

Is this online compass really free?

Yes. Free, no signup, no app to install, and no limit on use.

Why is my phone compass wrong?

Almost always magnetic interference (fridge, speaker, magnet, metal case, wireless charger) or an uncalibrated sensor. Move away from metal, remove magnetic accessories, and calibrate with a figure-8 wave.

How do I calibrate my phone compass?

Wave the phone in a figure-8 pattern, rotating your wrist, 3–4 times, away from metal objects. The magnetometer recalibrates against local interference and the reading should stabilise within seconds. See the figure-8 walkthrough on this page.

Why doesn't the compass work on my laptop or desktop?

Laptops and desktops do not have a magnetometer — the sensor this tool reads. No website can fix missing hardware. This page tells you that instead of showing a fake reading, and offers a demo mode so you can still try the dial. Open it on your phone for a live reading.

Does an online compass use GPS or my location?

No, not for the heading — it only reads the magnetometer, the chip that senses magnetic fields. Location is used only if you turn on Qibla mode, and only in your browser to calculate the bearing; nothing is sent to a server.

What is the difference between magnetic north and true north?

A phone compass points to magnetic north, which drifts and sits a few degrees off the geographic North Pole; the offset (magnetic declination) depends on where you are. For everyday orientation it barely matters; for map navigation, allow for it.

How accurate is a phone compass?

Most phones get within 5–10 degrees of the correct heading after calibration — plenty for hiking, checking window direction or pointing an antenna, but not surveyor-grade. This page shows an accuracy indicator when your device reports one.

Why does my compass show different directions in different rooms?

Magnetic interference varies room to room — wiring, appliances, metal furniture. The sensor is fine; the environment changes. Move away from metal and recalibrate with a figure-8.

Do I need to install an app to use a compass?

No — a browser compass reads the same sensor through the DeviceOrientation API; no app, no download.

Why does my compass need permission on iPhone?

iOS requires an explicit motion and orientation permission. Tap Start compass, then Allow when Safari asks. If you denied it, re-enable it in Settings → Safari → Motion & Orientation Access.

Can I use an online compass without internet?

Yes, once the page has loaded — the compass runs entirely on your phone's hardware and sensor data stays local; you could switch to airplane mode and it would keep working.

Why does my compass only show east and never west?

A stuck or badly uncalibrated magnetometer — calibrate with the figure-8, move away from metal, and if it persists the sensor may be damaged. The cause of any specific stuck-sensor report is not verified.

How do I find Qibla direction with my phone?

Use a compass with a Qibla mode (this page has one), or look up your city's Qibla bearing in degrees and rotate until the compass shows it. Calibrate first — an uncalibrated phone compass is the main reason Qibla apps read wrong. Note the Qibla bearing is from true north, while the phone reads magnetic north, so allow a few degrees.

What is a compass bearing?

Your heading in degrees clockwise from north: N = 0°, E = 90°, S = 180°, W = 270°.

Is my compass data sent to a server?

On this page, no — sensor data stays on your device and nothing is uploaded. Location is used only inside your browser for the Qibla calculation, and only when you ask for it. Check any compass page's privacy statement before trusting it.
