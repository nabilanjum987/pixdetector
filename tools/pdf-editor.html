The problem: editing a PDF means paying Adobe or uploading your life

Someone sends you a form, a contract or a letter as a PDF, and all you need is to add a few words, sign it, and fix one sideways page. The usual route is miserable: the big-name editors want an account and a subscription, the "free" ones upload your document to a server and stamp a watermark on the download, and the desktop apps cost money for a two-minute job. People ask the same questions again and again on forums and help threads: is there a PDF editor that is actually free, can I sign a PDF without printing it, and will my document stay private? What they want is a small, honest editor — add text, sign, rotate, reorder — with no account and no upload.

How do I edit a PDF for free?

Upload your PDF here, click on any page to add text or place your drawn or typed signature, rotate or reorder pages as needed, then download the edited PDF — free, with no signup, no watermark and no upload, because everything runs in your browser. A PDF editor is a tool that adds new content on top of a PDF — text boxes, a signature image, a new page order — and saves the result as a new PDF file.

This toolkit is built on pdf-lib, a library that rebuilds your PDF in your own browser. Your original file is never sent anywhere, and the downloaded file is a real PDF that opens in any reader. It cannot reflow or edit the text that is already in the document — a PDF stores finished pages, not an editable document — so if you need to change existing wording, convert the file to Word first and make a new PDF from there.

How to edit a PDF in 4 steps

Step 1: upload your PDF (or drag it onto the drop zone). Step 2: click a page thumbnail to select it, then click on the preview to place text or your signature — draw your signature on the pad, or type your name for a script-style signature. Step 3: rotate a sideways page, move pages up or down, or delete a page you do not need. Step 4: press Download edited PDF and save the new file.

What this editor can and cannot do

It can add text boxes in a size and colour you choose, place a drawn or typed signature anywhere on a page, rotate pages by 90 degrees, reorder pages, and delete pages. It cannot change, delete or reflow the text that is already printed in the PDF — that text is baked into the page, and no browser editor can rewrite it as if it were a Word document. White-out is not included either: covering old text with a white box only hides it visually, it does not remove it from the file. For forms, adding text over the blank lines works well; for changing existing wording, use the PDF to Word converter instead.

Is my PDF private here?

Yes. The file is read with pdf.js and rebuilt with pdf-lib entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, stored or shared, and there is no account that could keep a copy. That is the right setup for contracts, forms and personal documents. One honest note: a signature drawn here is an image on the page, not a certified digital signature — it looks and prints like a signature, but it does not carry the cryptographic proof a formal e-signature service provides.

FAQ

Is this PDF editor really free?

Yes. Add text, sign, rotate, reorder and delete pages with no signup, no payment, no watermark and no page limit on this page. Everything runs in your browser.

Can I change the text that is already in my PDF?

No. A PDF stores finished pages, not an editable document, so this editor cannot reflow or rewrite existing text. It adds new text, signatures and page changes on top. To change existing wording, convert the PDF to Word first.

How do I sign a PDF here?

Draw your signature on the pad, or type your name for a script-style signature, press “Use this signature”, then click on the page where it should go. Download the PDF and the signature is part of the file.

Can I rotate or reorder pages?

Yes. Select a page and rotate it left or right in 90-degree steps, move it up or down, or delete it. The download keeps your new page order.

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. The PDF is read and rebuilt entirely in your browser using pdf.js and pdf-lib. Nothing is uploaded, stored, or shared.

Is the signature legally valid?

The signature is a visible image on the page, like a scanned handwritten signature. It is not a certified digital signature with cryptographic proof, so for contracts that need formal e-signing, use a dedicated e-signature service.
