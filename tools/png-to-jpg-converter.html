The problem: the PNG is too big, and the logo turned black

PNG-to-JPG has one famous failure, and it is the first thing people search about: convert a logo with a transparent background and the see-through area comes out solid black — or white with a grey halo. It is not a bug in the image. JPG has no alpha channel, so a converter must decide what colour each transparent pixel becomes, and a lazy converter just drops the alpha and lets the hidden black pixel values show through. The second problem is the upload cap: a 12 MB PNG screenshot will not fit a 25 MB Gmail attachment, a 20 MB Discord limit, or a form that wants under 2 MB — and people discover that only after the upload fails. The third is the opposite surprise: a flat-colour screenshot that was 20 KB as PNG comes out at 78 KB as JPG, because JPG compression is built for photos, not flat graphics. People want a free converter that tells them what will happen to transparency before it happens, and shows the new size before they download.

A PNG to JPG converter is a tool that re-encodes a lossless PNG image as a compressed JPG file. PNG stores every pixel exactly and supports transparency, which makes it large for photographs; JPG uses lossy compression that discards fine detail the eye barely notices, typically shrinking a photo by 60–90%. The trade is permanent: once JPG compression discards data, converting back to PNG makes a bigger file but never restores the lost detail.

When PNG to JPG is the wrong move

Do not convert a logo, icon or screenshot with text if you can avoid it: JPG's compression creates ringing artefacts around hard edges, so text goes blurry while the file barely shrinks. Do not convert anything that needs transparency — a logo that must sit on different backgrounds stays a PNG, or becomes a WebP (see our PNG to WebP converter). And never convert your only copy: keep the original PNG, because JPG loss cannot be undone. Convert for the upload, the email, or the form — and keep the master safe.

FAQ

Is this PNG to JPG converter really free?

Yes. Convert as many PNGs as you like, free, with no signup, no watermark and no upload — everything runs in your browser.

What happens to PNG transparency when converting to JPG?

JPG does not support transparency, so transparent areas are filled with the background colour you choose — white by default. Pick the colour before converting: a converter that just drops the alpha channel fills them black instead.

How much smaller will my JPG be?

For photographs, expect 50–90% smaller — a 12 MB PNG photo typically becomes 1–2 MB at quality 85. For screenshots, logos and flat graphics the saving is much smaller (20–30%), and the file can even grow.

Why does text look blurry after converting a PNG screenshot to JPG?

JPG compression creates ringing artefacts around high-contrast hard edges like text. That is a property of JPEG compression, not a bug. Screenshots with text, UI elements and logos should stay PNG.

Can I convert JPG back to PNG and recover quality?

No. Once JPG compression discards image data, it cannot be recovered. Converting back to PNG only makes a bigger file. Always keep your original PNG.

Is my PNG uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion uses the canvas element and runs entirely in your browser. No files are transmitted — you can check your browser's network activity during conversion.
