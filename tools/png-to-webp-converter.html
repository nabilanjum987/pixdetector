The problem: your PNGs are slowing your website down

The PNG-to-WebP story starts in PageSpeed Insights: "Serve images in next-gen formats" next to a hero image that is a 2 MB PNG. The third is quality anxiety: which quality setting keeps the image looking the same, and will the transparency survive? Will WebP always make my site faster?

Not always, and that is worth saying plainly. WebP helps most when your images are large photographs or graphics — swapping those can cut a page's image weight by half. It helps least on images that are already small, or on flat graphics that PNG already compresses well. The biggest wins come from combining WebP with resizing the image to its display size: a 4000-pixel PNG resized to 800 pixels and converted to WebP is a different file entirely. Convert one large image, compare the sizes, and check it still looks right before converting the rest.

FAQ

What is the difference between PNG, JPG and WebP?

PNG is lossless with transparency but large; JPG is lossy, no transparency, much smaller — best for photos; WebP is Google’s modern format, lossy or lossless, keeps transparency, and is smaller than both — best for the web.

Why convert PNG to WebP?

Same photo, smaller file than JPG, AND transparency is kept — WebP is the web format; Google measured lossy WebP 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEG at equal quality.

Why convert PNG to JPG?

To shrink the file — a PNG photo typically becomes 5–10x smaller as JPG with little visible loss — so it fits email limits (Gmail 25MB, Outlook 20MB), forms and chats. JPG is also the format every form, email client and old app accepts, so convert to JPG here when a site rejects WebP.

What happens to the transparent background when I convert PNG to JPG?

It must become a solid colour — white on good tools, black on broken ones — because JPG cannot store transparency. Pick the background colour before converting; if you need transparency, use WebP or stay with PNG. This page shows a live preview of the colour before you convert.

Will converting to WebP reduce image quality?

Lossy WebP at quality 90% is invisible on most photos, at 75% slightly softer. Lossless WebP loses nothing, and this tool’s 100% setting is the browser’s highest quality — but true lossless WebP is not available from a canvas converter, so keep your PNG as the master copy. Screenshots and text stay sharpest as PNG.

How do I choose the right quality setting?

90% for reports and presentations (visually identical), 75–85% for web and quick sharing, and use target-size mode when a form demands an exact KB limit.

What quality should I use for printing?

For print, don’t use WebP — use JPG at 95–100% and set DPI to 300 if the tool offers it. WebP is a screen format. For screen use, WebP at 80–90% is enough.

Does converting remove metadata (EXIF)?

Browser (canvas) conversion strips camera data and location automatically — a privacy bonus. Server tools vary; MConverter states it preserves as much metadata as possible.

Why is my converted WebP bigger than the original PNG?

A small, simple PNG (logo, flat graphic) can be smaller than a WebP of the same image, and a high-quality WebP from a tiny JPG can grow too. Check the before/after sizes; if it grew, keep the original. This page flags it on every file instead of silently delivering a larger file.

Yes — any image converter handles WebP input; choose PNG to keep transparency, JPG for maximum compatibility. Use the format switcher above: upload the WebP and pick JPG or PNG as the output.
