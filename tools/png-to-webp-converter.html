The problem: your PNGs are slowing your website down

The PNG-to-WebP story starts in PageSpeed Insights: "Serve images in next-gen formats" next to a hero image that is a 2 MB PNG. PNG is the right format for a logo that needs transparency, but it is a heavy format for a photo or a large graphic on a web page, and every extra kilobyte is slower loading on a phone. The second frustration is the surprise in the other direction — people convert a PNG to WebP expecting a smaller file and get a bigger one, because a small flat-colour PNG or a tiny icon can compress better as PNG than as lossy WebP. The third is quality anxiety: which quality setting keeps the image looking the same, and will the transparency survive? People want a free converter that keeps the transparent background, shows the size before and after, and says plainly when WebP is not the right call.

A PNG to WebP converter is a tool that re-encodes a PNG image in Google's WebP format. WebP is a modern web image format: Google measured lossy WebP at 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEG images at equal quality, and it supports transparency like PNG, so a logo keeps its see-through background while the file shrinks. Every current browser can display WebP, and about 97% of users' browsers support it — but older software and some apps still cannot open it, so keep the original PNG.

Use WebP for images served on a website: photos, product shots, blog graphics and logos that need transparency. Keep PNG for the master copy, for screenshots with small text, and for anything that must open in every program. Use JPG instead of WebP when the image has no transparency and must open in older software — or use our PNG to JPG converter. And never convert a lossy file back and forth: always convert from the original PNG, not from a JPG you made earlier.

Will WebP always make my site faster?

Not always, and that is worth saying plainly. WebP helps most when your images are large photographs or graphics — swapping those can cut a page's image weight by half. It helps least on images that are already small, or on flat graphics that PNG already compresses well. The biggest wins come from combining WebP with resizing the image to its display size: a 4000-pixel PNG resized to 800 pixels and converted to WebP is a different file entirely. Convert one large image, compare the sizes, and check it still looks right before converting the rest.

FAQ

Is this PNG to WebP converter really free?

Yes. Convert as many PNGs as you like, free, with no signup, no watermark and no upload — everything runs in your browser.

Does WebP keep PNG transparency?

Yes. WebP supports an alpha channel like PNG, so transparent areas stay transparent. That is the main reason to choose WebP over JPG for logos and graphics.

How much smaller will my WebP be?

Typically 25–80% smaller than the PNG for photos and graphics — Google measured lossy WebP at 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEG at equal quality. Small flat-colour PNGs and tiny icons are the exception: they can come out bigger as WebP.

Will WebP make my website faster?

It can help a lot when your images are large, especially combined with resizing to the display size. It is not a guarantee — if the image is already small, or something else is slowing the page, the gain is small. Convert one large image and compare first.

Can I convert WebP back to PNG?

Yes, but the quality lost in a lossy WebP conversion never comes back. Always keep your original PNG as the master copy.

Is my PNG uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion uses the canvas element and runs entirely in your browser. No files are transmitted.
