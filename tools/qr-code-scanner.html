The problem: a QR code your phone will not read

QR codes are everywhere — menus, tickets, parcels, payment counters, posters — and the moment you actually need one, your phone camera often refuses to read it. The code is too dark, too small, blurred, glaring under a light, or printed on a screen your phone is struggling with. Worse is the code that lives on the same device: a screenshot in your gallery, or a QR code shown on your laptop screen while your phone is in your other hand. People keep asking for a free scanner that simply works without installing yet another app, and the most common complaint about scanner apps is that they fail in bad light or on dark, low-contrast codes. This page is the plain version of that ask: no app, no account, just the camera or the image, and the answer in seconds.

How to scan a QR code on your own screen

Your camera cannot photograph your own screen — and this is the use case the upload mode exists for. Take a screenshot of the code (on a phone, that is the code in your gallery or on a web page), then upload the screenshot here, or paste it straight onto this page with Ctrl+V (Windows) or Cmd+V (Mac). The screenshot is usually the sharpest version of the code you have, so it decodes more reliably than pointing a second camera at a screen.

Camera not working? The 3-step fix

Camera permission being blocked is the most common functional failure for web scanners — the tool looks broken when the browser simply never allowed the camera. Step 1: click the camera icon in the address bar and allow camera access for this site. Step 2: reload the page and tap Start camera again. Step 3: if it still will not start, use image upload instead — it works on every device even when the camera is blocked. Also check lighting and distance: move closer, improve the light, and clean the lens.

Why a QR code will not scan

Blur is the number one cause: a shaky photo or a code printed too small loses the fine squares the reader needs. Glare is second — tilt the screen or move the light so the code is evenly lit. A code cropped tight to its edge also fails, because scanners need the white margin (the "quiet zone") around the square. If the code is damaged, try a sharper photo; if it is very dark or low-contrast, brighten the image first. And if the code is on your own screen, take a screenshot and upload it here instead of pointing a second camera at it — that is usually the sharpest version you have.

Why won't my camera scan the QR code?

Usually camera permission is blocked in the browser — check the address-bar camera icon and allow access, then reload; also check lighting and distance.

Is it safe to scan a QR code?

Scanning itself is safe — the risk is the link it opens. Always preview the URL before tapping it, and don't enter passwords on a page a QR code opened. If the preview URL looks wrong, don't tap it — the code may have been tampered with or misprinted.

Can a QR code contain a virus?

A QR code can't contain a virus by itself, but it can link to a malicious site — preview the URL first.

What can a QR code contain?

A URL, plain text, Wi-Fi credentials, contact details (vCard), email, or payment info — a good scanner shows you the type before you act.

Why won't my QR code scan?
