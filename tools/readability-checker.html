The problem: writing that is accurate but unreadable

Bloggers, students and marketers keep hitting the same wall: a page can be accurate and still lose readers, because every sentence runs on for forty words. The most-searched complaint is not about grammar — it is about the score itself: “my Flesch score is low, how do I raise it?”, “what is a good readability score?”, and “why does Word say grade 15 when my readers are ordinary adults?” A second frustration is confusion between the two numbers: Flesch Reading Ease (0–100, higher is easier) and Flesch-Kincaid grade (a US school grade) use the same inputs but cannot be converted into each other, so people compare scores from different formulas and draw the wrong conclusion. A third is short text: with fewer than 100 words, one long sentence swings the score wildly, and several popular checkers never warn you. What people want is simple — paste text, see the number, see what it means in plain words, and know exactly which two levers move it: shorter sentences and shorter words.

A readability checker is a tool that estimates how hard a text is to read using a formula. This one uses the two classic formulas: Flesch Reading Ease, which combines average sentence length and average syllables per word into a 0–100 score, and Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level, which turns the same two measures into a US school grade. Both were designed for English, and both are estimates of reading difficulty — not of comprehension, accuracy, or quality.

How to check readability in 3 steps

Step 1: paste at least 100 words of your text into the box — a full paragraph or page, not one sentence. Step 2: read the Flesch Reading Ease score and the grade level, plus the word, sentence and syllable counts. Step 3: edit the longest sentences and the longest words, then check again and compare the before and after scores.

How to improve your readability score

Only two things move the formulas: average sentence length and average syllables per word. Split any sentence over 20–25 words into two — cutting average sentence length from 22 words to 16 lifts Flesch Reading Ease by about 9 points. Swap multi-syllable words for short ones: use instead of utilize, help instead of facilitate, show instead of demonstrate, about instead of approximately. Use active voice — “the study found results” is shorter than “results were found by the study” — and break paragraphs longer than four sentences into two. Then re-check: compare the new score with the baseline rather than guessing. Remember the limit: a formula cannot tell you whether your argument is clear, your facts are right, or your tone fits your reader — a grade-6 text with a weak argument still loses readers.

FAQ

Is this readability checker free?

Yes. Paste as much text as you like, free, with no signup and no account. Everything runs in your browser, so your text is never uploaded or stored.

What is a good Flesch Reading Ease score?

For general web content, 60–70 is standard — easy for most adults to read. Above 70 is very easy and ideal for a broad audience; below 50 is difficult and suits academic or technical content. Most blogs and marketing pages aim for 60–70.

What is the difference between Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch-Kincaid grade?

Both use the same inputs — sentence length and syllables per word — but give different outputs. Flesch Reading Ease is a 0–100 score where higher means easier. Flesch-Kincaid gives a US school grade: 8.0 means about an 8th-grade reading level. They cannot be converted into each other, so compare scores from the same formula.

How many words do I need for a reliable score?

At least 100 words. With very short text, one sentence or one long word changes the result a lot, and the SMOG formula was designed for 30 sentences. This tool warns you when your text is under 100 words.

Does a better readability score help my Google ranking?

Google does not use readability scores as a direct ranking factor. But easier-to-read content keeps readers on the page longer and gets shared more, which helps indirectly. The score is a writing aid, not an SEO trick.

Does the checker work for non-English text?

No — honestly, it does not. The Flesch formulas and the syllable rules are calibrated for English. Scores for non-English or heavily mixed-language text can be misleading, and this tool does not switch to language-specific formulas.

Related questions

How can I improve my Flesch score quickly? Split sentences over 20 words and swap long words for short ones — those two changes move the score most.

What grade level should I write at? Grade 6–8 for a general audience; grade 7–8 is the usual target for web content and plain-language documents.

Can a readability score be too good? A very easy score is fine for general readers, but for a specialist audience it can feel patronising — match the grade to your readers, not to the lowest possible number.

Does bullet-point formatting improve my score? It improves scannability for readers, but it does not automatically improve a formula score, which only counts sentences and syllables.
