The problem: your favourite song is three minutes long, and your phone wants 30 seconds

Everyone has tried this: you want the chorus of your favourite song as your ringtone, and the phone makes it hard. On iPhone the trouble starts before you even trim — the phone only accepts .m4r files, iTunes hides the "Create AAC Version" option when the import setting is wrong, and a file renamed from .mp3 to .m4r by hand simply never appears in the ringtone list. Worse, clips over 30 seconds are capped in Settings and a longer selection errors or is shortened, so people do the whole dance and hear nothing when a call comes in. On Android the format is easy, but most "free ringtone maker" apps are ad-heavy, push subscriptions, or want your contacts. And the ringtone sites that do work in a browser often charge, watermark, or upload your song to a server. What people actually want is plain: cut the best part of a song they own, keep it short, and get a file their phone accepts — without an app install, a subscription, or a surprise.

Getting it onto your phone — step by step

Android

Step 1: download the trimmed MP3 here. Step 2: save it to the Ringtones folder (your file manager usually offers “Set as ringtone” on a long-press). Step 3: go to Settings → Sound → Phone ringtone → My Sounds and pick it. For an alarm or notification, save it to the Alarms or Notifications folder instead and pick it under Alarm sound or Notification sound. You can also assign a tone to one contact from the contact's page in your phone app.

iPhone — GarageBand (no computer)

Step 1: download the trimmed clip here and save it into the Files app on your iPhone. Step 2: open GarageBand (free from the App Store) and start a new Audio Recorder song. Step 3: tap the loop icon, import the clip from Files, and drag it onto the timeline. Step 4: tap the down-arrow → My Songs, long-press the song, tap Share → Ringtone, name it, and tap Export. Step 5: pick it in Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone. GarageBand makes the real .m4r for you — this is the only honest way to get a custom ringtone onto an iPhone without a computer. Any site claiming to download a ready .m4r straight from the browser is not telling the truth.

iPhone — Finder on a Mac (no GarageBand)

Step 1: download the trimmed MP3 here. Step 2: in the Music app, import the MP3 and use File → Convert → Create AAC Version to make an .m4a file. Step 3: in Finder, rename the .m4a file's extension to .m4r (Finder will ask you to confirm — say yes). Step 4: connect the iPhone, open it in Finder, and drag the .m4r file onto the phone, then sync. Step 5: pick it in Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone.

Why won't my ringtone show up?

Check the clip length first — 30 seconds or less; a longer clip errors or is shortened. Then look in the right place: Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone, not in Music or Files — Files does not move audio into the ringtone picker. If the file is an MP3 renamed to .m4r by hand, it will never appear: it must be a real AAC .m4r from GarageBand or the Finder step. And if the song is a DRM-protected Apple Music track, it stays dimmed in GarageBand and cannot be used — use a file you own.

Picking the right 30 seconds

The chorus is almost always the right choice — it is the part everyone recognises in two seconds. Start the clip a beat before the chorus hits so the ringtone does not begin mid-word, and let the fade-out soften the ending instead of cutting the music dead. A 15-second clip works fine too, and it is safer if your phone only rings briefly before going to voicemail.
