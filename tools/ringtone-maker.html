The problem: your favourite song is three minutes long, and your phone wants 30 seconds

Everyone has tried this: you want the chorus of your favourite song as your ringtone, and the phone makes it hard. On iPhone the trouble starts before you even trim — the phone only accepts .m4r files, iTunes hides the "Create AAC Version" option when the import setting is wrong, and a file renamed from .mp3 to .m4r by hand simply never appears in the ringtone list. Worse, clips over 40 seconds are silently ignored, so people do the whole dance and hear nothing when a call comes in. On Android the format is easy, but most "free ringtone maker" apps are ad-heavy, push subscriptions, or want your contacts. And the ringtone sites that do work in a browser often charge, watermark, or upload your song to a server. What people actually want is plain: cut the best part of a song they own, keep it short, and get a file their phone accepts — without an app install, a subscription, or a surprise.

A ringtone is a short audio clip, usually 15 to 30 seconds, that a phone plays when a call comes in. Android accepts MP3 files directly, while iPhone only accepts AAC audio in a file ending .m4r, capped at 40 seconds. Trimming means cutting the song down to that short section; the fades keep the clip from starting or ending with an abrupt cut.

How to make a ringtone in 3 steps

Step 1: upload your song — MP3, WAV, M4A or OGG, a file you own; it never leaves your device. Step 2: pick the best 30 seconds with the sliders or the First / Middle / Last 30s buttons, keep it at 30 seconds or under, and tick fade in and fade out. Step 3: play the selection to check it, then download the MP3 or WAV ringtone and set it on your phone.

Getting it onto an iPhone, step by step

Download the trimmed clip here, then save it into the Files app on your iPhone. Open GarageBand (free from the App Store), start a new Audio Recorder song, tap the loop icon, import the clip from Files, then tap Share and choose Ringtone. GarageBand converts the clip to a real .m4r and adds it to Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone. This one extra step is the only honest way to get a custom ringtone onto an iPhone without a computer — any site claiming to download a ready .m4r straight from the browser is not telling the truth.

Picking the right 30 seconds

The chorus is almost always the right choice — it is the part everyone recognises in two seconds. Start the clip a beat before the chorus hits so the ringtone does not begin mid-word, and let the fade-out soften the ending instead of cutting the music dead. A 15-second clip works fine too, and it is safer if your phone only rings briefly before going to voicemail.

FAQ

Is this ringtone maker really free?

Yes. Make as many ringtones as you like, free, with no signup, no upload and no watermark. Everything runs in your browser and the ringtone downloads straight to your device.

Can I make an iPhone ringtone here?

Halfway. This page trims your song and exports an MP3 or WAV, but iPhones only accept ringtones as .m4r files, and no browser can create a .m4r. Export the clip here, then use free GarageBand on your iPhone to turn it into a ringtone — the steps are in the guide below.

How long should a ringtone be?

30 seconds or less. iPhones ignore ringtones longer than 40 seconds, and most people answer or miss the call within 30 seconds anyway. Pick the chorus or the catchiest 15 to 30 seconds of the song.

Why won't my ringtone show up on my iPhone?

Almost always one of three things: the clip is longer than 40 seconds, the file is an MP3 renamed to .m4r instead of a real .m4r, or the transfer did not complete. Keep the clip under 30 seconds, make a real .m4r through GarageBand, and check the file name ends in exactly .m4r — not .m4r.m4a.

Can I use a song from YouTube or Spotify as a ringtone?

Only if you have the audio file itself. Streaming songs and DRM-protected downloads cannot be turned into ringtones — the file is locked. Use an MP3 or WAV you own and can copy, then trim it here.

Is my audio uploaded anywhere?

No. Your song never leaves your device. It is trimmed and exported entirely in your browser, and nothing is stored after you close the page.
