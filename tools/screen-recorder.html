Screen, window or tab — and the audio question

A screen recorder captures what is on your display as a video file. The choice you make in the browser's picker matters. A whole screen captures everything, including notifications and anything else that pops up — fine for a full demo, risky for a tutorial where a private message could appear. A window captures one app only, which is the safest choice for most demos: the viewer sees the app and nothing else. A tab captures one browser tab, and it is the only choice that reliably captures system audio — the sound from a video playing in that tab, for example. If you need the sound of what is playing, share a tab and tick the browser's own “Share audio” box in its picker.

Microphone audio is separate from system audio, and the two fail in different ways. The microphone is straightforward: tick “Include microphone”, allow access, and your voice is recorded alongside the screen. System audio is the flaky one — it often fails outright, or only works on a tab share, depending on the browser and the operating system. If your recording comes back without system sound, that is the usual cause, not a bug in the page: share a tab, tick “Share audio”, and try again. And if you need webcam and screen together, this tool cannot do it — it records screen plus microphone only. For a picture-in-picture webcam, use OBS or your operating system's camera app.

Two honest limits before you record. First, the output is WebM, not MP4 — WebM will not open in iMovie, Photos or some versions of Premiere, so plan a second transcode with HandBrake or FFmpeg if your editor needs MP4. Second, this is for quick captures and demos, not production: there are no scenes, no live streaming and no multi-source mixing, and long recordings are bounded by your browser's memory. For a full production with scenes and a webcam, OBS is the honest choice — and it is free. For a quick “show me how this works” capture, this page is faster: press record, show it, stop, download.

Built-in recorders already on your computer

Windows — Game Bar (Win + Alt + R): built in since 2015, starts immediately in Full HD (1920×1080) with audio, no time limit, no watermark; the file lands in Videos, then Captures. The catch: it records only one app window, not the desktop or File Explorer. Windows — Snipping Tool video (Win + Shift + S): opens the Snipping Tool in video mode for a quick capture. The app blocks audio — FaceTime, Netflix and WhatsApp calls mute it. FAQ

How do I record my screen online for free?

Open a browser-based recorder, click Start, pick a tab, window or your full screen, allow the permission, record, then stop and download the WebM — no install or signup on this page.

Is there a free screen recorder with no watermark?

Yes — this page and browser tools like Jupitrr and Waridi Tech export watermark-free files; Screenlet’s free plan adds a small watermark (removed by its $29 one-time Lifetime plan); YoRecord’s free exports carry a small “yorecord.com” watermark. Check the watermark line before you record.

How do I record my screen with sound?

In Chrome, tick “Share tab audio” (or system audio when sharing the whole screen) in the browser’s share window — if you skip that checkbox, the computer’s sound is not included. Add your microphone separately for narration.

Can I record my screen on my iPhone or Android in the browser?

No — phones do not allow screen capture in the browser; use the phone’s built-in recorder (iPhone: Control Center screen recording; Android: built-in recorder in quick settings).

Why does my screen recording have no sound?

Usually one of three things: the microphone was off in the recorder, “Share tab audio” or system audio wasn’t ticked, or the app blocks audio (FaceTime, Netflix, WhatsApp calls mute it). Check the toggles first, then the app.

How long can I record for free?

It varies by tool: Loom’s free plan stops at 5 minutes, YoRecord caps free recordings at 10 minutes (20 on Pro), while Waridi Tech and Jupitrr claim no time limit. This page imposes no time limit, but long recordings are bounded by your browser’s memory.

Do I need to download anything?

No for browser recorders — they run in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. Screenlet needs a Chrome extension; OBS and ShareX are desktop downloads.

What format will my recording be?

WebM on this page — the browser-native format; most browser tools offer MP4 (Jupitrr, Waridi, YoRecord), and Drefly also exports MOV, MKV, AVI and GIF.

Can I record my screen and webcam at the same time?

Yes on some tools — Capscreen, Screenlet, Zight and Drefly all offer screen plus camera together, with the webcam as a picture-in-picture overlay. This page records screen plus microphone only.

Is an online screen recorder private?

Browser-based ones record locally — the file stays on your device and is never uploaded on this page (Jupitrr, Waridi and Drefly work the same way); tools that offer share links or cloud sync (Zight, ScreenApp, Drefly’s Drive sync) upload only when you choose to share.

Why is my recording black when I record Netflix or a video call?

Copy-protected content (Netflix, Apple Music) and some calls (FaceTime, WhatsApp) block capture or mute audio for legal and privacy reasons — no recorder can bypass that.

Can I record just one window instead of my whole screen?

Yes — the browser’s capture picker offers tab, window, or entire screen; Game Bar on Windows records one app window only.

Does it work on Mac and Safari?

Yes — browser recorders run on macOS in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari; macOS built-in Cmd + Shift + 5 records the screen but not system sound without an extra driver. This page works best in Chrome, Edge or Firefox; Safari support is limited.

Can I pause and resume a recording?

Some tools can (ScreenApp); many simple browser tools cannot — this page can: press Pause, then Resume.

How do I record a Zoom or Teams meeting?

Record the meeting window or tab with microphone plus system or tab audio ticked; on a phone use the built-in recorder; note that some meeting apps notify participants or block capture — check the app’s own rules first.
