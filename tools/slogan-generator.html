A slogan generator is a tool that turns a brand name and a short description into tagline ideas. A slogan (also called a tagline) is a short, memorable phrase — usually 3 to 7 words — that sums up what a brand promises. The best-known slogans work because they are short, specific and easy to say out loud: Nike's "Just Do It" has run unchanged since 1988, which is exactly why people remember it.

How to make a slogan in 3 steps

Step 1: type your brand name and what you do — be specific, because "hand-roasted coffee" produces far better slogans than "a company". Step 2: pick a tone that matches your brand, then generate and re-generate until a few lines stand out. Step 3: read your shortlist aloud, test it on a few people who match your audience, and check it is not already trademarked before you use it.

What makes a slogan memorable?

The slogans people remember share four traits. Short: 3 to 7 words — if you cannot say it in one breath, it is too long. Specific: it names the one thing that makes you different, not a general claim like "quality" or "excellence". Easy to say: it still sounds right when read aloud, and it often uses rhythm, rhyme or alliteration. Yours alone: your competitor could not plausibly use the same line. If a generated slogan fails any of these, treat it as a rough draft and rewrite it — the tool gives you the raw material, not the finished line.

What to check before you use a slogan

Two checks matter before a slogan goes on a website, a sign or packaging. First, search it: type the exact phrase into a search engine and check whether another business already uses it in your market — a slogan that is already associated with someone else will confuse customers. Second, check trademarks: in the US, search the USPTO trademark database; in the UK, the IPO register. Short descriptive phrases are hard to protect, but a slogan that copies a registered mark can force an expensive rebrand. This generator does not check trademarks or search the web, so neither check happens here — do them yourself before you commit.

Should a slogan be liked or remembered?

They are not the same outcome. A 2023 Journal of Consumer Research paper that analysed 800+ slogans found shorter lines and familiar words were liked more, while longer lines, concrete or less-common words, and included brand names were more likely to be remembered. And there is a trap in the middle: if people remember the slogan but credit the wrong company, the company has bought advertising for its competitors. Decide first whether your line must be remembered or liked — then pick the tone and the length to match.

FAQ

How do I create a slogan for free?

Type your brand name and what you do into a free slogan generator, pick a tone, and copy the lines you like — then rewrite the best one in your own words, because template output is a first draft, not a finished brand line.

What makes a good slogan?

Short, easy to say and repeat, built on one true idea about your brand, and distinctive enough that it could not belong to a competitor. Under eight words is the usual advice — but a 2023 Journal of Consumer Research finding is that memorability and likability trade off: longer lines with concrete or less-common words and the brand name are remembered more, while shorter lines with familiar words are liked more. Decide first whether the line must be remembered or liked.

Can I use a generated slogan commercially?

Short taglines are generally not protected by copyright, but check it is not already trademarked in your market before building a brand around it.

Why do most slogan generators give generic results?

Because most generators combine a small set of templates with generic words like “quality” and “excellence” — phrases any business could use. The fix is to be specific about what you do: “hand-roasted coffee” gives far better slogans than “a company”, and a tone choice steers the style further.

Why did I get the same slogans twice?

Some slogan tools use fixed templates with no randomness, so pressing Generate again returns the same lines. This tool shuffles its templates and word banks on every click, so re-generating gives fresh combinations — but with a small template set, repeats still appear. If the same lines keep coming back, change your topic word or your tone.

How long should a slogan be?

Aim for 3 to 7 words. Short slogans are easier to remember and read cleanly under a logo or in an ad. If you cannot say it in one breath, it is too long.

Can AI write better slogans than a template tool?

Sometimes. A template tool like this one is honest about what it does — it combines fixed sentence templates with word banks. An AI chatbot can write lines tailored to your brand, but its output is often generic and theory-heavy too: one creator’s public critique of ChatGPT for business tasks was exactly that it gives vague best-practice advice that sounds right and says nothing. The practical route is both: generate a batch here, then rewrite the best line in your own words.

What is the difference between a slogan and a tagline?

Nothing important — the two words mean the same thing in practice: a short, memorable phrase that sums up a brand. Some people use “tagline” for the line under a logo and “slogan” for a campaign line, but they are interchangeable.

Should my slogan include my brand name?

Usually not. A slogan that stands on its own reads stronger and feels less promotional. Let the name sit next to the slogan on the logo instead of inside it.

How do I pick the best slogan from the list?

Read each one aloud, picture it next to your logo, and keep the ones that are easy to say and instantly clear. Test the shortlist on a few people who match your audience.

What if none of the slogans feel right?

That is normal — generated slogans are first drafts. Take the closest one and rewrite it in your own words, keeping the angle but changing the wording until it sounds like your brand.

How often should I change my slogan?

Rarely. Consistency builds recognition — Nike has used “Just Do It” since 1988. Changing a slogan every year means starting from zero each time.
