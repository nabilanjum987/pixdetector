The problem: picking fairly when everyone is watching

Teachers calling on students, giveaway hosts drawing a winner, friends settling where to eat — the hard part is rarely the choice itself. It is making the choice in a way the whole group accepts. Pick by hand and someone suspects favouritism. Use a hat and the draw happens out of sight. The complaints people raise about existing spinner wheels are consistent: slow pages crowded with ads, a sign-up wall before the first spin, and wheels that cap entries or hide the result behind a pro plan. What people actually want is simple — paste a list, spin, and watch the result land where everyone can see it.

The classroom version of this problem is the sharpest one. A teacher asks a question and the same three hands shoot up, while seventeen other students go quiet — not because they do not know the answer, but because they know they will never be asked. A wheel the whole class can watch fixes that: when the selection happens in front of everyone, the “why him again?” argument loses its fuel. The wheel picked him — not you. The same logic holds for a giveaway: a winner announced with no visible draw is just the host's word.

Is the spin really fair?

Yes. Every slice is the same size, and the winning slice is picked with crypto.getRandomValues() plus rejection sampling — the same secure random source browsers use for encryption — with plain Math.random only as a fallback. Shuffles use the Fisher–Yates algorithm, because the familiar sort(() => Math.random() - 0.5) shortcut is biased, not uniform. Spins are independent: a name that just won is exactly as likely to win again on the next spin, unless you remove it. The wheel has no memory, no favourites and no way to weight one entry over another. Your entries never leave your device; nothing is uploaded to a server — the list is saved in this browser only.

FAQ

How do I spin a wheel online for free?

Paste your names or options, one per line, press SPIN THE WHEEL, and the wheel lands on one at random — free, with no account and nothing to download. Every entry gets an equal slice, so every entry has the same chance.

Is a spin-the-wheel picker really random and fair?

Yes, when it uses the browser's crypto random source (crypto.getRandomValues()) with rejection sampling — every entry then has exactly the same chance. A wheel built on a naive sort(() => Math.random() - 0.5) shuffle is genuinely biased, which is why this page shuffles with Fisher–Yates instead.

How do I pick a random student fairly in class?

Paste the class list, mark absent students with the Here checkbox so they are never picked, leave fair (no-repeat) mode on, and spin where the whole class can see it. Everyone gets one turn before anyone gets a second.

Can the same person win twice in a row?

With pure randomness, yes — repeats are normal. If that is a problem, leave “Remove winner after each spin” on, so each name can only be drawn once per round.

What is fair (no-repeat) mode, and why is it on by default?

Fair mode takes each winner off the wheel until the whole list has had a turn, then the round resets. It is on by default because pure random picks can call on the same student twice while skipping a quarter of the class — and to a class keeping score, that feels like favouritism, not randomness.

How many entries can the wheel hold?

Up to 500 entries. If you paste more, only the first 500 are used, and the page says so. With very long lists the slice labels get small, so the entry list below the box is the place to check your names.

How do I mark a student absent?

Use the Here checkbox next to each name in the entry list. Unchecked names stay on your list but are never picked, so an absent student cannot stall the lesson and the fair round does not skew.

What happens when everyone has had a turn?

The page shows “Round complete — every entry has had a turn” and offers a reset. Press “Reset round” to put all names back on the wheel and start again.

Why shouldn't I re-spin when I don't like the result?

Because re-spinning destroys the trust the wheel exists to create. The moment you spin again because the “wrong” person was selected, everyone watching concludes the draw is only random until you disapprove of it.

Can I use this wheel for a giveaway or raffle?

Yes — paste the entrants, spin live on screen, leave remove-winner on so nobody wins twice, and record your screen while you spin. The recording is free proof that the draw happened once, in the open.

Is Math.random() good enough for a draw?

For a casual game, yes. For a draw anyone might challenge, no — Math.random() is pseudo-random, and a naive shuffle built on it is biased. This page draws winners with crypto.getRandomValues() and rejection sampling, and shuffles with Fisher–Yates.

What is wrong with sort(() => Math.random() - 0.5)?

It looks like a shuffle but it is not uniform — some orders come up far more often than others. The correct shuffle is Fisher–Yates, which this page uses for its shuffle button.

Are my entries uploaded anywhere?

No. Your entries stay in your browser on your own device and are saved in this browser's local storage only, so the list is still there when you come back. Nothing is sent to a server. Clearing your browsing data wipes the saved list.

Can I import a list from a spreadsheet?

Yes — use the CSV import button, or paste straight from a spreadsheet: one entry per line, or comma-separated. The wheel takes up to 500 entries, and duplicate labels count as separate chances.

Does the wheel work on a projector?

Yes — press “Projector mode” for fullscreen with a huge winner display, confetti and a sound toggle, plus a spin-length choice from 2 to 12 seconds. The whole room can watch the pick happen.
