The problem: picking fairly when everyone is watching

Teachers calling on students, giveaway hosts drawing a winner, friends settling where to eat — the hard part is rarely the choice itself. It is making the choice in a way the whole group accepts. Pick by hand and someone suspects favouritism. Use a hat and the draw happens out of sight. The complaints people raise about existing spinner wheels are consistent: slow pages crowded with ads, a sign-up wall before the first spin, and wheels that cap entries or hide the result behind a pro plan. What people actually want is simple — paste a list, spin, and watch the result land where everyone can see it.

How do I spin a wheel to pick something at random?

Type or paste your entries one per line, press SPIN THE WHEEL, and watch the wheel slow down until the pointer lands on one entry — free, with no signup and no download, because the whole tool runs in your browser. Every entry gets an equal slice of the wheel, so every entry has exactly the same chance of winning. Turn on "Remove winner after each spin" when you need several different winners in a row, for example drawing a presentation order or a list of prize winners.

A spin the wheel tool is a visual random picker: a circle divided into equal slices, one slice per entry, with a pointer that stops on exactly one of them. The winner is chosen with your browser's secure random generator before the animation plays, so the spin is a reveal, not a trick — the wheel cannot be steered toward a favourite entry.

How to use the spin the wheel tool in 3 steps

Step 1: replace the sample entries with your own — one name, prize or choice per line, up to 60 entries. Step 2: press SPIN THE WHEEL and watch the pointer settle. Step 3: read the winner in the big result box, then spin again or turn on "Remove winner" to draw the next one without repeats.

Is the spin really fair?

Yes. Every slice is the same size, and the winning slice is picked with crypto.getRandomValues() — the same secure random source browsers use for encryption — with plain Math.random only as a fallback. Spins are independent: a name that just won is exactly as likely to win again on the next spin, unless you remove it. The wheel has no memory, no favourites and no way to weight one entry over another. Your entries never leave your device; nothing is uploaded or stored on a server.

What can you decide with a wheel?

Classroom turns and cold-calling, giveaway and raffle winners, team picks, presentation order, dinner choices, game-night decisions, chores, and any "who goes first?" question. For a raffle with many prizes, turn on "Remove winner after each spin" so nobody wins twice. For a class list, spin once per question and keep everyone paying attention — anyone could be next.

FAQ

Is this spin the wheel tool really free?

Yes. You can spin as many times as you like with no signup, no payment, no ads on the wheel and no download. Everything runs in your browser.

Is the result really random?

Yes. The winning entry is picked with your browser's secure random generator (crypto.getRandomValues), and every entry gets an equal slice of the wheel, so every entry has exactly the same chance.

Can I use my own names or choices?

Yes — that is the only way it works. Type or paste your own entries, one per line, up to 60. The tool has no built-in names.

How do I pick several different winners?

Turn on “Remove winner after each spin” before you spin. Each winner is taken off the wheel, so nobody is picked twice.

Are my entries uploaded anywhere?

No. Your entries stay in your browser on your own device. Nothing is sent to a server, stored, or shared.

Why did the same entry win twice in a row?

Because spins are independent — the wheel has no memory. A name that just won is exactly as likely to win again, unless you turn on “Remove winner after each spin”.
