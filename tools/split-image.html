The problem: one photo, nine posts, wrong order

Instagram grid puzzles look simple until you try one. People split a photo in the wrong order and the profile shows a scrambled mess, or they post the tiles one by one and a single new post knocks the whole mosaic out of line. Others cut a small photo into nine pieces and wonder why every tile looks soft — each tile is only a ninth of the original, so a low-resolution source falls apart. And the most common complaint in forums and how-to guides is the download step: tools that make you save 9, 16 or 25 tiles one click at a time, with filenames that give no clue which tile goes where. People want equal tiles, numbered in posting order, in one ZIP — without uploading a personal photo to a stranger's server.

How do I split an image into tiles for free?

Upload your photo here, pick rows and columns (3×3 for an Instagram grid, 1×3 for a carousel), and click Split image. Every tile is numbered in posting order — tile 01 is the bottom-right piece you post first — and you can download them all at once as a single ZIP, or one by one. It is free, with no signup and no upload, because the split runs in your browser. The maximum grid is 10×10, and tiles keep your image's original resolution.

An image splitter cuts one picture into equal rectangles arranged in rows and columns. A 3×3 grid makes nine tiles; a 1×3 split makes three wide strips for a carousel. The tiles are crops, not resizes: each one is a piece of the original pixels, so a tile is only as sharp as the part of the photo it came from.

What grid should I use for Instagram?

For a profile puzzle, use 3 columns × 3 rows and post the tiles in the numbered order, bottom-right first and top-left last, in one session with no other posts in between — every new post shifts the grid. For a swipeable carousel, use 1 row × 3 columns and upload left to right. Keep faces and text away from the cut lines, because the seams fall exactly where the grid says. Instagram's profile grid now shows 3:4 rectangles, so square tiles get their sides trimmed in the grid view — the full tile still shows when someone taps the post.

Will splitting make my photo blurry?

Splitting itself adds no blur: each tile is a direct crop of the original pixels. What makes tiles look soft is starting too small. A 1080 px photo cut 3×3 gives 360 px tiles, which look soft when Instagram shows them at 1080 px. Start with a photo at least three times the tile size you want. PNG tiles are lossless but bigger; JPG at 92% quality is the practical choice for posting, and WebP is smallest where it is accepted.

FAQ

Is this image splitter really free?

Yes. Split as many images as you like and download every tile as a ZIP — no signup, no watermark, no upload. Everything runs in your browser.

What grid do I use for a 3×3 Instagram puzzle?

Use 3 columns and 3 rows. Post the tiles in the numbered order the tool gives you: tile 01 first (bottom-right) and tile 09 last (top-left), all in one session, or the grid shifts.

What is the biggest grid I can split into?

10 columns by 10 rows — 100 tiles. Most people need far fewer: 3×3 for a profile grid, 1×3 for a carousel, 2×2 for four equal parts.

Will the tiles lose quality?

No. Each tile is a direct crop of the original pixels, so it keeps the quality of that part of the photo. Tiles only look soft if the source image was already small for the grid you chose.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The image is read and cut in your browser with the canvas element. Nothing is uploaded, stored or shared.

Should I download PNG, JPG or WebP tiles?

Use JPG for posting to Instagram and social apps (small files, universal). Use PNG when you need lossless quality, and WebP for the smallest files on websites that accept it.

Can I split a panorama into a carousel? Yes — use 1 row and 3 (or more) columns on a wide photo, then upload the tiles left to right as one multi-image post.
