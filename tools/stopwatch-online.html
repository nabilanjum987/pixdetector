The problem: stopwatches that freeze when you switch tabs

The most reported problem with online stopwatches is the same one that haunts online timers: start timing, switch to the thing you are actually timing, and the clock has frozen or lost time. The cause is identical — a stopwatch that counts its own ticks is at the mercy of how often the browser lets a hidden tab run, and browsers deliberately slow hidden tabs down to save battery. The second frustration is lap lists that tell you nothing: a wall of times with no split, no total, and no way to see which lap was fastest. And the third is the simplest — a stopwatch with no way to copy the laps out, so you end up retyping them by hand.

How do I use a free online stopwatch?

Press Start — or hit the Space bar — and the stopwatch counts up from zero in hundredths of a second. Press Lap (or L) at each split to record it without stopping the clock; every lap shows its split time and the running total, with your fastest lap highlighted green and your slowest red. Press Space to pause and resume exactly where you left off, and R to reset. It is free, with no signup and no ads, and the Copy laps button puts the whole list on your clipboard as plain text.

A stopwatch is a tool that measures elapsed time: it counts up from zero and records how long something takes, lap by lap. A lap is one section of what you are timing — a running lap, a swim length, a section of a talk — and the split is how long that section alone took, while the total is the time since you first pressed Start. A good online stopwatch shows both numbers for every lap, because the split is what tells you where you sped up or slowed down.

How to time laps in 3 steps

Step 1: press Start (or Space). The display counts up in hundredths. Step 2: press Lap (or L) at each split — the clock keeps running while the lap is recorded. Step 3: press Space to pause, then Copy laps to save the list, or R to reset and start over.

What people time with an online stopwatch

Runners and athletes time laps and intervals, and the fastest/slowest highlight is exactly what interval training needs. Cooks and bakers time steps in a recipe. Students time study sessions and presentations — a talk that runs 12 minutes when it should run 10 shows up clearly in the lap list. Teachers time classroom activities, and speakers use laps to mark the sections of a talk. The lap list turns one total into a record of each part, so you can see where a session took more or less time than you expected.

Why this stopwatch does not lose time in background tabs

Elapsed time is calculated from timestamps — the moment you pressed Start, and the current moment — not by counting ticks. The display stops redrawing while the tab is hidden, because the browser will not let it, but the timestamps keep their meaning: when you come back, the display shows the correct time again. The same deadline-and-timestamp method is what keeps the countdown timer and the Pomodoro timer accurate on this site.

FAQ

Is this online stopwatch really free?

Yes. Time as much as you like, free, with no signup, no ads and no download. Everything runs in your browser.

How accurate is the stopwatch?

It displays hundredths of a second and measures elapsed time from your device clock, so it stays accurate even if you switch tabs. The biggest source of error is your own finger on Start and Stop, not the clock.

Does the stopwatch keep running if I switch tabs?

Yes. Elapsed time is calculated from timestamps, not by counting ticks, so switching tabs or minimising the window does not lose time. The display redraws with the correct time as soon as you come back.

What is the difference between a lap and a split?

The split is the time for one lap on its own — from the last lap press to this one. The total is the time since you first pressed Start. This stopwatch shows both for every lap, and highlights your fastest and slowest laps.

Can I copy my lap times?

Yes. Press Copy laps to put the whole lap list on your clipboard as plain text — lap number, split and total — ready to paste into a spreadsheet or a note.

What is the difference between a stopwatch and a timer?

A stopwatch counts up from zero to measure how long something takes. A timer counts down from a set time to zero and rings when it ends. For countdowns, use the free online timer on this site.

Related questions

Can I time more than one thing at once? Not on one page. Open this stopwatch in a second browser tab and each tab keeps its own time.

Does the stopwatch work on my phone? Yes. Open this page in your phone's browser — the big display and buttons work on a phone screen, and the keyboard shortcuts are replaced by the on-screen buttons.

What happens if I reload the page? The stopwatch resets to zero. Keep the tab open for as long as you are timing something, and copy your laps before you close it.

Can I use this stopwatch for a race? For casual timing, yes. For an official result, no — use certified timing equipment. This is a browser tool, and reaction time on Start and Stop is part of the measurement.
