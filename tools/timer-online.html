The problem: online timers that run slow the moment you look away

The most common complaint about online timers is not about features — it is about trust. You set a 25-minute timer, switch to the document you are actually working on, and come back to find the timer says 19 minutes left when 25 have passed. The cause is well documented: most browser timers count ticks, and browsers throttle background tabs to save battery, so a tick-counting timer runs slow the moment you stop watching it. The second complaint is simpler: timers buried under ads, pop-ups and sign-up walls, or timers that never make a sound because the page never asked for a click first.

How do I start a free online timer?

Type hours, minutes and seconds, or tap a preset like 5, 25 or 60 minutes, then press Start. The timer counts down live and rings when it reaches zero — free, with no signup and no ads. It is measured against your device clock, so it stays accurate even when you switch tabs, and the remaining time shows in the tab title. To measure how long something takes instead, switch to Count up mode and press Start.

An online timer is a browser tool that tracks a length of time for you: it counts down from a set duration to zero, or counts up from zero to measure elapsed time, and signals when the countdown ends. The honest test of any online timer is what happens when you stop looking at it — a good one keeps true time in a background tab, because it compares the current clock against a fixed end time instead of counting its own ticks.

How to use this online timer in 3 steps

Step 1: set a time — type hours, minutes and seconds, or tap a preset. Step 2: press Start. The big display counts down; press Fullscreen for a classroom, gym or presentation display. Step 3: when it reaches zero, a beep plays. Press Stop alarm to silence it, or Reset to run the same time again. In Count up mode, press Start to begin measuring and Pause to hold the time.

Why this timer does not drift in background tabs

Pressing Start records the exact moment the timer should end against your system clock. Every reading after that is the difference between that moment and now. A hidden tab, a throttled page, or a laptop that slept and woke cannot change the answer, because no ticks are ever counted. The same deadline method is what keeps the countdown accurate, and the tab title carries the remaining time so you can pick the timer out of a crowded tab strip without clicking back to it.

FAQ

Is this online timer really free?

Yes. Use it as often as you like, free, with no signup, no ads and no download. Everything runs in your browser.

Does the timer keep running if I switch tabs?

Yes. This timer is measured against your device clock, not by counting ticks, so it stays accurate in a background tab and the remaining time shows in the tab title. Keep the tab open — closing it stops the timer.

Can the timer count up instead of down?

Yes. Switch to Count up mode and press Start — the timer counts up from zero, like a stopwatch, until you stop it. Use it to measure how long something takes.

Will I hear the alarm when the timer finishes?

Yes, a beep plays when the countdown reaches zero, as long as your device volume is on and the tab is open. Browsers only allow sound after you have clicked on the page, so start the timer with a click or tap — which you do anyway.

What is the difference between a timer and a stopwatch?

A timer counts down from a set time to zero and rings when it ends. A stopwatch counts up from zero to measure elapsed time. This page does both: countdown mode and count-up mode.

Can I use this timer for a Pomodoro session?

Yes — tap the 25 min preset for a focus session and the 5 min preset for a break. For the full cycle with automatic breaks and a session counter, use the free Pomodoro timer on this site.
