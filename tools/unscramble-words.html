The problem: letters that look like nonsense

Every word-game player knows the feeling: you are handed TODKLWH, or a daily jumble, or a Wordle rack, and the letters look like nonsense — until someone points out the word hiding in them. The complaints people post are consistent. First, “I came up with a great word and it was ruled invalid” — a word that is real in a dictionary but not in that game's list, or a British spelling in a US game, or slang the list does not allow. Second, the opposite: the solver returns obscure words like TAEL or TELA that nobody would dare play because they might be challenged. Third, blank tiles: people do not know how to tell a solver they have a blank, or the solver has no blank support at all. And fourth, the daily-puzzle rush — Wordle, Connections, Strands, Quordle — where people want the longest word fast, grouped by length, not a wall of 200 unsorted results. What they want is a solver that shows the longest playable words first, says plainly which list it uses, and lets them filter by length or exact anagrams.

A word unscrambler is a tool that takes a set of jumbled letters and lists the valid English words you can spell from them, including shorter words made from a subset of the letters. An anagram, by contrast, uses every letter exactly once — LISTEN becomes SILENT, ENLIST and TINSEL.

How to unscramble words in 3 steps

Step 1: type your letters in any order — up to 12, with “?” for each blank tile. Step 2: pick the shortest word to show and choose “All words” or “Exact anagrams only”. Step 3: read the results longest-first and tap any word to copy it.

Unscrambler vs anagram solver — what is the difference?

An unscrambler lists every word you can make from your letters, including shorter ones: LISTEN gives you silent, enlist, tinsel, listen, inlets, lines, tiles, and dozens of 3- and 4-letter words. An anagram solver only keeps words that use every letter exactly once. Use the unscrambler when any valid word counts — Scrabble racks, Words With Friends, word jumbles — and the exact-anagram mode when the puzzle uses every letter, like a newspaper jumble.

Mostly, but not guaranteed. Scores, BINGOs and blanks

This tool shows no point scores — results are sorted longest first. That is a useful starting point, but the longest word is not always the best play: a big word can open a Triple Word Score for your opponent, and Scrabble and Words With Friends value several letters differently, so a score shown for one game is wrong for the other. A BINGO is using all seven tiles in one play, which earns a 50-point bonus on top of the word score. This tool does not flag BINGOs, but its "Exact anagrams only" mode lists the words that use every letter you typed — those are your BINGO candidates. For blanks, type ? for each one, up to two; a blank stands for any letter, and in real Scrabble a blank scores zero points.

Filters: what you can narrow, and what you can't

You can set the shortest word to show (2–5 letters) and switch between "All words" and "Exact anagrams only". What this tool does not have is starts-with, ends-with or contains filters to fit a word onto a board position — a gap the bigger solvers cover. A 6–7 letter rack can return dozens of words, so use the length filter first and scan the longest group.

FAQ

How do I unscramble letters into words?

Type your letters into the box in any order and press Unscramble. Every real word they can form appears automatically, grouped longest first. Use ? for a blank tile and switch to Exact anagrams only when the puzzle uses every letter.

What is a word unscrambler?

A tool that takes a set of letters and lists every valid English word you can form from them, including shorter words, for Scrabble, Words With Friends, Wordle, jumbles and crosswords. This one checks a built-in list of 1,771 common words, not a full dictionary.

What is the difference between an unscrambler and an anagram solver?

An unscrambler lists every word you can make, including shorter ones; an anagram solver only returns words that use every letter exactly once. LISTEN gives SILENT, ENLIST and TINSEL as anagrams, plus shorter words like LINES and TILES from an unscrambler.

How do I use a blank tile?

Type ? for each blank, up to two. The solver tries every letter in that slot, the same as a Scrabble blank. In real Scrabble a blank scores zero points; this tool shows no scores, so there is nothing to adjust.

How do I find the highest scoring word?

This tool does not show point scores; it sorts results longest first, which is a good starting point but not the same as the highest score. Scrabble and Words With Friends value several letters differently, so check the tile values in your game before you play.

Is using a word unscrambler cheating?

Not always. It depends on the rules you agreed: fine for solo play or learning, and considered cheating in competitive settings. Agree the rules before the game starts, for example that the game is phone-free.

Why does the game reject a word my unscrambler found?

Different games use different dictionaries. Scrabble (TWL/NWL), Words With Friends (a modified ENABLE list) and mobile apps each use their own list, so a word valid in one may not exist in another. Check a word against your game's own dictionary before you play it.

Can I use an unscrambler for Wordle?

Yes, for the word-finding part: set the shortest word to 5 letters and look at the 5-letter group. It does not have a Wordle mode for green and yellow letter positions, so use a dedicated Wordle helper for that.

How many words can I get from one set of letters?

A common, vowel-balanced set of six or seven letters often returns 20 to 80 real words on the big solvers; an awkward set heavy on rare consonants returns far fewer. This tool's 1,771-word list returns fewer than the big 170,000-word solvers.

Does letter order matter when I type them in?

No. Enter your letters in any order; the tool checks every possible arrangement regardless of how you typed them.

Can I narrow results to a specific word length?

Yes. Set the shortest word to show, or switch on Exact anagrams only for results using all of your letters at once. This tool has no starts-with, ends-with or contains filter, so you cannot narrow by a letter pattern.

Are the scores for Scrabble or Words With Friends?

This tool shows no scores at all. Most solvers that do show standard Scrabble tile values; Words With Friends values several letters differently, so use a Words With Friends-specific solver if you play that game.

What is a BINGO in Scrabble?

Using all seven tiles in one play. It earns a 50-point bonus on top of the word score. This tool does not flag BINGOs, but its Exact anagrams only mode lists the words that use every letter you typed, which are your BINGO candidates.

Is the unscrambler free?

Yes. It is free to use in any modern browser, with no sign-up and no limits. Everything runs in your browser, so nothing you type is sent to a server.

Is anything I type stored or sent anywhere?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser and nothing you type is sent to a server.
