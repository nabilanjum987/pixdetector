The problem: letters that look like nonsense

Every word-game player knows the feeling: you are handed TODKLWH, or a daily jumble, or a Wordle rack, and the letters look like nonsense — until someone points out the word hiding in them. The complaints people post are consistent. First, “I came up with a great word and it was ruled invalid” — a word that is real in a dictionary but not in that game's list, or a British spelling in a US game, or slang the list does not allow. Second, the opposite: the solver returns obscure words like TAEL or TELA that nobody would dare play because they might be challenged. Third, blank tiles: people do not know how to tell a solver they have a blank, or the solver has no blank support at all. And fourth, the daily-puzzle rush — Wordle, Connections, Strands, Quordle — where people want the longest word fast, grouped by length, not a wall of 200 unsorted results. What they want is a solver that shows the longest playable words first, says plainly which list it uses, and lets them filter by length or exact anagrams.

How do I unscramble letters into words?

Type your scrambled letters above — in any order — and press Unscramble. Every word in our built-in list that those letters can spell appears at once, grouped from longest to shortest. Use “?” for a blank tile, and switch to “Exact anagrams only” when the puzzle uses every letter. It is free, with no signup, and it runs in your browser.

A word unscrambler is a tool that takes a set of jumbled letters and lists the valid English words you can spell from them, including shorter words made from a subset of the letters. An anagram, by contrast, uses every letter exactly once — LISTEN becomes SILENT, ENLIST and TINSEL.

How to unscramble words in 3 steps

Step 1: type your letters in any order — up to 12, with “?” for each blank tile. Step 2: pick the shortest word to show and choose “All words” or “Exact anagrams only”. Step 3: read the results longest-first and tap any word to copy it.

Unscrambler vs anagram solver — what is the difference?

An unscrambler lists every word you can make from your letters, including shorter ones: LISTEN gives you silent, enlist, tinsel, listen, inlets, lines, tiles, and dozens of 3- and 4-letter words. An anagram solver only keeps words that use every letter exactly once. Use the unscrambler when any valid word counts — Scrabble racks, Words With Friends, word jumbles — and the exact-anagram mode when the puzzle uses every letter, like a newspaper jumble.

Are the words valid for Scrabble and Words With Friends?

Mostly, but not guaranteed. Our list is a curated set of common English words, not an official tournament dictionary (the big solvers use the ENABLE list of about 170,000 words, and even that is not the official Scrabble list). A word here may still be challenged in competitive play, and a valid tournament word may be missing here. For a casual game, this list is fine; for a tournament, check the official word list for your game and region before you play an unusual word.

FAQ

Is this word unscrambler free?

Yes. It is free to use in any modern browser, with no sign-up and no limits. Everything runs in your browser, so nothing you type is sent to a server.

How many letters can I enter?

Up to 12 letters at once, which covers every standard Scrabble or Words With Friends rack. You can include up to two “?” blank tiles, each standing for any one letter.

How is this different from an anagram solver?

An unscrambler lists every word you can make, including shorter ones. An anagram solver only keeps words that use every letter exactly once. Switch this tool to “Exact anagrams only” when you want just the full-length matches.

Why is a word missing from my results?

This tool uses a compact built-in list of common English words, not the 170,000-word ENABLE list the big solvers use. Very new words, informal slang, British-only spellings and proper nouns are generally not included. If a word is missing, it may still be valid in your game — check a full dictionary.

Are the words valid for Scrabble?

Most are, but this is not an official tournament dictionary, so a word shown here may still be challenged in competitive play. Check your game's official list before playing an unusual word.

Can I use it for Wordle?

Yes, for the word-finding part: set the shortest word to 5 letters and look at the 5-letter group. It does not have a Wordle mode for green and yellow letter positions — use a dedicated Wordle helper for that.

Related questions

What does an unscrambler do with a blank tile? A “?” stands for any one letter — “teals?” finds words using those five letters plus one more.

Does letter order matter when I type them? No. Enter your letters in any order; the tool checks every arrangement regardless of how you typed them.

What is the longest word I can make from LISTEN? SILENT, ENLIST, TINSEL and LISTEN itself — all 6-letter anagrams of the same letters.

Can I unscramble letters for a crossword? Partly — this tool finds words from letters, but it has no pattern mode (like ?EA?) for crossword clues. A crossword solver with pattern matching is better for that.
