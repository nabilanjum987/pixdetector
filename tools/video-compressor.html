About This Tool

Videos are big because every second is dozens of still pictures, and a phone recording at 4K can easily pass 100 MB a minute. That is fine on your phone, but it is a problem the moment you need to send the clip in a message, attach it to an email, or upload it to a form with a size limit. This compressor shrinks the file by re-encoding it at a lower bitrate — and, if you choose, at a lower resolution — so the same video takes up far less space while still looking good on a screen.

Everything happens inside your browser. There is no upload, no queue and no server doing the work, which is why there is no file-size limit beyond what your own device can handle — and also why your video stays completely private. The trade-off is that browsers can only re-encode into formats they natively support, so the result is a WebM file, and the picture is re-encoded without its audio track. WebM plays directly in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari, and if you need an MP4 or the original sound, a free desktop tool such as HandBrake or FFmpeg will convert it in seconds.

Pick the preset that matches your goal. Small (~1 Mbps) is for the tiniest file — good enough for a quick preview or a chat message where size matters more than detail. Balanced (~2.5 Mbps) is the sweet spot for most phone footage, screen recordings and clips shared online. High (~5 Mbps) keeps the most detail for videos you plan to keep or show on a big screen. Dropping the resolution helps too: a 4K clip scaled to 720p needs far fewer bits to look sharp, so combining a lower resolution with the Balanced preset is often the biggest single saving.

FAQ

Is this video compressor really free?

Yes. Compress as many videos as you like with no watermark, no sign-up and no file-size limit beyond what your browser can handle. Everything runs locally in your browser.

Why is the compressed video a WebM file?

Browsers can only re-encode video into formats they natively support, and WebM (VP8/VP9) is the one every modern browser can write. WebM plays in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari, and most editors and converters accept it. If you need an MP4, convert the WebM afterwards with a free tool such as HandBrake or FFmpeg.

Does the compressed video keep its audio?

No. This compressor re-encodes the picture only, so the downloaded WebM is video-only and has no audio track. If your video needs its sound, use a desktop tool such as HandBrake instead — it keeps the audio and usually compresses further.

How long does compression take?

Roughly as long as the video itself. The video is played through in real time while the browser re-encodes it, so a 2-minute clip takes about 2 minutes. Keep the tab open and in the foreground while it runs.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. Your video never leaves your device — it is read, re-encoded and downloaded entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and nothing is stored after you close the page.

Will compressing reduce the video quality?

Yes, a little — compression works by throwing away picture detail, and a lower bitrate means a smaller file with softer detail. The Balanced preset (~2.5 Mbps) looks good for most phone and screen recordings; use the High preset (~5 Mbps) when quality matters more than size, and the Small preset (~1 Mbps) when you just need the tiniest file.
