The problem: every "free" converter has a catch

The complaint people repeat most about free video converters is the catch hidden behind the word "free". Reviewers report FreeConvert making them wait twenty minutes and then prompting them to pay. Convertio caps free files at 100 MB and Zamzar at 50 MB, so a real phone clip is over the limit before it starts. Others make you upload a private video to a server, wait in a queue behind paying users, and trust them to delete it afterwards. And the classic one: an iPhone MOV that will not play on a Windows PC, because Windows has no HEVC codec by default. People want a converter that just works — no size cap, no watermark, no upload, no account — and tells them the truth about what it can and cannot output.

A video converter is a tool that re-encodes a video — it reads the pictures and sound of one file and writes them again in a different container, size or quality. The container is the box (MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV); the codec is how the pictures inside are compressed (H.264, VP8, VP9). Changing the container alone is fast; re-encoding takes longer but is what actually shrinks a file or changes its resolution. This tool re-encodes into WebM, the one format browsers can write.

Why WebM, and where does it play?

WebM is the video format browsers can actually write. It plays natively in Chrome, Edge and Firefox, in VLC on any computer, and on Android. It does not play in older Apple software or some TV apps — that is the honest trade-off of a free in-browser converter. If your destination needs MP4, the free desktop route is HandBrake (open the file, pick the "Fast 720p30" preset, start) or VLC (Media → Convert/Save → MP4). Both are free, and both keep your file on your own computer. If you landed here looking for MP4 specifically, the video to MP4 page explains the honest route.

Container vs codec — the confusion behind half the searches

MP4, WebM and MOV are containers — the box the video sits in. H.264, HEVC, VP8 and VP9 are codecs — how the pictures inside are compressed. What decides whether a file plays is the codec inside, not the name on the end. That is why renaming a file does not fix playback, and why the same .mov file sometimes plays and sometimes does not: iPhones do not always record with the same codec settings.

FAQ

What is the best free video converter?

For most people, HandBrake (free, open source, no limits, desktop) — for a quick one-off with no install, a no-upload browser converter; for small files online, CloudConvert's free tier (25 conversions/day, 1 GB).

Is there a truly free video converter with no watermark?

Yes — HandBrake, VLC and FFmpeg are free with no watermark; online, VideoSnap, VidShift, XConvert and Restream all claim no watermark on their free tiers in this run's SERP. Check the size cap before uploading.

Can I convert a video without uploading it?

Yes — browser tools (VideoSnap, VidShift, Vexo) process locally, and this page does too: your video is re-encoded in your browser and nothing is uploaded. Desktop tools (HandBrake, VLC) never upload at all. Server tools (XConvert, Restream, CloudConvert) do upload.

Can I convert a video to MP4 for free?

Yes — almost every converter offers MP4/H.264 output free. On this PixDetector page, no: this tool outputs WebM only (see the honest-limit box) — use a desktop tool or a server converter for a real MP4.

Why is my converted video lower quality?

Re-encoding always loses something; aggressive bitrate/CRF settings lose more. Use a high-quality preset, keep the original resolution, or remux without re-encoding when the codec already fits.

How do I convert MOV to MP4?

Upload the MOV to a converter and pick MP4/H.264, or use HandBrake/VLC free on desktop. iPhone HEVC MOV files need a full transcode, not just a rename.

Can I convert MKV to MP4 without losing quality?

Yes, if the video inside is already H.264 — remuxing just changes the container and loses nothing. If it is another codec, it must be re-encoded.

What is the difference between MP4, WebM and MOV?

They are containers, not codecs: MP4 plays almost everywhere, WebM is smallest for modern browsers, MOV is Apple's container. What matters for playback is the codec inside (H.264, HEVC, VP9).

Is it safe to use a free online video converter?

Reputable ones (CloudConvert, FreeConvert, Convertio) use HTTPS and delete uploads within 24 hours. For sensitive footage, use a local tool instead — nothing leaves your device.

Why won't my video play after converting?

Usually the player lacks the codec, or the file was renamed rather than converted. Try VLC, or re-convert to MP4/H.264 + AAC.

Can I convert a video on my phone?

Yes — browser converters work in a mobile browser; large files are slow on phones because the phone does the encoding.

How long does video conversion take?

A short clip converts in seconds; a long or 4K file takes minutes, and on server tools the upload time usually dominates. Browser tools depend on your own CPU. On this page conversion runs in real time — a 2-minute video takes about 2 minutes.

Can I convert a video to audio (MP3)?

Yes — most converters offer audio-only output (MP3, AAC, WAV, FLAC, OGG); VideoSnap and VidShift both list it. This page always outputs a video file — for audio only, use the Video to MP3 Converter linked below.

What is the biggest file a free converter takes?

It varies by tool: Convertio 100 MB, Zamzar 50 MB, CloudConvert/FreeConvert 1 GB free. Browser tools are limited by your device's memory instead.

Do I need to install software to convert a video?

No — browser converters need no install; desktop converters (HandBrake, VLC) need one install and then work offline with no caps.

Related questions

Can I convert a video without installing software? Yes — this page does it in the browser. The trade-off is WebM output and real-time speed; desktop software is faster and can write MP4.

Why won't my iPhone video play on Windows? iPhones record MOV with the HEVC codec, which Windows often cannot decode without a paid codec pack. Converting to a widely supported format fixes it.

Does converting a video reduce its quality? Re-encoding always loses a little quality. High quality at the original size loses the least; stepping down to 360p and Low quality loses the most.

Is there a file size limit? No server limit, because nothing is uploaded — the practical limit is your device's memory. Very large files convert slowly, so trim them first if you can.
