The problem: every "free" converter has a catch

The complaint people repeat most about free video converters is the catch hidden behind the word "free". Zamzar caps free files at 50 MB. AConvert stops at 200 MB. Clideo converts for free but stamps a watermark unless you pay $6 a month. Others make you upload a private video to a server, wait in a queue, and then delete it "within 24 hours" — if you trust them. And the classic one: an iPhone MOV that will not play on a Windows PC, because Windows wants a codec pack that costs $0.99. People want a converter that just works — no size cap, no watermark, no upload, no account — and tells them the truth about what it can and cannot output.

If you need a real MP4 file, use a free desktop tool like HandBrake or VLC: browsers physically cannot encode MP4, and any page that claims otherwise is misleading you.

A video converter is a tool that re-encodes a video — it reads the pictures and sound of one file and writes them again in a different container, size or quality. The container is the box (MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV); the codec is how the pictures inside are compressed (H.264, VP8, VP9). Changing the container alone is fast; re-encoding takes longer but is what actually shrinks a file or changes its resolution. This tool re-encodes into WebM, the one format browsers can write.

Why WebM, and where does it play?

WebM is the video format browsers can actually write. It plays natively in Chrome, Edge and Firefox, in VLC on any computer, and on Android. It does not play in older Apple software or some TV apps — that is the honest trade-off of a free in-browser converter. If your destination needs MP4, the free desktop route is HandBrake (open the file, pick the "Fast 720p30" preset, start) or VLC (Media → Convert/Save → MP4). Both are free, and both keep your file on your own computer.

FAQ

Is this video converter really free?

Yes. Convert as many videos as you like, free, with no signup, no watermark and no upload — everything runs in your browser.

What format does the converted video come out as?

WebM (.webm). Browsers can record video but cannot encode MP4 (H.264), so no honest in-browser converter can hand you a real MP4 file. WebM plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and VLC. If you need a real MP4, use a free desktop tool like HandBrake or VLC.

Why does conversion take as long as the video itself?

Because the conversion works by playing your video through once and recording it at the new size and quality. A 2-minute video takes about 2 minutes to convert. Keep the tab open while it runs.

Is my video uploaded to a server?

No. Your video is read and re-encoded in your browser with the canvas and MediaRecorder APIs. Nothing is uploaded, stored, or shared.

Does the converted video keep its sound?

Yes, when your browser allows it — the tool routes the video's audio into the recording. If a browser blocks that, the conversion still finishes but the output is video-only, and the page tells you so.

Which video files can I upload?

Any video your browser can play: MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV and AVI in most cases. If a file will not load, it is usually an unsupported codec (for example some HEVC clips) — convert that file with HandBrake first, then use this tool.

Related questions

Can I convert a video without installing software? Yes — this page does it in the browser. The trade-off is WebM output and real-time speed; desktop software is faster and can write MP4.

Why won't my iPhone video play on Windows? iPhones record MOV with the HEVC codec, which Windows often cannot decode without a paid codec pack. Converting to a widely supported format fixes it.

Does converting a video reduce its quality? Re-encoding always loses a little quality. High quality at the original size loses the least; stepping down to 360p and Low quality loses the most.

Is there a file size limit? No server limit, because nothing is uploaded — the practical limit is your device's memory. Very large files convert slowly, so trim them first if you can.
