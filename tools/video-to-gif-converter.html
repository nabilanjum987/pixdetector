The problem: the GIF comes out huge, blurry, or both

The complaint people repeat most about video-to-GIF converters is the file size. You turn a 5-second clip into a GIF and get a 25 MB file that will not upload anywhere — to Discord, to an email, to a forum. The second complaint is quality: gradients go banded, dark scenes go noisy, faces go muddy. Both are the same cause: GIF is a 1987 format that stores every frame as a separate picture with at most 256 colours, while the video it came from compresses motion between frames and holds millions of colours. A 10-second 1080p clip can become a 300 MB GIF if you do not trim it down first. People want the reaction, the bug demo, the 3-second moment — small, loopable, and actually uploadable.

Longer clips, full width and high frame rates all make the file explode, so keep them short.

A video to GIF converter takes the frames of a video clip and writes them as an animated GIF — a single image file that plays on a loop wherever images work: chat, email, docs, forums, GitHub. GIFs autoplay without a player, which is why they survive as the internet's reaction format, but they carry no sound and only 256 colours per frame, so they are always a trade of quality for universality.

How to convert a video to a GIF in 4 steps

Step 1: upload your video — it is read in your browser and never uploaded. Step 2: trim the moment with the start and end boxes (15 seconds maximum; 2–5 is best). Step 3: pick a width and frame rate — 480 px and 10 fps is the usual balance. Step 4: press Make GIF, wait for the frames to encode, and download. Check the file size before you share it; if it is too big, shorten the clip or drop to 320 px and 8 fps.

Slack, Discord, X and most websites also accept short looping MP4 or WebP animations, which are often 5–10 times smaller at better quality. Use GIF when it has to work absolutely everywhere — email signatures, some documentation tools, old forums — and use a short video everywhere else.

FAQ

Is this video to GIF converter really free?

Yes. Make as many GIFs as you like, free, with no signup, no watermark and no upload — everything runs in your browser.

Why is my GIF so much bigger than the video?

GIF is a 1987 format with no modern compression: every frame is stored as a separate picture with at most 256 colours. A 5-second clip can easily become a 25 MB GIF. Keep clips to 2-5 seconds, 480 px wide and 10-12 fps — that usually lands between 1 and 3 MB.

Why does the GIF look worse than the video?

GIF can only hold 256 colours per frame, so gradients and dark scenes band or get noisy, and fine detail is lost. That is a limit of the format, not of this tool. If you need better quality and a smaller file, use a short looping MP4 or WebP animation instead — most chat apps accept those now.

Does a GIF have sound?

No. GIF is an image format and cannot carry audio. The sound in your video is dropped when it becomes a GIF.

How long can my GIF clip be?

This tool allows up to 15 seconds, but the sweet spot is 2-5 seconds. Longer clips get very large and convert slowly, because every frame has to be drawn and encoded in your browser.

Is my video uploaded to a server?

No. Your video is read and turned into a GIF in your browser with canvas and the gif.js library. Nothing is uploaded, stored, or shared.

What frame rate should a GIF be? 10–12 fps looks smooth for most clips. Your video is probably 30 or 60 fps, and keeping all those frames would double or triple the file size for no visible gain.

Why is my GIF blurry? Usually it is the 256-colour limit, or the clip was upscaled. Start from a sharp source, keep the width at or below the source width, and use the Best colours setting.

Can I convert a long video to a GIF? You can trim any 15-second window out of it here, but converting a whole long video is a bad idea — the file would be enormous. Cut the one moment that matters.
