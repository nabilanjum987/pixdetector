The problem: the GIF comes out huge, blurry, or both

The complaint people repeat most about video-to-GIF converters is the file size. You turn a 5-second clip into a GIF and get a file several MB in size that will not upload anywhere — to Discord, to an email, to a forum. The second complaint is quality: gradients go banded, dark scenes go noisy, faces go muddy. Both are the same cause: GIF is a 1987 format that stores every frame as a separate picture with at most 256 colours, while the video it came from compresses motion between frames and holds millions of colours. A 10-second clip at full size can become many times larger than the video it came from if you do not trim it down first. People want the reaction, the bug demo, the 3-second moment — small, loopable, and actually uploadable.

A video to GIF converter takes the frames of a video clip and writes them as an animated GIF — a single image file that plays on a loop wherever images work: chat, email, docs, forums, GitHub. GIFs autoplay without a player, which is why they survive as the internet's reaction format, but they carry no sound and only 256 colours per frame, so they are always a trade of quality for universality.

And one honest question to ask first: do you even need a GIF? Slack, Discord, X and most websites also accept short looping MP4 or WebP animations, which are usually several times smaller at better quality. Use GIF when it has to work absolutely everywhere — email signatures, some documentation tools, old forums — and use a short video everywhere else.

How the colours are chosen

Each frame's 256 colours are sampled from that frame itself (per-frame NeuQuant quantisation in the gif.js encoder), not from one fixed generic palette. That is why flat-colour clips and UI demos usually look clean here. This is not a true two-pass global palette — a palette built from the whole clip in a first pass, as Clipy advertises for its server tool — so gradients and skin tones can still band. Pick "Best colours" if the clip matters, "Smaller file" if the size matters more.

GIF too big? The fix, in order

Check the size on the finished GIF above before sharing. If it is over the cap: 1. Shorten the clip — every second cut is frames you do not have to store. 2. Drop to 320 px wide. 3. Drop to 8–10 fps. 4. Pick the "Smaller file" quality. If it is still too big, the clip is not a GIF job — see the box below.

GIF or MP4?

If the clip needs sound, smooth motion or a small file, use a short looping MP4 or WebM instead — the free video converter makes one from the same clip, usually several times smaller at better quality. Use GIF only where an image is required and a video will not do: chat, email, docs and forums that autoplay images.

What this tool does not do

No text or caption overlay on the GIF — add captions in your video editor first, or use a GIF editor that offers them. No true two-pass global palette (see above). No batch mode — one clip at a time. And no sound, ever: GIF is a silent image format by design.

FAQ

How do I convert a video to GIF for free?

Upload a short clip to a free converter (XConvert, Zight, GIFClear, Clipy, Ezgif), trim to the moment, pick FPS and size, download. On this page it runs in your browser with no upload.

Why is my GIF so big?

GIF stores every frame as an image with only 256 colours and weak compression — a 10-second 30 fps GIF is 300 full images. Shorten the clip, drop FPS to 10–15, resize to 480px.

Why does my GIF look bad / grainy?

The 256-colour ceiling: the converter maps your video's millions of colours onto 256 slots, and a generic palette picks the wrong ones. A palette built from your clip looks much better — this page samples each frame's palette from that frame itself; see “How the colours are chosen” above.

How long should a GIF be?

2–6 seconds is the sweet spot (XConvert); Zight says clips under 10 seconds give the best balance; GIFClear caps at 15 seconds.

What FPS should I use for a GIF?

10 FPS for chat/email, 15 FPS for smoother motion, up to 50 FPS for high-action clips. Lower FPS is the fastest way to shrink the file.

Can a GIF have sound?

No — GIFs are silent by design. If the audio matters, send the video or use a muted looping MP4/WebM instead.

How do I make a GIF under 1 MB?

Resize to 480px wide, drop the palette to 64 colours, reduce FPS to 12–15; still over? Shorten the clip or convert to WebP/MP4.

What size GIF can I send on Discord?

Discord's cap is 20 MB in XConvert's preset (one guide lists 10 MB free / 50 MB Nitro Basic); keep reaction GIFs well under that — 480px, 10–15 FPS, a few seconds.

Can I convert a video to GIF on my phone?

Yes in a mobile browser, but large sources are slow on phones; short clips work best.

Is there a video to GIF converter with no watermark?

Yes — XConvert, Zight, Clipy, GIFClear and Ezgif all claim no watermark in this run; Flixier's free GIF maker also claims no watermark on export.

Can I make a GIF from a YouTube / TikTok / Instagram video?

Download the clip first (where the platform allows it), then convert it — the converter itself takes a file, not a URL, on most free tools.

Why is my GIF not animating?

Usually it was saved or shared as a still image (some apps show only the first frame), or the file was re-encoded as a static image on upload. Download the .gif file itself and open it in a browser to check.

Can I add text to a GIF?

Yes — Flixier and Imgflip offer caption/text overlays; Ezgif has a separate GIF editor for text and effects. This page does not add text — add captions in your video editor first.

What is the best free video to GIF converter?

For quality, Gifski (Mac, free) is the testers' pick; for online use, Ezgif is the long-standing default (free, no watermark, ads intrusive); for privacy, Zight or GIFClear process in your browser.

Should I use GIF or MP4 for a website demo?

MP4/WebM — a GIF of the same clip is usually several times larger and slower to load; GIF wins only where an image is required (chat, email, README, docs) because it autoplays with no player.
