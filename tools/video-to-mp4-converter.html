The problem: "MOV won't play on Windows"

The most common version of this problem is an iPhone video that will not play on a Windows PC — black screen, sound only, or an error asking you to buy a codec. Since iOS 11, iPhones record MOV files with the HEVC (H.265) codec, which Windows often cannot decode without the $0.99 HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store. The second trap is the rename trick: changing .mov to .mp4 does nothing, because the extension is only a label — the video inside is still MOV. The third is the "free" converter that caps files at 50 MB, watermarks the result, or uploads your private video to a server. People want one thing: a file that plays everywhere, without paying, signing up, or uploading.

How do I convert a video to MP4 for free?

Honestly, you cannot get a real MP4 file from any browser tool — browsers can record video but cannot encode MP4 (H.264), so every "free online MP4 converter" either uploads your file to a server to encode it there, or quietly hands you a different format. This page does the honest in-browser version: upload your video, pick a size and quality, press Convert, and download a WebM (.webm) file that plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and VLC — free, with no upload and no watermark. For a genuine .mp4 file, use free HandBrake: open your video, pick the "Fast 720p30" preset, press Start, and you get a real H.264 MP4 that plays everywhere.

A video to MP4 converter changes a video's container — the box the pictures and sound sit in — from MOV, MKV, WebM or AVI into MP4, the format nearly every player, editor, TV and website accepts. Sometimes only the box changes (a fast "remux"); usually the video is re-encoded into H.264, the codec inside most MP4 files. MP4 is popular because it is the one format almost everything agrees on, not because it is technically the best.

How to get a real MP4 free (HandBrake, 3 steps)

Step 1: download HandBrake (free, open source) and open your MOV or MKV file in it. Step 2: pick the "Fast 720p30" preset — it is H.264 MP4, the most compatible choice. Step 3: press Start. The file stays on your computer the whole time, and the result plays on Windows, Android, TVs and in every browser. VLC can do the same: Media → Convert/Save → add the file → choose "Video – H.264 + MP3 (MP4)" → Start. Or use the tool above for a quick WebM version when WebM is enough.

Stop the problem at the source (iPhone setting)

If you keep hitting this, change one iPhone setting: Settings → Camera → Formats → "Most Compatible". Your iPhone will then record H.264 MP4-style video that opens on Windows without any conversion. The trade-off is bigger files — HEVC exists to save space — so keep the "High Efficiency" setting if storage matters, and convert only the copies you share.

Related questions

Can I change .mov to .mp4 just by renaming it? No. Renaming changes the label, not the video — players will still see a MOV file, and many will refuse it. It has to be re-encoded.

Will converting to MP4 lose quality? Re-encoding always loses a little. At High quality and the original size the loss is hard to see; converting the same file again and again adds up, so keep the original.

What is the difference between MP4 and MOV? Both are containers, and the video inside can be identical. MP4 is the universal one; MOV is Apple's version, usually holding HEVC video from iPhones.

Why does the tool above download a .webm file? Because WebM is the one format browsers can write. The tool is honest about it in the note at the top of the page.
