The problem: "MOV won't play on Windows"

The most common version of this problem is an iPhone video that will not play on a Windows PC — black screen, sound only, or an error asking you to buy a codec. Since iOS 11, iPhones record MOV files with the HEVC (H.265) codec, which Windows often cannot decode without the $0.99 HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store. The second trap is the rename trick: changing .mov to .mp4 does nothing, because the extension is only a label — the video inside is still MOV. The third is the "free" converter that caps files at 50 MB, watermarks the result, or uploads your private video to a server. People want one thing: a file that plays everywhere, without paying, signing up, or uploading.

How do I convert a video to MP4 for free?

Honestly, you cannot get a real MP4 file from any browser tool — browsers can record video but cannot encode MP4 (H.264), so every "free online MP4 converter" either uploads your file to a server to encode it there, or quietly hands you a different format. This page does the honest in-browser version: upload your video, pick a size and quality, press Convert, and download a WebM (.webm) file that plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and VLC — free, with no upload and no watermark. For a genuine .mp4 file, use free HandBrake: open your video, pick the "Fast 720p30" preset, press Start, and you get a real H.264 MP4 that plays everywhere.

A video to MP4 converter changes a video's container — the box the pictures and sound sit in — from MOV, MKV, WebM or AVI into MP4, the format nearly every player, editor, TV and website accepts. Sometimes only the box changes (a fast "remux"); usually the video is re-encoded into H.264, the codec inside most MP4 files. MP4 is popular because it is the one format almost everything agrees on, not because it is technically the best.

Renaming .mov to .mp4 does not work

Renaming changes the label on the file, not the video inside — the codec stays exactly as it was, so a HEVC file still fails in players without HEVC support. It has to be properly converted, not renamed.

Remux vs transcode — the fast path most tools never mention

Converting a video can mean two very different jobs. A remux only repackages the video — the pictures and sound are copied untouched into a new container. If the video inside is already H.264, an MOV only needs repackaging into MP4: it finishes in seconds with zero quality loss. A transcode decodes the video and encodes it again, which takes minutes for a long clip and always loses a little quality.

The fast path: if your file is already H.264, use a remux-capable tool instead of a full converter — XMedia Recode's "Lossless Direct Stream Copy" and Clipy's "Fastest" mode are the named examples. Clipy quotes about 1 second for a typical iPhone clip remux and 10–15 seconds for a full transcode (vendor's own figures, not independently verified). The in-browser tool on this page cannot remux — it always re-encodes, which is why it runs in real time.

Stop the problem at the source (iPhone setting)

If you keep hitting this, change one iPhone setting: Settings → Camera → Formats → "Most Compatible". Your iPhone will then record H.264 MP4-style video that opens on Windows without any conversion. The trade-off is bigger files — HEVC exists to save space — so keep the "High Efficiency" setting if storage matters, and convert only the copies you share.

FAQ

How do I convert a video to MP4 for free?

Use HandBrake or VLC free on desktop (no caps, no watermark), or a free online MP4 converter (XConvert, Restream, CapCut) for small files. On this PixDetector page the in-browser tool outputs WebM, not MP4 — see the box at the top for the free MP4 route.

How do I convert MOV to MP4?

Upload the MOV to an MP4 converter and pick MP4/H.264, or use HandBrake/VLC; in Windows Photos, open the MOV → Trim → Save As also produces an MP4.

Why won't my iPhone video play on Windows?

The file is usually HEVC (H.265) inside a MOV container, and Windows has no HEVC codec by default. Install the HEVC Video Extension, play it in VLC, or convert it to MP4/H.264.

Can I just rename .mov to .mp4?

Sometimes it plays, but it does not convert anything — the codec inside is unchanged, so HEVC files still fail in players without HEVC support. Convert properly instead.

Does converting MOV to MP4 reduce quality?

It can — re-encoding loses a little, and aggressive settings lose more; converting can also remove HDR metadata. Keep the original resolution and use a high-quality preset, or remux without re-encoding when the codec already fits.

What is the best free MOV to MP4 converter?

For one-offs, XConvert's MOV page or Clipy (both free, no watermark, no signup in this run's SERP); for unlimited use, HandBrake or VLC on desktop.

Can I convert WebM to MP4?

Yes — WebM screen recordings are a named use case. The codec usually needs a transcode, not just a rename.

Can I convert MKV to MP4 without losing quality?

Yes if the video inside is H.264 — remuxing changes only the container. If it is another codec, it must be re-encoded.

How do I make a video small enough for Discord?

Convert to MP4 and cap the size — Discord's free limit is 20 MB; XConvert offers a Discord 20 MB target-size preset, and resizing to 720p plus a lower CRF gets most clips under it. On this page the Discord preset below picks settings to fit 20 MB, but the output is WebM, not MP4 — use the box above for a real MP4.

Is there a video to MP4 converter with no watermark?

Yes — XConvert, Restream, CapCut, Jupitrr and Zight all claim no watermark in this run's SERP; HandBrake and VLC never watermark.

Can I convert video to MP4 on my phone?

Yes in a mobile browser, but large files are slow on phones; for iPhone clips, converting on a computer is faster.

How long does MOV to MP4 take?

A remux (codec already fits) takes seconds — Clipy quotes about 1 second for a typical iPhone clip remux, 10–15 seconds for a full transcode (vendor's own figures, not independently verified). A long 4K HEVC transcode takes minutes.

Will the converted MP4 keep its audio?

It should — MP4/H.264 + AAC is the standard pairing; the source audio carries over whenever the browser can decode it. If audio is missing, the source audio codec was unsupported — re-convert with AAC selected.

Can I convert MP4 to MP4 to make it smaller?

Yes — re-encode the MP4 at a lower resolution/bitrate or higher CRF; that is compression, not a format change.

Is MP4 the best format for YouTube / Instagram / TikTok?

Yes for uploads — MP4/H.264 is the accepted format on all three; WebM is for web playback, MOV is for Apple workflows.

Related questions

Can I change .mov to .mp4 just by renaming it? No. Renaming changes the label, not the video — players will still see a MOV file, and many will refuse it. It has to be re-encoded.

Will converting to MP4 lose quality? Re-encoding always loses a little. At High quality and the original size the loss is hard to see; converting the same file again and again adds up, so keep the original.

What is the difference between MP4 and MOV? Both are containers, and the video inside can be identical. MP4 is the universal one; MOV is Apple's version, usually holding HEVC video from iPhones.

Why does the tool above download a .webm file? Because WebM is the one format browsers can write. The tool is honest about it in the note at the top of the page.
