The problem: chasing WPM and getting slower

The most common typing complaint is a plateau: the score has been stuck at the same number for weeks, and pushing harder only makes it worse. The cause is usually accuracy. At 92% accuracy you make about 8 errors per 100 keystrokes, and at 70 WPM that is 5 to 6 errors a minute — each one costs time to notice and fix. The second complaint is scores that cannot be compared: one site reports gross WPM (everything typed), another reports net WPM (mistakes subtracted), and a 30-second burst reads 10 WPM higher than a 60-second sustained test. People want one honest number — net WPM with accuracy beside it — and a clear way to improve it.

Accuracy is correct keystrokes divided by total keystrokes, as a percentage.

The 5-minute job-style test

Most typing tests are 15 or 60 seconds, and short tests flatter you: a 15-second burst reads far higher than a sustained test. Employers know this, which is why job-style tests run 5 minutes — long enough for fatigue and accuracy drift to show. Most people score 5–10 WPM lower on five minutes than on one minute. Use the 5-minute mode above when you want the number an employer would trust, and use 60 seconds for quick daily tracking. The result card states the length, so nobody mistakes a sprint for an endurance score.

Why we show the median of 3, not your best

One test is a sample: the same typist can swing 5–10 WPM between runs on luck alone — an easy passage, a good minute, a warm-up effect. A single best score is the most flattering number and the least honest one. Take three back-to-back tests with the same length and the same mode, and the median is your real speed: it ignores the lucky run and the bad run alike. This page stores your last 3 tests under the same length and mode (in your browser only) and shows the median, best and worst side by side.

Why scores differ across sites

The formula is the same everywhere — correct characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes — but the text and rules are not. A common-word pool reads higher than a passage with punctuation, capitals and numbers; a site that lets you ignore errors reads higher than one that makes you correct them; a 15-second burst reads higher than a 60-second test. That is why one typist can be 40 WPM on one site and 60 on another, and neither site is lying. Compare yourself only on the same site, same length, same settings — and use the quote, code and punctuation modes here when you want a score that transfers to real emails, data entry and programming.

How to improve your WPM (the part that actually works)

First, learn touch typing: all ten fingers, home row ASDF and JKL;, eyes on the screen, not the keys. It feels slower for a week, then it is the single biggest speed gain available. Second, prioritise accuracy over speed: practise at 80% of your maximum comfortable speed with zero errors, and treat 97% accuracy as the gatekeeper for every WPM increase — speed with errors teaches bad habits, speed with accuracy builds muscle memory. Third, drill your problem keys: after each test, note which words you missed most — usually pinky-finger keys (P, Q, A, Z, semicolon) and combinations like "tion" and "qu". Fourth, practise a little every day: 10 to 15 minutes of focused practice beats one long weekly session, and one 60-second test a day tracks whether it is working.

FAQ

What is WPM?

Words per minute — typing speed. One “word” is counted as 5 characters including spaces, so WPM = (characters typed ÷ 5) ÷ minutes. On this page, net WPM counts only correct characters, and raw WPM counts everything typed.

What is a good typing speed?

Rough ranges from the top pages: beginner below 25, average 30–40, good 45–60, fast 70+. For office work 40–50 is standard; transcription and legal roles typically ask 70–90+. Kwerty reports its own median attempt at 36 WPM — that is the brand’s own data, not an independent benchmark.

Why is my WPM different on different sites?

The formula is the same but the text and rules are not: word-pool difficulty, punctuation, whether errors must be corrected, whether spaces count, and test length all move the score. Compare yourself only on the same site, same length, same settings — that is why this page shows the median of your last 3 tests under identical conditions.

What is the difference between gross WPM and net WPM?

Gross WPM is raw speed with no penalty; net WPM subtracts errors (net = gross − errors per minute). Net is the honest, productive number — the one employers care about. This page shows both side by side, so the accuracy cost is visible, not hidden.

How is WPM calculated?

Correct characters ÷ 5 ÷ elapsed minutes. Accuracy = correct key attempts ÷ total attempts × 100. Backspace does not erase earlier errors on most sites. On this page, spaces count as characters, and a word only counts as correct when the word and its following space are typed correctly.

Is 120 WPM good?

Yes — exceptional. Most people type 30–70 WPM; 120 with high accuracy is elite territory, and competitive typing starts around 120–140.

How can I increase my typing speed?

Ten-finger touch typing on the home row (ASDF JKL;), accuracy first, 15–30 minutes daily; creators in this run recommend a minimum of 30 minutes a day, up to 1–3 hours. Expect roughly 10–15 WPM in 30 days of daily practice — that is Kwerty’s own claim, not independently verified.

How long does it take to reach 100 WPM?

No fixed timeline — it depends on starting speed and daily practice. The “WPM Diary” creator in this run went from 45 WPM and was stuck at 55 on Day 4; treat months, not days, as the honest answer.

How many tests should I take to know my real speed?

Take three back-to-back tests with the same duration and settings and use the median — a single result is a sample, and best-of-five sits above the first attempt purely from variance (Kwerty’s own data — brand claim, not verified). This page does it for you: after each test it shows the median, best and worst of your last 3 tests under the same duration and mode.

Can I get a typing certificate for free?

Ratatype issues a free certificate (shown in the viral reel at 43 WPM); most test sites, including Monkeytype, do not. Check whether the employer accepts it before relying on it. This page does not issue a certificate — it gives you a shareable result card that states test length, mode and net vs gross WPM so the number is verifiable.

Does a typing test work on my phone?

Yes, in a mobile browser, but scores on a touchscreen keyboard are not comparable to a physical keyboard — treat phone results as practice only, and compare phone scores with phone scores.

What is accuracy, and what should it be?

The share of correct keystrokes. Below 90% means you are typing faster than your fingers can execute — slow down; accuracy first raises net WPM faster than raw speed does. 95%+ is the target for lessons on several sites.

What is raw WPM / consistency?

Raw WPM counts all keystrokes including errors; consistency measures how steady your speed is second by second. A big gap between raw and net WPM means errors, not speed, are your problem. This page shows raw WPM, net WPM, a consistency score and a per-second speed graph after every test.

What is a 5-minute typing test for?

Job-style endurance: it exposes fatigue and accuracy drift. Most people score 5–10 WPM lower on five minutes than on one minute, and the five-minute number is the one employers trust (LearnType). Use the 5-minute mode on this page for the employer-trusted number.

Is Monkeytype accurate?

It is consistent with itself, not with other sites — its common-word pool and rules tend to read higher than stricter sites. Use it to track your own progress on identical settings, not to compare against another site’s number. The reported 350 WPM auto-invalidation is debated in comments and was not verified against Monkeytype’s own docs in this run.
