The problem: chasing WPM and getting slower

The most common typing complaint is a plateau: the score has been stuck at the same number for weeks, and pushing harder only makes it worse. The cause is usually accuracy. At 92% accuracy you make about 8 errors per 100 keystrokes, and at 70 WPM that is 5 to 6 errors a minute — each one costs time to notice and fix. The second complaint is scores that cannot be compared: one site reports gross WPM (everything typed), another reports net WPM (mistakes subtracted), and a 30-second burst reads 10 WPM higher than a 60-second sustained test. People want one honest number — net WPM with accuracy beside it — and a clear way to improve it.

Accuracy is correct keystrokes divided by total keystrokes, as a percentage.

How to improve your WPM (the part that actually works)

First, learn touch typing: all ten fingers, home row ASDF and JKL;, eyes on the screen, not the keys. It feels slower for a week, then it is the single biggest speed gain available. Second, prioritise accuracy over speed: practise at 80% of your maximum comfortable speed with zero errors, and treat 97% accuracy as the gatekeeper for every WPM increase — speed with errors teaches bad habits, speed with accuracy builds muscle memory. Third, drill your problem keys: after each test, note which words you missed most — usually pinky-finger keys (P, Q, A, Z, semicolon) and combinations like "tion" and "qu". Fourth, practise a little every day: 10 to 15 minutes of focused practice beats one long weekly session, and one 60-second test a day tracks whether it is working.

FAQ

Is this WPM test really free?

Yes. Take as many 60-second tests as you like, free, with no signup, no ads and no download. Everything runs in your browser.

What is WPM and how is it calculated here?

WPM means words per minute. By convention, one word counts as five characters, including spaces. Net WPM is (correct characters divided by 5) divided by minutes — mistakes do not count. Raw WPM counts all characters typed. Always quote net WPM with accuracy beside it for an honest number.

What is a good typing speed?

Below 25 WPM is beginner, 30 to 40 is average, 45 to 60 is good, 70 and above is fast. Most office and data-entry jobs ask for 40 to 60 WPM, and government exam targets are usually 30 to 35 WPM. These are rough ranges, not rules.

Why is my accuracy more important than my speed?

Because mistakes cost time twice: once to make them, once to fix them. At 92% accuracy you make about 8 errors per 100 keystrokes — at 70 WPM that is 5 to 6 errors a minute. Slowing down 5 to 10 WPM to reach 98% accuracy usually delivers faster net output. Accuracy first, speed follows.

Why is my 60-second score lower than my 30-second score?

Short tests reward bursts of speed; a 60-second test measures sustained typing, and most people score 5 to 10 WPM lower on it. The 60-second number is the one employers trust, because it exposes fatigue and accuracy drift. Use 60 seconds for benchmarks.

How can I improve my typing speed?

Learn touch typing — all ten fingers, home row ASDF and JKL;, eyes on the screen. Practise at 80% of your maximum speed with zero errors, drill the keys you mistype most, and test daily: one 60-second test a day tracks progress, and 10 to 15 minutes of focused practice moves the needle.
