🎵 YouTube to MP3 Converter Free

There is no safe one-click button that turns any YouTube video into an MP3 — and most sites promising one are either illegal, unsafe, or both. This guide shows the legal ways to save YouTube audio for offline listening, from YouTube Premium to creator downloads and your own videos.

⚠️ Copyright first — read this before downloading anything

Only download audio that you own, that is licensed for download, or that the creator has explicitly made available. Downloading someone else's music, podcast or video from YouTube without permission violates YouTube's Terms of Service and can infringe copyright — even if it is only for personal use and you never share it. The methods below stay on the right side of that line.

How to save YouTube audio, method by method

Pick the method that matches what you are trying to do — listening offline, owning a track, or recovering your own audio.

1. YouTube Premium — offline downloads in the app

This is the official way YouTube itself offers. Downloads stay inside the YouTube app rather than becoming MP3 files, but they work reliably and the quality is high.

  1. Subscribe to YouTube Premium in the YouTube app or on the YouTube website.
  2. Find the video or playlist you want available offline.
  3. Tap Download under the video, or the download icon on a playlist.
  4. Choose a quality (low, medium or high) to balance sound against storage space.
  5. Listen in Library → Downloads whenever you have no connection.

Two things to know: downloads expire if your subscription lapses, and you need to reconnect to the internet at least once every 30 days to keep them. You also get ad-free viewing and background play as part of the same subscription.

2. YouTube Music — the simplest option for songs

If what you want is music rather than video, YouTube Music is the cleaner tool for the job. It has the same catalogue as YouTube, organised as songs, albums and playlists instead of videos.

  1. Open the YouTube Music app and sign in with the same Google account.
  2. Search for the song, album or playlist you want offline.
  3. Tap the download icon on the song or playlist page.
  4. Manage downloads in Library → Downloads, and set download quality under Settings if you are short on space.

The free tier lets you stream with ads but does not include downloads — offline listening needs a YouTube Music Premium or YouTube Premium subscription. A handy extra: turn on smart downloads and the app automatically saves a rotating selection of your favourite songs overnight on Wi-Fi.

3. Creator-provided downloads — real MP3 files you actually own

Many artists sell or give away their music outside YouTube, and these downloads are real files — usually MP3 or lossless FLAC — that you can play in any app, copy to any device and keep forever.

  1. Bandcamp: search for the artist, buy the track or album, and download it in MP3, FLAC or another format of your choice. Many releases are name-your-price, including free.
  2. SoundCloud: only some tracks have a Download button under the player — that button means the artist has enabled downloading. If there is no button, the track is stream-only.
  3. Artist websites and newsletters: independent musicians often offer free downloads, demos and stems in exchange for an email sign-up.
  4. Creative Commons libraries: sites like Free Music Archive host tracks released under licences that explicitly allow downloading and reuse — always check the licence attached to each track.

This route is usually the best-sounding one, too: a Bandcamp FLAC or 320 kbps MP3 beats the compressed audio a converter site would rip from a video stream, and the money goes to the artist.

4. Converting audio from your own videos

If you uploaded the video, the audio is yours to use however you like. You do not need a converter site at all.

  1. Download from YouTube Studio: open Studio, go to Content, click the three dots next to your video and choose Download. You get back a copy of the file you uploaded.
  2. Export your whole channel with Google Takeout: at takeout.google.com, select YouTube, and Google will package all your uploads for download.
  3. Extract the audio on your computer: open the downloaded video in a free desktop tool such as VLC (Media → Convert/Save) or Audacity, and export the soundtrack as an MP3.
  4. Skip the round trip: if you still have the project, export the audio directly from your video editor or recording app — that keeps the original quality instead of re-compressing an upload.

The same applies to audio you recorded yourself for a video you have not uploaded yet: the cleanest MP3 comes from your own source files, never from a downloaded copy of the finished video.

Why most free converter sites are risky

Search "YouTube to MP3" and you will find hundreds of sites offering instant conversion. Here is what they usually do not tell you.

  • Fake download buttons: the big flashing "Download" button is often an ad. The real link is small, hidden, or appears only after several clicks through pop-ups.
  • Malware and adware: many of these sites push installers, browser extensions or "update your player" prompts that install unwanted software. Security researchers flag converter sites among the most common sources of adware infections.
  • Your data is the product: free conversion has to be paid for somehow. Expect tracking, aggressive ad networks, and in some cases the links and URLs you paste being logged.
  • Poor audio quality: the MP3 you get is usually a low-bitrate re-encode of YouTube's already-compressed audio stream — noticeably worse than the original, and worse than a legal download from the artist.
  • They vanish and reappear: converter sites are regularly shut down, blocked or sued, then return under a new name. A site that worked last month may be gone — or replaced by a lookalike running scams — today.
  • They break YouTube's rules: downloading YouTube content outside the official features violates the platform's Terms of Service. That can mean strikes or loss of access to your Google account in the worst case, and it takes revenue from the creators you are listening to.

If a converter site is your only option for a track you cannot find anywhere else, at minimum never install anything it asks you to install, never allow browser notifications from it, and never enter an email address or payment details. But for music you actually like, one of the four legal methods above is faster, safer and better sounding.

That is why every site promising a free, instant YouTube-to-MP3 conversion is operating in a grey area at best — and why so many of them are unsafe. FAQ

Is it legal to convert YouTube videos to MP3?

Only in limited cases. Downloading a YouTube video or its audio without permission violates YouTube's Terms of Service and can infringe copyright, even for personal use. The legal routes are YouTube Premium offline downloads, YouTube Music, downloads the creator has explicitly enabled, and audio from videos you uploaded yourself.

Does this page convert YouTube videos to MP3?

No. This is a guide, not a converter. A static website cannot pull audio from YouTube and convert it in your browser, and any site that claims to do it for free is either breaking YouTube's rules or making money some other way — usually ads, installers or your data. This page explains the legal methods that actually work instead.

How can I listen to YouTube music offline legally?

Subscribe to YouTube Premium or YouTube Music Premium, then tap the download button on a song, album or playlist in the app. The downloads live inside the app so you can listen without a connection, and they stay available as long as your subscription is active and you reconnect to the internet at least once every 30 days.

Can I download the audio from my own YouTube videos?

Yes. In YouTube Studio, open Content, click the three dots next to your video and choose Download to save a copy of the upload. For your whole channel at once, use Google Takeout and select YouTube. You can also export the audio straight from the video editor or recording app you made it in, which keeps the original quality.

Why are most free YouTube to MP3 sites risky?

Because the free conversion is rarely the product — you are. These sites are known for fake download buttons, aggressive pop-ups and redirects, bundled installers that carry adware or malware, and harvesting the data you paste in. The audio quality is often poor, the sites get shut down regularly, and using them breaks YouTube's Terms of Service.
